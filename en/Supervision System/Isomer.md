# Isomer Supervision System

Location: Hypnotherapy Room

Urgency: ⚠

A casual chat with Isomer somehow drifts into the topic of working late.

- Share notes

  You two trade late‑night survival tips in earnest. They're practical enough... though you quietly hope you'll never need them.

Lady Pearl's show is a surprise hit at the Bureau, and when the curtain falls, one performer draws all the praise.

- Who was it?

  Someone tells you it was Isomer. She handled multiple roles on her own, capturing each character's inner world with uncanny precision—even the smallest emotional shifts landed perfectly.

  Not the answer you expected.

### 「Coffee Room Chat」

Location: Outdoor Activity Area

Urgency: ⚠⚠

You drag yourself in, desperate for caffeine, only to find Langley and Isomer already there.

Isomer's cup of black coffee looks bitter enough to be ink. Langley is calmly grinding beans, each motion as deliberate as if she were handling classified intel.

Isomer: I have nothing against hand‑ground coffee... but if the goal is waking up, the process could stand to be a little more efficient.

Langley: You're not wrong. If all you want is a jolt, plain black coffee works. But coffee's value isn't limited to staying awake.

- Step in and grab a cup

  Langley: What do you think, rookie? As fellow leaders who rely on coffee to get through the day, we'd love your take.

  Isomer: Indeed. A third‑party perspective might give us the most objective verdict.

  Before you realize it, you're already holding a cup in each hand.

  Langley: A good hand‑ground cup is more than a stimulant. The grind, the pour, the extraction... every step demands patience.

  Langley: Much like intelligence work. Rush it and you lose the flavor. Wait too long and you miss the moment. What matters is finding order within the chaos.

  Isomer: I understand that, though my reason for preferring bitter coffee is simpler.

  Isomer: It doesn't dress up reality or promise pleasure. It just delivers clarity. The world is complicated enough. Coffee can be simple.

  - Hahahaha.

    You try to laugh it off, but the two exchange a brief glance—a quiet understanding you're clearly not part of.

    Langley: Good. Looks like our rookie is ready to give it a try.

    Isomer: Fair enough. If you're not offering opinions, the least you can do is taste it yourself.

    You look down at the two cups in your hands. One is stark and bitter. The other contains something more layered.

    So you pour both into the new one‑liter travel mug you just bought, and thank them together.

### 「Before the Butterfly Flaps」

Location: Energy Furnace

Urgency: ⚠⚠

A tiny insect drifts through the Bureau corridor. Isomer pauses, opens a ventilation panel with practiced ease, and lets it out.

The precision of the gesture makes it look less like releasing a bug and more like quietly averting a crisis.

- Ask about the reason

  Isomer: If I hadn't, it would've landed on a staff member's nose in three minutes and made them sneeze.

  Isomer: That sneeze would've startled the person beside them carrying coffee. The spill would've hit an aging outlet, shorted it, and knocked out power on this entire floor.

  Isomer: And since the Bureau is running a coordination test on the backup security system, the outage would've been flagged as an external breach.

  Isomer: From there, we'd have corridor lockdowns, emergency protocols, and urgent support calls to other departments.

  Isomer: And in the end, because of a management error causing an unnecessary response, your monthly bonus would've been docked entirely.

  - Is that... actually true?

    Isomer: Of course not, Chief. I was joking.

    Isomer: The real reason is that the ventilation's bug filters need replacing.

    You study Isomer's calm, unreadable expression and genuinely can't tell whether she is joking.

    That evening, you approve the ventilation maintenance request anyway.

### 「The Art of Leadership」

Location: Management Center

Urgency: ⚠⚠⚠

Isomer's just come out of a meeting. Her composure holds, but there's a faint tiredness around her eyes.

- Check in on her

  Isomer: Thank you for asking, Chief. I'm fine. The meeting had five agenda items, seventeen conflicting opinions, and at least six people trying to hand off their responsibilities.

  Isomer: I revised budgets, reassessed risks, and drafted contingency plans. Most issues were settled before adjournment.

  - Ask about her meeting strategies

    Isomer: Never let anyone walk in with only a problem. Make them bring a proposal, the reasoning behind it, and someone willing to stand behind it.

    Isomer: Choose the proposal closest to the right answer, then ask the dissenters to identify its risks.

    Isomer: Let the sharp minds correct each other instead of waiting for you to deliver the final word.

    - Ask how she tells who's right

      Isomer: Usually, no one is completely right. Everyone holds part of the truth, and everyone avoids part of the cost.

      Isomer: There's never just one path forward. The difference lies in the cost, the risk, and who pays for failure.

      Isomer: Sometimes the best you can do is choose the least bad option.


      - How to Maintain Authority

        Isomer: Authority isn't something you cling to. The harder you try to hold it, the less stable it becomes.

        Isomer: Let your subordinates do their jobs. Set clear boundaries. And when the results come in, own your share of them.

        Isomer: I despise politics. Ironically, academia is full of it. If not for the research itself, I'd never have touched any of it.

        Isomer: Besides, leadership isn't my responsibility anymore. Let's talk about something lighter, Chief.

      - How to Protect Subordinates' Rights


      <br>

      Isomer: Protect them like a lion protects its pride. Fight for their resources, shield them from unwarranted blame, and when it matters, answer the call as the one in charge.

      Isomer: But let them hunt too. Protection doesn't mean keeping them behind you forever.

      Isomer: Your teachable heart is admirable, Chief. I was the same way once.

      Isomer: And if I'm honest... I admire how you sometimes set all of this aside and move forward on instinct alone.

### 「The Science of Steak Searing」

Location: Greenhouse

Urgency: ⚠⚠⚠

You follow the delightful smell to the kitchen and find Isomer at the stove, pan‑searing a steak.

The steak sizzles in the cast iron pan. Butter foams at its edges, mingling with rosemary and garlic. Isomer watches the heat with steady focus, her movements as composed as ever.

- Mmm! That smells wonderful.

  Isomer: Perfect timing, Chief. I'm testing a revised steak recipe.

  Isomer: If you don't mind, I'd like you to taste it and share your thoughts.

  You glance at the recipe beside her. It's the most detailed one you've ever seen—seasoning ratios measured to the milligram, heat timings to the second, even a color chart for every stage of browning.

  - Ask about the recipe

    Isomer: Strictly speaking, it's not original. It's an improved version of existing ones.

    Isomer: My standard is reproducibility. Instructions like "sear until golden brown" are, to me, irresponsibly vague. So... my recipes tend to run long.

    Isomer: There. The steak is done. Please, try it.

    She slides the plate toward you and hands over a detailed evaluation form.

    Beyond "good" or "bad," it lists over a dozen criteria: texture, flavor complexity, finish duration, likelihood of future consumption, and more.


    - Fill it out diligently

      You work through the steak and the form at the same time. Isomer sits beside you, chin resting in one hand, watching quietly.

      Isomer: You filled it out very thoroughly. As feedback samples go, this is ideal.

      Isomer: And if I shift the subject of observation to you... I'd say I've gathered some interesting data.

    - Dig in


    <br>

    The form is the last thing on your mind. You just keep eating. It's easily the best meal you've had in a while.

    Isomer: They say the sincerest compliment a chef can receive is watching someone truly enjoy the food. Given your pace, I think the feedback speaks for itself.
