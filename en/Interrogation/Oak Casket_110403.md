# Oak Casket Interrogation

## Oak Casket - Interrogation I

> Intelligence before completion:
> After the Rustfire serial event ended, Oak Casket returned with you to the MBCC.
> She seemed to be in low spirits all the time. Like other Sinners, she was somewhat free to do as she pleased in the MBCC, but she rarely engaged in outdoor activities.
> However, there are still many mysteries surrounding the Drifter Camp, and you need her help.

> Intelligence after completion:
> Oak Casket might still harbor doubts about you.
> You decide to win her trust in a way she can understand and accept.

### Interrogation Transcript

The lights in the interrogation room are turned off. Someone is murmuring in the darkness.

Oak Casket: *You shall endure hardships and surpass all stelae in this world. You shall meet the golden falcon, and the winged traveler will guide you to the light. Where there is light, there you shall be.*

You find this poem oddly familiar.

When you walk in, Oak Casket nods to greet you.

She sits in a relaxed posture in the interrogation chair, gently gazing at you as though she is the one in control of the interrogation room.

Oak Casket: You're here.

Oak Casket: What do you seek from me this time?

Oak Casket's tone is calm and unperturbed, yet you can feel a sense of distance.

Chief: Your support, or something along the lines.

You gently flip open the folder, revealing several pictures of the ruined Romanesque Hall after the explosion of the Black Ring. You slide it forward, but Oak Casket only glances at it before returning her gaze to you.

Chief: The Rustfire crisis in West District is over now. The people of West District and the Eastside City Council hope to start the reconstruction of Syndicate as soon as possible.

Chief: We also deeply regret the severe damage the Romanesque Hall suffered during the battle, but we've already planned to repair it, and... construct a monument for those who lost their lives in the disaster.

Chief: Now, we need someone who has the required knowledge to assist us in our work.

Chief: We believe there aren't any other better candidates than you.

You thought Oak Casket would agree to repair the hall and the surrounding stelae despite her look of disdain. But to your surprise, Oak Casket voices her refusal.

Oak Casket: Regretfully, there isn't much I can do.

Chief: But... no one knows the number of stelae in Romanesque and the list of the deceased better than you do.

Chief: If there are any procedural concerns, I will assist you.

You try to persuade her in a soft voice, but Oak Casket remains cold-faced and adamant.

Oak Casket: The living caused the death of the deceased, and they build the stelae and create the archives to comfort themselves.

Oak Casket: They believe that by doing all this, they can atone for their sins and gain the forgiveness of the deceased.

Oak Casket: Then they can bid the past farewell and continue to live in this world burden-free.

Oak Casket: I refuse to participate in that.

Having said that, Oak Casket leans back in the interrogation chair, signaling the end of the conversation.

Chief: You've misunderstood me.

You sigh and patiently explain to Oak Casket.

Chief: We are not doing this for our own sake, nor to comfort ourselves... but to always remember the pain inflicted by this disaster...

Chief: The Romanesque Hall preserves the dignity of the deceased after death and resolves their final regrets. It is one of the most precious places in Syndicate, and also one of the important witnesses of this disaster.

Chief: That's why we want to rebuild it.

Oak Casket's attitude softens a bit. She stops refuting you.

Oak Casket: Yes... Where they are is the best place in the world... They are the most wonderful, peaceful, and reliable friends...

Oak Casket: Alive, they were the heroes of Rustfire, the refugees of the Drifter Camp, and my good neighbors. After their death...

Oak Casket: They returned to Romanesque. I dressed them up the best I could and listened to the words they left in the last moments of life.

Oak Casket: Answering their murmurs, I resolved many of their regrets... but there were always some I couldn't settle...

Chief: Can you tell me more about these regrets?

Oak Casket smiles.

She lifts her finger deftly, as if some invisible souls are lingering in the blank space of the interrogation room, lamenting their regrets to her.

Oak Casket: They take on many forms. A child complained he had never seen his parents. A man couldn't get over his hatred for the gang who murdered his family. An old woman said she hadn't had one good thing in life and that her life was pointless...

Chief: Is this related to that unresolvable regret?

Chief: Or... is this one of the reasons you refuse to help us?

Oak Casket: ...

In the silence, you can hardly hear her breathing.

After a while, she bends down slightly, leaning in closer, her unevenly colored eyes eerie in the dimness.

Oak Casket: Answer me...

Her voice is icy, like a question from the dead.

Oak Casket: Do you believe that... the future is always hopeful?

**【Advance】**

- Yes.

**end**

Chief: I believe in hope, just like I believe you're trying to help those who have lost hope.

You hold her gaze and tell her your choice.

Oak Casket sighs deeply, leaning back in the chair.

Oak Casket: You're stubborn, aren't you?

Oak Casket: Then let me tell you a story.

Oak Casket: Have you heard this story about a falcon and three pockets before?

Chief: No.

Oak Casket: This is a story told to me by a priest a long time ago. I've heard it dates back to before Nightfall.

Oak Casket: When people first came to this world, the golden falcon opened the way for them and gave them three pockets: One filled with wealth, one with power, and one with hope.

Oak Casket: The falcon told them the pocket of wealth could be found where merchants gathered and the pocket of power could be found in war.

Chief: I guess the pocket of hope was the hardest to find.

Oak Casket: You're always smart at times like this.

Oak Casket smiles and continues while gazing at you.

Oak Casket: It's a pity that the priest... died before he finished telling this story.

Chief: Is that priest the one whose regret you couldn't resolve?

Oak Casket doesn't answer your question. Instead, she starts reciting that strange poem softly into the darkness.

Oak Casket: You shall endure hardships and surpass all stelae in this world...

Oak Casket: You shall meet the golden falcon, and the winged traveler will guide you...

She mutters emotionlessly, sounding more like she's mocking the poem than praying.

Oak Casket: Those who have lost hope... can't even resolve their regrets.

Oak Casket: Chief, where do you think we can find the pocket of hope?

**【Choose One】**

- I've found it before.

- I may not find it now.

**end**

Chief: I once found the pocket of hope with my own hands.

Chief: Perhaps the pockets of hope have become scarce in our world now, so much so that the regrets and the deceased you've ever encountered have never seen hope.

Chief: But the world has already started to change.

Chief: One day, the pocket of hope will appear before everyone's eyes.

Chief: Perhaps the pocket of hope doesn't exist in our world now.

Chief: But there are always people resisting this terrible world, striving to bring the pocket of hope back to this world.

Chief: Maybe we're indeed the unlucky ones. Maybe the world after Nightfall is just so terrible that hope no longer exists naturally.

Chief: But as long as we persist, one day, the pocket of hope will appear before everyone's eyes.

Oak Casket: Do you think it is something that everyone can have?

Oak Casket is watching you, her eyes gleaming with interest.

Chief: Yes, as long as they're willing to persist.

Chief: I've been through countless hopeless situations... Yet I'm still standing here, instead of lying beneath a gravestone in Romanesque.

Chief: I believe that as long as you face it, one day you'll find a way to resolve even that unresolvable regret.

Oak Casket remains silent.

But from the other end of the shackles, you feel a tiny ripple of Mania.

It's impossible to fully convince her of a hopeful future within such a short conversation. But you want to at least show her the possibilities.

Chief: I have a suggestion, Oak Casket.

You take the spread-out file, remove the photos on it and put them in your pocket, then draft an application on the paper.

Chief: If I can't persuade you from the living's perspective, then we'll let the dead decide.

For the first time, some semblance of surprise emerges on Oak Casket's expressionless face.

Oak Casket: Let the dead decide? How are you going to conduct this *judgment* when you can't even communicate with them?

Chief: I will sign a document permitting your return to the Drifter Camp for the reconstruction of Romanesque's stele garden. And I'll accompany you as your supervisor.

Chief: We'll resolve that regret together.

Upon hearing your proposal, her mismatched eyes radiate an almost hysterical brilliance.

Oak Casket: ...You're very bold, Chief.

You haven't seen her like this in a long time. The messenger of the dead who mocks the living recklessly.

Chief: If the dead approve of me... I hope you will too, and cooperate with us to rebuild West District.

Oak Casket: Haha... After peeking into death, are you reaching to the message between life and death now too?

She looks at the application you handed over, a neat signature already printed after the supervisor's name. Oak Casket picks up the pen on the desk and signs her own name.

Oak Casket: Very well, I accept your proposal.

Oak Casket: I will do my best to assist you, but you must touch the gravestones of Romanesque and listen to the whispers of the dead, and you can't leave even if it becomes too much for your mind to bear.

Oak Casket: Don't disappoint me.

As you exit the interrogation room, Oak Casket looks much more *pleased* than before.

You complete the application as quickly as possible, but Nightingale is still concerned.

You reassure her that Oak Casket has helped you many times before and that she'll do the same this time. Finally, Nightingale relents.

## Oak Casket - Interrogation II

> Intelligence before completion:
> You brought a permit to leave for Oak Casket.
> As promised, you will accompany her to the Romanesque Hall to resolve the obsession that even Oak Casket couldn't handle.

> Intelligence after completion:
> From the obsession, you can only gather very little information. There seems to be something hidden behind the information...
> You need to investigate further.

### Interrogation Transcript

Romanesque Hall

The stele garden now looks starkly different than it did when you first came here. Most of the stelae have collapsed, and the white marble blocks scatter among the weeds. Some graves have cracked, revealing the coffins.

When you walk into the stele garden, a crow flutters its wings and flies away from a stele. You notice a bunch of black vines climbing on the stele, with a small yellow flower blooming on it.

Chief: What kind of flower is that?

Seeing the flower, Oak Casket's gaze sharpens. She squats down next to the vines and gently caresses the small flower.

Oak Casket: Do you hear anything?

You listen carefully, but the stele garden is like a frozen space, without even a whisper of wind.

Chief: ...No.

Oak Casket: This is an oak flower. Or rather, an oak flower that's been branded.

Chief: But these vines are not even branches of an oak.

Oak Casket: This is the branding effect. These vines grew from an oak coffin, so its flower is a duplicate of an oak flower. The person buried under this stele was a gardener who was fond of plants.

Oak Casket: There is an oak tree behind the stele garden. Half of it was blown away by the explosion of the monastery, but it still survived.

Chief: Because of this gardener?

Oak Casket: Yes, he cared for every plant within his sight and dreamed of living in a forest, even though he never saw a real forest in his life.

Oak Casket: Before his death, he asked me not to use dried wood for his coffin. He wished to become one with these plants.

Chief: Did he die in this war as well?

Oak Casket: No... much earlier than that.

Oak Casket smiles gently.

Oak Casket: He died in a gang fight.

Oak Casket: He was the monastery's gardener. The monastery had some peaceful days until a gang threatened them and made the place their hideout for some big shot.

Oak Casket: Soon, the fact that the big shot was hiding in the monastery was discovered. Other gangs besieged the monastery, and many people died in that siege.

Oak Casket: At that time... when I returned to the monastery... he was on his deathbed.

Oak Casket: He was the first person I laid to rest.

Chief: Did he leave behind any regrets?

Oak Casket: He was worried about that oak tree behind the monastery.

As dusk falls, you look toward the back of the stele garden in the twilight. There is nothing there, only vast and empty darkness in the distance.

Oak Casket: It didn't survive the last war. But I kept part of it.

Oak Casket spreads her hand to reveal an acorn in her palm. She places the acorn next to the small flower, watching it.

You walk along the path of the stele garden, fixing the damaged tombstones along the way. Oak Casket seems to remember every tombstone owner and the locations of all tombstones and coffins.

A nearly worn-out hat sits on a tombstone. Oak Casket gently removes the hat and starts mending it.

Chief: Is this hat yours?

Oak Casket: No, it belonged to a little girl named Nasha. The one buried here was her best friend. That child was sickly her whole life. Nasha probably worried that she would catch a cold from the wind and rain here.

Chief: ...Did the child die of illness?

Oak Casket shakes her head slightly.

Oak Casket: The gang took her away. Two days after they brought her back, her mother found her in a reaction pool at the Drifter Camp. I made her a wig and a mask so that she could look like herself in departure.

Her gentle, sad gaze falls on the tombstone again, where the little girl's name is engraved, but the name has long since faded.

Chief: ...What about Nasha? Hasn't she come to replace this hat? It looks... quite old.

Oak Casket: She can't come anymore.

Oak Casket: That's her tombstone.

Following Oak Casket's gaze, you see another newer tombstone behind the hat bearing Nasha's name and date of death.

Oak Casket: A conflict broke out when the Executive Committee came to clear the Mania contamination. An incendiary bomb hit her room.

Oak Casket: The fire burned for three hours before it was extinguished. Nasha's family asked me to help find the remains in the ruins. When I got there, there was nothing but charred debris and ashes from the fire.

Oak Casket: The fire swallowed the residential building. I heard cries from both adults and children. The firefighters were stuck outside the block. By the time they got in, the flames had reached the roof.

You can almost visualize the fire from Oak Casket's description. The sky is dyed red. The flames blend into the sunset, like a flock of bright red sparrows struggling to escape.

Not far from Nasha's tombstone, there is a cluster of dilapidated stelae. You stop there and find that the greater part of the nearest tombstone has been blasted away, leaving only a small broken stub. The ground has also been damaged, revealing half a medicine bottle shallowly buried in the soil.

Half of the glass bottle is shattered, spilling shiny buttons and broken glass bits all over the ground.

Chief: What is this?

Oak Casket: Stars.

You remain silent, quietly waiting for Oak Casket's elaboration.

Oak Casket: His name was Humar. He was bullied from a young age for having white hair and skin. His mother gave birth to him in a basement and he was kept away from sunlight for many years.

Oak Casket: People told him that his mother went where the stars were, so he started collecting these *stars*. Later, someone told him that the Drifter Camp was in a high and starry place and that when the night was clear, you could even touch them, so he came. Only eight years old, he came carrying his only belongings.

She picks up the bottle and carefully puts the buttons back into the remaining half of the bottle.

Chief: There are so many buttons. He must have spent a long time collecting them.

Oak Casket: I collected some for him, but he didn't want them. He only kept the ones he collected himself.

Oak Casket: I still remember his expression at that time. He liked those buttons but couldn't accept them... His eyes were wide open. It was quite amusing.

Oak Casket: He asked me where I got so many *stars*, so I took him where I found the buttons. The morgue in the monastery.

Chief: Then what happened next?

Oak Casket: After that, he became my playmate.

Oak Casket: When I was a child, I often went to the morgue by myself. It was the quietest place in the monastery. I brought him to the room, thinking it would scare him...

Oak Casket: But he was just envious of me, for I could have such a big room all to myself... Oh dear.

Oak Casket lets out a soft gasp. You step forward to help steady the glass bottle, catching the *stars* almost spilling out.

Oak Casket's gaze travels from the glass bottle to you. The light from the bottle is reflected in her pupils, warming up her gaze as she looks at you.

Oak Casket: One night, he appeared in the morgue again, but this time... he was unrecognizable.

Oak Casket: He was tricked into climbing a high-rise building in Syndicate to pick stars, and ended up falling.

Oak Casket: His final regret was only a bottle full of buttons.

Oak Casket rises and walks into the cluster of broken stelae.

Oak Casket: Most of the people buried around here were from the monastery... These people fell into eternal slumber because of the living.

Oak Casket: You see, the dead won't hurt you, only the living will.

Her tone is calm, as if she's merely stating an objective fact.

You want to object and tell her that's not true, that there are many trustworthy and good people among the living. But perhaps because the wind in the graveyard is too quiet, somehow you can't bring yourself to speak.

The two of you proceed in silence for a while until Oak Casket stops in front of a somewhat weathered tombstone. She stares at the stele for a long time without saying a word.

Chief: Whose tombstone is this?

Oak Casket: I don't know.

Oak Casket: But what remains here... is the regret I cannot resolve.

Oak Casket: I've resolved the regrets of the dead from all walks of life. Those who passed away in accidents, those who chose to go because they couldn't see hope...

Oak Casket: Yet I've never truly understood the message the person lying beneath this tombstone has left me.

You reach out your hand toward Oak Casket.

Oak Casket: I'm afraid it's difficult. She didn't leave much information.

Oak Casket is much gentler in the Romanesque Hall than in the interrogation room.

Chief: Give it a try.

Chief: This time there are two of us. We should be able to hear more.

She looks into your eyes, as if she's glimpsing through something you don't know. In the end, she takes off her gloves and covers your eyes.

The chill of Oak Casket's palms still remains over your eyes. Your vision is blank.

Gradually, a warm light arises, dispelling the chill in your eyes.

You find yourself in a meadow under the warm sunlight. It's a beautiful sunny day.

Perhaps because the tombstone has stood there for too long, you can't make out clearly what you're seeing. You can only vaguely see a girl reciting something under a tree.

Her voice is so soft that you can't hear what she's reciting. You turn to look at Oak Casket, who's also straining her ears to listen.

Chief: What is she reciting?

Oak Casket doesn't respond.

Meanwhile, the figure of an adult approaches and joins the girl in her recitation.

Like a melody, the peaceful and warm voice floats in the air with the sunlight. The light about the girl grows brighter and brighter.

???: Hardships... Traveler... Light...

These words somehow ring a bell.

Wanting to hear more clearly, you strain to step forward, but end up stepping into a sea of white light.

The two of you have returned to the stele.

The link of the shackles still remains, so you feel the repressed emotion from Oak Casket almost as soon as you come back.

Why? The sun was so warm and mellow.

Chief: Did you just see the same things as you had previously?

Oak Casket: ...

Chief: Before we came, we made an agreement that you would do your best to assist me.

Oak Casket: ...This time, I could hear her recitation.

You sense the emotion coming from the shackles, guiding Oak Casket.

Chief: The poem she recited... I only heard a few words, but I'm certain I've heard it somewhere before.

Chief: Do you remember?

Oak Casket falls silent again, but through the shackles, you can feel what she's feeling... Something like contempt or anger... Or perhaps... sadness.

Oak Casket: Such a foolish and despicable poem...

Oak Casket: I've forgotten about it.

Oak Casket: Do I need to recall it, Chief?

Her tone is somewhat harsh, but you feel that her sadness outweighs her anger.

You don't want her to be stuck in this kind of pain, so you shake your head slightly.

You stand next to Oak Casket for a while until the emotion on the other end of the shackles subsides.

Chief: You said that everyone sleeping here was connected to the monastery.

Chief: Are there any other people from the monastery still alive? Or people related to the monastery. They may know something.

Chief: ...If remembering will cause you pain, then let me find these answers.

After a moment of silence, Oak Casket finally answers you.

Oak Casket: There's a doctor who's closely related to the monastery... but I'm not sure if he's still alive.

Chief: It's okay. Let's try to find him.

Before leaving the stele garden, you look back. The land where the oak tree once grew is empty.

There's still not a breath of wind in the stele garden. The tombstones are gray, just as those *stars* sealed in the glass jar are.

## Oak Casket - Interrogation III

> Intelligence before completion:
> Oak Casket once mentioned that everyone buried beneath those monuments had something to do with the monastery.
> Since this doctor has close ties with the monastery, perhaps you can gain information relevant to the obsession from him.

> Intelligence after completion:
> Oak Casket shared some of her past with you.
> From her story and her attitude, you were able to confirm that the obsession has something to do with her.
> You need to talk to Oak Casket.

### Interrogation Transcript

The journey isn't peaceful. You pass collapsed houses and ruins while Oak Casket remains unusually silent. The only thing that alleviates your unease is the faint, unique fragrance on her.

You keep wondering what the little girl was reciting, and what the light represents. The recitation sounded like a prayer from someone from the monastery, but Oak Casket's unusual reaction tells you that this is no simple prayer.

Thinking this, you look at Oak Casket walking ahead of you.

She seems to never want to stop for the scenery around her, yet she's the messenger who always stops and turns back to listen to those who can't go on.

Oak Casket: What's the matter?

Noticing that you haven't kept up, Oak Casket turns around and asks simply.

Chief: Nothing... I'm just a little surprised by the lack of signs of habitation here.

Oak Casket: This place...

Oak Casket looks at the flat land around her with a calm expression that doesn't give away any emotions.

Oak Casket: It's because this place is a mass grave.

Oak Casket: Initially, stray dogs would forage around here in packs. No one wanted to be near here.

Oak Casket: Later, to curb Mania, this place was hastily buried and covered up. But even though the smell was gone, most people still avoided this area. Only until recently... After the war ended, people began to remember this place after Romanesque's been destroyed.

Only then do you see some recent mounds on the land originally covered with weeds. Not far away, someone is erecting a plank of wood in front of a mound as a makeshift gravestone.

As the man plants the plank into the earth, he keeps muttering something, his voice drifting in the air along with the flickering phosphorescence.

*You shall endure hardships... Meet the golden falcon... Traveler will guide you to the light...*

You finally understand why those words in the vision sounded so familiar.

**【Advance】**

- I've heard that poem before.

**end**

Oak Casket: Perhaps you have.

**【Advance】**

- From you.

**end**

Chief: You were reciting it when I entered the interrogation room.

Oak Casket: *You shall endure hardships and surpass all stelae in this world... You shall meet the golden falcon, and the winged traveler will guide you to the light...*

Oak Casket: ...

Oak Casket: Now you want to ask: Where did you learn about this poem?

Oak Casket: Well... This is an elegy that reminds the dead to let go of all suffering and prays that they can travel to the beautiful and peaceful land of eternity.

Chief: Have you heard someone recite it?

Oak Casket: More precisely, someone recited it for me.

Chief: But you just said this is an elegy for the dead...

Before entering the interrogation room, you looked over the investigation report on Oak Casket.

She was born during Syndicate's most chaotic period. Orphaned since a young age, she was adopted by a priest from the monastery. When she was about thirteen, the monastery was destroyed in a gang fight, and the priest died that year.

Ever since then, the teenage girl has disappeared from the official records.

How many deaths and departures must she have witnessed over all these years to become what she is now?

Oak Casket: You're looking very earnest now. Are you that eager to delve into my past?

Chief: As the Chief of the Bureau, and as your friend, I want to know.

Oak Casket gives a slight smile, but it doesn't melt the frosty chill on her face.

Oak Casket: Very well... I'm looking forward to your response to this story, little one.

Oak Casket: It took place when this mass grave hadn't been covered up yet.

Oak Casket: At that time, I was one of the hundreds of corpses in the pit. But I was not dead. I was thrown in alive.

The smell of blood stung her nose. The little girl struggled to open her eyes.

So stinky. So disgusting. I want to get out of this place.

But she couldn't move. Rigid bodies were lying on top of her limbs, belly, and shoulders. The body she was leaning against was also slowly turning cold.

She finally realized what was happening. She looked up and saw the gray sky several meters above.

The gravely injured girl was lying at the bottom of the pit helplessly.

Little Girl: I'm still alive...

Weak and with her throat parched, she could only make a faint noise.

Little Girl: Please... Help me...

Soon after, footsteps came from above the pit.

Two figures stood at the edge of the pit and noticed the little girl at the bottom with her eyes open.

Little Girl: Father... Doctor...

Little Girl: Help me... Help me, please...

They seemed to be muttering something. A moment later, another body was thrown down.

Old Priest & Doctor: *You shall endure hardships and surpass all stelae in this world. You shall meet the golden falcon, and the winged traveler will guide you to the light...*

Hearing the words, she watched more and more bodies being thrown into the pit. Dirt was being shoveled onto her head, covering her view.

Little Girl: *You shall meet the golden falcon, and the winged traveler will guide you to the light.*

Oak Casket: What's the point? He told me to seek the light when he cast me into the dark.

Oak Casket recalls placidly, her voice gradually merging with the little girl's in the vision. A rough guess arises in your mind.

Oak Casket: The living believe their prayers can cleanse the souls of the dead, helping them find relief. They cover up their sins with such grandiose rhetoric, as if they can earn forgiveness from the dead just like that.

Oak Casket: This poem won't bring light. It's just an excuse for the living to absolve themselves and alleviate their guilt.

Oak Casket: Once they finish reciting the poem, the death of the deceased has nothing to do with the living anymore.

Oak Casket looks at you composedly.

Oak Casket: Alright, little one, you got your story...

Oak Casket: In return, can you give me a guiding answer?

You can't find the strength or words to argue with her anymore. After all, what words carry enough weight to comfort who was once discarded by the living?

Oak Casket shrugs. Without taking one last glimpse at this land, she walks straight ahead.

You remain silent all the way, following Oak Casket to an old residential building.

The building is on the verge of collapse, the exterior wrapped in a mess of electrical cables. You climb the narrow staircase to the first floor and knock on the door, but no one answers.

Chief: Are you sure this is the place?

Oak Casket: I can't be mistaken.

Oak Casket produces a thin slice of metal out of nowhere and slides it through the door gap effortlessly, like performing surgery. When the door opens, the entire frame shakes like it may collapse at any moment.

As soon as the door opens, a stench hits you. Several rats squeal and scurry past you. The living room is filled with all sorts of garbage, from which a bunch of flies take off and try to land on you.

You walk through a dark corridor. The light should be on, as you can hear the hum of electricity in the light bulb, but it's not lit. Perhaps it's stayed on for too long. The faucet is stuck with rust, and occasionally you can hear water dripping.

Oak Casket: Hmm, he's still alive.

Chief: What?

Oak Casket draws the curtains of the room open. With the faint morning light filtering in through the window, you see a person lying on the bed.

Half of the body is shriveled up under a faded old quilt, revealing only a dried and wrinkled face and sparse white hair. If it weren't for his breathing, you'd almost mistake it for a dried-up skeleton.

Chief: Is this the doctor?

Oak Casket doesn't speak and simply nods. Her expression remains indifferent. You can't tell what she thinks of this situation.

You lean in toward the doctor, trying to wake him up.

Chief: Excuse me, Sir.

The doctor doesn't move. You smell the stench of bedsores. He must have been paralyzed for quite some time, and with no one taking care of him, his bed reeks of excrement.

Chief: Is this... nutritional fluid?

You see a half-used bottle of liquid lying on the bedside table, guessing it might be what's keeping him alive.

He's neither dead nor alive, cut off from all possibilities of communication.

Oak Casket: What a shame. He's worthless in his state.

Chief: ...What?

Oak Casket: Isn't he? In his current state, he can't even speak clearly.

Oak Casket: But if he died, we could at least learn something from him through my ability.

Oak Casket: The dead are honest while the living are furtive. The dead are tranquil while the living are restless. The dead are eternally peaceful and loyal; as the root of all evil, the living create nothing but chaos.

Oak Casket: You see, I like the dead a little more than the living.

Oak Casket's eyes somewhat soften as she says this.

Oak Casket: Given his condition, only death can grant him relief.

Oak Casket: What's the point of clinging to life in such a body in this world? The living don't need him, but the dead would surely welcome a new friend breaking free from worldly ties.
