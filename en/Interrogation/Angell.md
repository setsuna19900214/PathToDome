# Angell Interrogation

## Angell - Interrogation I

> After verifying some uncertain details of a previous case with Angell and learning about how she's been doing, you decide to show her "the sunlit world" in a different way and plan an outdoor day-off with her.

Angell: When you look up from this angle, you can see the sky through the narrow gap between the high walls of the alley. Despite its small size, the sky appears incredibly blue and vast.

Angell: I don't normally have the chance or desire to gaze upon the sky like this, but today, I realize it's quite nice.

Angell: The only thing that makes this feel less comfortable is the rock against my upper back. But that's okay, I've grown numb to the pain.

Angell: A flock of white birds fly across that tiny gap, free and unrestrained. In the meantime, my life is slowly draining away.

Angell: I always knew this would happen eventually, one way or another. After all, tomorrow is not guaranteed in this line of work; my life could end anytime and anywhere, nothing surprising.

Angell: My vision starts to blur, a sign of hallucinations due to extreme weakness.

Angell: I hear jumbled footsteps that appear close one moment, and distant the next.

Angell: Out of nowhere, I think of the goldfish in the corner of the living room, their blood-red tails swaying in the water before my eyes.

Angell: Some say that before death, one sees what they can't let go of, and the person they want to see the most.

Chief: ...Angell? Angell!

Angell: Seems like it's true.

Chief: What happened?! Stay with me...

Angell: Because just before I fall into complete darkness, I see your face.

In a corner of the MBCC cafeteria, you sit opposite Angell at a rather inconspicuous dining table.

Between you are a few empty plates that were filled with side dishes, along with a stack of bowls.

Since it's not mealtime, there aren't many people in the cafeteria, but Angell still receives some curious gaze from the people who pass by.

However, she remains calm and composed, like she usually does, and finishes her sixth bowl of red bean soup, unfazed by the unfamiliar surroundings.

Finding Angell unconscious in a Syndicate alley nearly sent you into a panic. You hastily rush her back to the MBCC for urgent medical care.

Fortunately, the physical examination showed that she simply passed out from extreme hunger, and she was fine after some glucose.

Chief: When was the last time you ate...?

Angell eats quietly, even elegantly, but her speed is astonishing. As her bowl empties once more, you push your untouched bowl of red bean soup towards her.

She accepts without hesitation, simply nodding her thanks.

Angell: Can't remember, but it's been quite a while.

Angell brushes off the question, clearly unwilling to dwell on this topic.

Chief: Okay...

After she devours another bowl of soup, you offer:

Chief: Do you want more? If it's still not enough, I can cook some more...

Angell: Thanks, but it's okay.

She sets down her spoon and the empty bowl.

Angell: I'm full now.

She looks at you, then at the empty bowl in front of her, and smiles with a hint of nostalgia.

Angell: Your red bean soup tastes amazing, just like always. I never thought I'd have it again.

She smiles at you across the table, reminding you of the time back at the safe house. You smile back at her.

Chief: Nonsense. I'm right here. You can always tell me when you have a craving.

Angell: ...Thank you.

After expressing her gratitude once more, Angell gets up, ready to leave, but you hold her back.

Chief: Angell, wait.

Chief: I know you're more than capable of getting out of the Bureau whenever you want, but would you mind if we talk a bit more elsewhere? It's just the standard procedure around here.

Angell: What procedure?

Despite not knowing what you mean by "procedure," Angell follows you, heading deeper into the Bureau.

Angell takes a seat in the interrogation room, scanning the shackles hanging from the armrests, various security devices in the room, and the video camera in the corner that's been turned off.

Angell: Is this where you usually interrogate Sinners?

She sounds indifferent, showing no emotion, but you can't help feeling nervous. Worried she might misunderstand, you hurriedly explain.

Chief: Don't worry. There are no guards outside, and I didn't put the shackles on you. This is just the standard procedure for all new Sinners.

Chief: Just think of it as us finding a quieter place to talk, okay?

Angell's gaze softens as she looks back at you.

Angell: I'm alright, but you seem really nervous.

Chief: ...

Angell leaves you speechless for a moment, then the corner of her lips curls up. Seemingly in a good mood, she initiates a new topic of conversation.

Angell: You want to talk, let's talk.

Angell: Let's start with... how did you find me?

You didn't just stumble upon her, of course.

After sending you back, Angell vanished completely like a raindrop fallen into the deep waters of the dark web.

It's difficult for ordinary people and the authorities to reach the deeper ends of the dark web. You found nothing despite using every possible means and searching through every channel you could think of.

But yesterday, you saw a post about "Angell's whereabouts" on the surface of the dark web. You took your chances and bought the information from them, and that's how you found Angell, who had passed out on the street.

Chief: Someone was selling your intel on the dark web yesterday. Is it because you made too many enemies in the kidnapping mission, and they wanted revenge?

Angell: ...It could be.

Angell doesn't seem too surprised, merely nodding thoughtfully.

She doesn't question who provided the information, but you couldn't tell her even if she did. The poster used a disposable account, and you couldn't find any useful information from it.

Chief: Speaking of which...

**【Advance】**

- Could you help me with something?

**end**

Angell: What?

You open a file and push it across the table to Angell.

Chief: This is the operation report for the entire case.

Chief: I couldn't see many things clearly when they were happening, but I'm beginning to piece them together while reviewing the case and writing the report.

Chief: There are still some details I'm not too sure about. Could you help verify them?

Angell glances at the report and says without hesitation:

Angell: Yes, I can.

Chief: Alright, let's start with the background of this case.

Chief: With the reconstruction of the West District underway, the gangs found it increasingly difficult to survive, forcing them to flee to this mysterious dark web created by some unknown figure.

Chief: The dark web's covert and intricate nature allows them to carry out a series of attacks on key figures in the West District, including me, making it difficult for the authorities to respond quickly.

Angell: ...

Angell narrows her eyes, holding back what she wants to say and letting you continue.

Chief: This was our analysis at first, but as the operation unfolded, we realized we had overlooked a very important force—

Chief: The masterminds hidden in the deeper area of the dark web.

Angell nods slightly in agreement.

Chief: As you said before, people like us only have access to the surface of the dark web. So, at first, I assumed that the first round of the counterattacks I secretly organized using your account had eliminated all the enemies.

Chief: But then you told me about the deeper area of the dark web, and the results of the operation showed that there indeed was a hidden force—the real gang leaders and the Hypercube company who are upset by the new mines.

Chief: They were the real masterminds and financiers. The people we caught before were just thugs and minions.

Chief: We didn't realize they were behind the scenes and almost let them slip away. If it weren't for your covert assistance, we would never been able to solve the case.

Angell: ...

Angell's gaze flickers.

You flip through the report and point to the "Joint Operation" section.

Chief: You put news about me on the dark web, asking those who were interested to come find you with their offers. But selling me for a good price wasn't your real intention, was it?

Since there's nothing to hide anymore, Angell admits openly.

Angell: No. I leaked your information to bait them.

Angell: Those people were so eager to get rid of you and the news threw them off. They were worried you'd be rescued, so they hastily recruited some people to make a second assassination attempt.

Angell: They were ill-prepared. That's why they gave themselves away, and finally gave you the chance to solve the case.

You nod with a smile.

Chief: And that's not the only time you've helped me.

Angell looks at you, surprised and puzzled.

Angell: What do you mean?

Chief: I'm talking about the time you went out every night and came back injured. You never told me what you were up to, but come to think of it, you must have been dealing with those who wanted to kill me, right?

Angell: ...Yes.

**【Advance】**

- Thank you.

**end**

Chief: For protecting me all this time.

Angell: No need.

Angell shakes her head, but her gaze softens.

Angell: I was just doing what the client wanted. The instructions were very clear: kidnap and guard the MBCC Chief until delivery, no killing involved.

You ponder for a moment, a new doubt crossing your mind.

Chief: Was that job from a gang?

Angell: Yes.

Chief: Isn't that strange? Those people clearly wanted me dead.

Chief: ...Perhaps the job was tampered with?

Angell: It's possible.

Chief: A dark web case is inherently far more challenging to tackle than typical cases, and this time we even found out about the dark web's deeper layer. What exactly is this dark web? What encryption technology does it use? When was it developed, and by whom?

Angell goes deep into thought for a moment.

Angell: I only know that the technology used by the dark web is extremely bizarre and highly secure, making it difficult to crack... I know nothing about its origins and creators.

Chief: Then, who do you think has the ability to interfere with the jobs in the deep dark web?

Angell: ...

After a brief silence, Angell lowers her gaze.

Angell: No idea. I'm just a contractor who only cares about completing the jobs.

Chief: I see.

Angell moves in her seat to adjust her posture, and you catch a glimpse of a few wounds under the hem of her clothes that weren't there before.

**【Advance】**

- Put away the documents

**end**

Chief: I've got everything I need. Now, let's talk about you.

Chief: What happened to you? How come you passed out on the streets?

Angell: I had no money, couldn't afford to eat.

Chief: ...

Such a candid and straightforward answer leaves you momentarily speechless.

This is not an easy thing to admit openly, but Angell shows no sign of embarrassment.

Angell: This job has caused me a great deal of trouble. Though you might have caught all the gangsters, many others took this opportunity to seek revenge. Many of them were also hitmen.

Angell: They thought I had betrayed the dark web and turned to the authorities. I know a lot of their secrets, so those guys are trying to get rid of me.

Angell: I've been followed closely by them 24/7. I couldn't withdraw money, or go back to the safe house to get food...

Angell: That's why I ended up like that.

Despite her light and matter-of-fact tone, you can imagine the hardship she went through.

Chief: ...Did that job affect your credibility on the dark web?

Angell: Not really. No one cares about credibility there. After I deal with those guys, business will only get better, because I've proven myself.

Angell: Besides, it's not like I betrayed my client. I did exactly what the job asked me to do. They wanted me to protect you by kidnapping you, they will thank me for it.

Chief: Good.

The conversation winds down. Angell stares at you for a bit.

Angell: Anyway, thanks for searching for me and the meal. Once everything is settled, I will repay you.

You understand the meaning behind her words—she doesn't plan to stay at the MBCC.

**【Advance】**

- "Repay"

**end**

Angell: What do you mean?

Chief: Well, you've got nowhere else to go right now, why not stay here for a couple more days?

Angell: ...Is this how you want me to repay you?

Chief: Yeah. I happen to be on a break at the moment, so you could join me for a bit of a holiday.

When you were living together, you had been trying to show her the beauty of the sunlit world by picking certain jobs for her, but you also wanted to take her out on the streets to experience the life of ordinary people.

This is something you've always wanted to do since you first met her, but you never had the chance. And now, this might be the perfect opportunity.

She always says she's meant to lurk in the shadows, but once she feels the sun's warmth and witnesses the flourishing lives under the sunlight, perhaps she will change her mind.

Angell: ...

She frowns, likely sensing your intention.

However, perhaps due to your help this time, or because she's gotten used to you planning things for her, she cannot bring herself to refuse.

Noticing her silence, you give a sly smile.

Chief: Alright, since you're not saying anything, I'll assume you agree.

Chief: I'll plan our trip for the next couple of days.

## Angell - Interrogation II

> You and Angell come to a newly established farmers' market in West District, becoming one of the many vendors.
> Business is initially slow, but after Angell deals with some troublemaking thugs, your vegetable stall is swept clean by the cheering crowd.

Chief: Fresh and organic veggies! Have a look!

The new farmers' market in West District today welcomes a new stall and its two owners. You and Angell, each sitting on a small stool, have occupied a prime spot at the entrance.

Under a canopy supported by four sticks, a variety of fresh, dewy vegetables are laid out. You got up at dawn to fetch them from the farmers.

Chief: Tomatoes, cabbages, potatoes, cucumbers, carrots! We have all you need!

Dressed in casual clothes and a straw hat, you energetically call out to potential customers, all the while watching the people as they come and go.

After the Rustfire incident, the West District's economy is slowly reviving, which has led to the emergence of large markets like this.

Despite the simplicity of the stalls and the occasional troublemaking thugs in the area, the market remains lively and busy.

It's early morning, yet the market is already bustling with customers and vendors, their enthusiastic shouting filling the air. Everyone's eyes are filled with anticipation for a better life, a sight you've never seen in the West District before.

Angell: You do this on your break?

A calm, deep voice comes from beside you, prompting you to turn your head and look at Angell. Unlike you, who has blended into the bustling market, she stands by your side like a shadow, still carrying the black bag with her blade.

Chief: Why not? Just peddling some vegetables. Compared to what I do at MBCC, this is definitely a break.

Chief: Besides, I've always wanted to experience the life in West District.

Chief: All we have here are trivial daily matters, no assassins or mercenaries seeking revenge. The biggest danger is probably someone slipping on a banana peel or some rotten vegetables on the ground.

Angell: Really...

After what you said, Angell gradually relaxes, lowers her guard, and starts to observe this new environment.

She looks at the customers and vendors, listening to them chatting, gossiping, or haggling, her eyes filled with curiosity and interest, seemingly fascinated by everything happening around her.

You smile without saying anything, and continue calling out to attract passersby. Right now, you blend in perfectly with the hundreds of ordinary people bustling about for their livelihoods in the market.

Who could have guessed that the two people running this little stall are a former fearsome hitman on the dark web and the hero of the West District.

Despite sitting at the perfect spot and your voice going hoarse from shouting, not many people have stopped by your stall for some reason.

As you take a moment to rest your throat and drink some water, a lady stops in front of your stall, and you immediately put down the water bottle to greet her:

Chief: Hey there, want some tomatoes? Some of the freshest ones you can find around here! Look at the size, and the color!

Middle-Aged Woman: ...Hmm, not bad. Give me two of these, and some cabbage as well.

She holds the tomatoes in her hand, scrutinizing them from all angles before finally nodding her head.

Middle-Aged Woman: I'm buying a lot here, how about a discount?

She raises her head with the imposing look she uses when haggling with vendors, but as soon as her gaze locks with Angell's, she backs away a little, quickly putting down the tomatoes.

Middle-Aged Woman: Hey!

Middle-Aged Woman: ...I changed my mind. You can keep them.

You immediately grab the lady, attempting to salvage the deal.

Chief: Of course, I'll give you a discount. How about I round it down for you?

She seems tempted by your offer, but after sneaking a glance at Angell again, she shudders and pushes your hand away, hurrying off while muttering in fear.

Middle-Aged Woman: Oh dear, is she trying to kill someone with that look?

Angell turns to you, somewhat puzzled.

Angell: ...Is she talking about me?

Chief: Probably...

Angell: What's wrong with my expression? This is my serious face, and many clients liked it. Why isn't it working here?

Her puzzled expression amuses you. You wave your hands, sitting back down and stretching lazily.

Chief: Forget it, nothing's wrong with slow business. It's nice to get some sun.

Boy: Granny, you're back!

Old Lady: Sigh, that stingy butcher... We're all neighbors here, and he wouldn't even help butcher a chicken...

Boy: Ah...

Old Lady: Don't worry. I'll keep asking around. You'll definitely have your chicken soup today.

You look towards the fruit stall next to yours. The stall owner, an old lady, is holding a tied-up chicken, while her grandson stands beside her, looking dejected.

**【Advance】**

- Inquire

**end**

Chief: Hello there, can I help you with anything?

The old lady walks over, holding her grandson by the hand and slightly lifting the chicken in her other hand to show you.

Old Lady: Sigh, one of our relatives has sent us a chicken, and I thought a chicken soup would be perfect for my grandson here, so I went to ask the butcher to handle it for me.

Old Lady: But he refused to help because the chicken wasn't bought from his shop.

Chief: That's unfortunate...

She hesitates as she looks at you and Angell, somewhat embarrassed.

Old Lady: Um... Could you help me butcher this chicken?

You turn around and ask Angell.

Chief: Angell, do you think you can help out?

Angell answers with her actions instead—she swiftly draws a gleaming blade from her bag, staring intensely at the chicken in the old lady's hand, chilling the air around her.

Boy: Ahhh! She's so scary. Granny, forget the chicken! I wanna go home... Wahhh...

Just as you are about to take the chicken from the old lady and hand it to Angell, the grandson starts crying. He snatches the chicken from his grandma and runs back to his stall, cowering in a corner and trembling as he stares at you and Angell.

Old Lady: Huh? Sigh, this kid... Please don't take what he said to heart. Sorry for taking up your time...

Most likely feeling apologetic, the old lady picks out a generous amount of vegetables from your stand without haggling. You pack a full bag of vegetables and hand it over with a comforting smile.

Chief: It's alright. Are you sure you don't need help with the chicken?

Old Lady: Thank you, but don't worry about it. Oh, and I've been watching you for a while; this peddling business isn't as easy as it looks.

The old lady continues as she takes Angell's hand and pats it. The latter seems a bit uncomfortable but doesn't pull away.

Old Lady: When I first started my stall, I couldn't sell anything either, but it got better over time. Keep at it. My stall is just over there. We're friends now, ask away if you need help.

Chief: Of course, thank you very much.

After finishing the conversation and watching the old lady return to her stall, you are soon drawn to the commotion not far away.

The nearby vendors and customers all drop what they were doing and walk over; you also grab Angell and approach the crowd to see what's happening.

Hooligan: Stop talking and pay up!

You squeeze into the crowd. A few arrogant hooligans come into view, snatching a few crumpled bills from an emaciated man's hand.

The leader scoffs contemptuously, then frowns and curses after counting the money.

Hooligan: That's all you have?! What the **** do you mean?

Hooligan: We work hard here every day to maintain order and keep this place safe. You think it's easy?

Elderly Man: This is all the money I've earned these past two days. My wife is sick and I have to take care of her, please have mercy...

The old man pleads pitifully.

Hooligan: Did I ask? I don't ******* care even if she dies! Just give me my money, you hear?!

With new markets and profits, the rats that once lurked in the shadows now emerge. These troublemakers are some low-level thugs who couldn't even join a gang in the past.

Now that almost all of the gangs are gone, they seem to think that they can finally take over and rule the West District.

These thugs have grown accustomed to dominating this place, and the people of the West District have endured oppression for so long. Even though times have changed, the influence of gangs still lingers, and people are still too intimidated to fight back.

Boy: Baddies! Stop bullying him!

Suddenly, a young but sharp voice breaks the silence. You look down and see the boy who Angell had scared earlier; he has let go of his grandma and made his way to the front of the crowd, his face reddened as he yells at the thugs.

Hooligan: Get lost, you little brat.

The thugs ignore the boy, shoving him impatiently. He tumbles to the ground, scraping his palms and knees.

Seeing this, his grandma rushes over, positioning herself in front of her grandson like a lioness guarding her cub, and begins cursing at them.

Old Lady: Protection fee? Nonsense! What did you protect?!

Old Lady: When the West District was in trouble, none of you scum was there. Now that the Black Ring is gone, you're out here pushing us around again.

Old Lady: Let me tell you, times have changed. Those gangs have all been wiped out, and now you're just a bunch of nobodies. You don't get to boss us around anymore!

Hearing the old lady's words, murmurs ripple through the crowd, with many nodding in approval, their faces no longer as terrified as before.

Hooligan: You ************* wanna mess with me?!

In a fit of rage, the thug spits on the ground and, with his accomplices, menacingly brandishes their weapons, ready to strike the old lady.

Hooligan: I'm going to teach you a lesson, and show you who runs the West District these days!

Just as the leader of the thugs is about to strike, his bat is suddenly sent flying by someone. The crowd only catches a glimpse of a silver flash before Angell's blade is already inches from the thug's throat.

You quickly ask the spectators to step back a bit to prevent them from getting hurt, and to give Angell room.

Chief: Careful now, everyone step back a little...

Before you can finish, Angell has already kicked those guys out of the market's fence. They scream as they fly over your heads, crashing onto the newly paved road outside with a dull thud.

These lowlifes may lack other talents, but they are experts at fleeing for their lives. From the brief "fight" just now, they also realize that Angell is clearly not someone they can mess with, so they immediately turn tail and bolt.

After a brief silence, thunderous applause and cheers erupt around you and Angell.

Even the little boy who was scared of her gets up from the ground, runs over, and grabs the hem of Angell's clothes, his eyes sparkling with admiration.

Boy: Wow! You're amazing, miss...

Angell: ...

Angell is obviously taken aback by his reaction. Unsure of how to respond and afraid of frightening him again, she only averts her gaze, frozen in place.

You step forward with a smile to rescue her by taking the boy's hand and sending him back to his grandma.

But before you two can catch a breath, the cheering subsides, and the customers at the market, along with the vendors, rush over to support your business.

The two of you busily collect money and bag vegetables. The once hard-to-sell vegetables are quickly swept up by the eager crowd, leaving not even a single leaf behind.

Chief: Good, good, we can close the stall early.

You count the money you've just collected and are quite pleased with today's earnings. You hand half of it to Angell.

She looks at you, puzzled.

Angell: What's this for?

Chief: Your pay for the day.

Angell: But I did nothing.

Chief: It's not all about the goods in business, advertising is important too.

Chief: You handled those thugs skillfully; that was great advertising.

You pack everything up, throw away the empty boxes that were used to store the goods, and put the stools, cloth, and sticks into a corner. You stretch leisurely afterward.

Chief: Alright, everything's sold out and now we've got some money, let's go enjoy life.

## Angell - Interrogation III

> The movie themed around the Drifter Camp and Rustfire evokes many emotions and memories in you. You try to subtly persuade Angell to start a new life like the characters in the film. Angell responds by sharing her past, making it clear she belongs in darkness and the underworld, with no chance of coming back under the sunlight.

There aren't many people at the cinema on a weekday afternoon, with only six or seven viewers scattered in the vast place. As you and Angell take your seats in the back row with drinks and popcorn, the movie begins.

The film being shown is a recent blockbuster, an epic story adapted from the incidents of Rustfire and Drifter Camp.

The movie starts with a wide shot of a city in ruins, with a massive and intimidating Black Ring floating above it.

Your eyes light up. Pleasantly surprised, you tug at Angell's sleeve.

Chief: Wow, look at that Black Ring, it's pretty well done. Looks like they really invested in the CGI for this movie!

Angell: ...Okay.

Angell glances at you and nods in agreement under your expectant gaze.

The movie's reaching its climax. As one of the witnesses of the story and character inspirations for the movie, you feel like you're revisiting those indelible memories.

The journey was fraught with many hardships and difficulties, but now that it's all in the past, looking back on those times brings a sense of reflection and relief...

Everyone's efforts and perseverance bear meaning, and those sacrifices were not wasted. Darkness will fade, and the hopeful future that once seemed unreachable will eventually come.

Just like what you saw at the farmers' market today, the West District still has a long way to recovery, and various problems remain. But now, people no longer remain silent in the face of oppression and choose to stand up and fight back.

They all truly believe that the era of despair has become the past, and the future of this place will only get better.

As you contemplate, many amusing anecdotes from those tough times also reemerge in your memories.

In the movie, the group of protagonists overcomes obstacles and vanquishes their enemies along the way, making for a thrilling journey to witness.

Chief: Hold on, this part is way too exaggerated and idealized...

Chief: We were in such a mess back then; everyone was wounded and scattered. It was far from heroic.

In the most critical moments of battle, a character keeps cracking jokes to lighten the mood, drawing not only eye rolls from his companions on the screen but also a few muffled laughs from the audience.

Chief: Hahaha, that line does sound like something he would say...

Immersed in the movie, you find it difficult to suppress your comments with someone sitting next to you. As you enthusiastically share your thoughts, you turn and notice Angell looking at you as well.

You smile, a bit embarrassed, and lean in to whisper.

Chief: Sorry, you can't focus because I'm talking too much?

Angell: No.

Angell pauses, then adds.

Angell: Your commentary... is very interesting.

After saying this, Angell slowly turns her head back to focus on the movie, the light from the screen flickering on her profile.

At the end of the film, a once sunless alley is finally bathed in light, and the laughter of children dispels the dreadful silence. The last shot lingers on a small white flower blooming amidst the dark mire.

The screen fades to black, and the lights in the cinema come on, leaving a long list of credits rolling.

With the soothing end credits music playing, the other viewers slowly exit, leaving just you and Angell in the now empty cinema.

Feeling stiff from sitting for so long, you stretch and turn to Angell with a smile.

Chief: What do you think of the movie?

Angell: Why do you ask?

Chief: After overcoming all the hardships, these people finally live happily and comfortably in the new West District. Sounds pretty good, don't you think?

Angell is smart. She picks up on what you're trying to say, but she shows no reaction and simply replies softly:

Angell: Yeah, but I'm not like them.

Angell: From the very beginning, they had their goals and beliefs. They wanted to bask in the sunlight, and to achieve that goal, they tried their best to break free from the darkness.

Angell: At the same time, there are also people like me on the fringes of the world, who don't see themselves in that picture.

Chief: What kind of life do you want?

Angell: The life I have now is good enough. I've already chosen my path.

Chief: ...

What made you lose your desire for the sunlight? How did you become who you are today? And why did you make such a choice?

You have many questions, but looking into her tranquil eyes, you suddenly don't know where to start.

There's a moment of silence between you two, Angell is the first to break it.

Angell: I've heard that in your world, people like me are considered criminals that need to be captured and shackled, is that true? I'm not too sure.

Chief: ...Yes, if you don't mind.

Her eyes darken.

Angell: But I won't stay at the MBCC.

Chief: I know.

**【Advance】**

- Shackle commission.

**end**

Chief: Let's turn this into a commission. I put the shackles on you, and you tell me about your past.

Angell: What do you mean?

Chief: We have a bail regulation here. Under this regulation, I have the authority to determine whether a Sinner can be released on bail by evaluating their cases and current condition.

Chief: As for the shackles, think of them as a form of security. I won't use them to control you or restrict your freedom.

Angell thinks for a moment, then nods.

Angell: Sure.

The crimson glow of the shackles fades, and Angell remains impassive. You pull back your hand.

Chief: Alright, let's start from the beginning.

Chief: How did you end up in this profession?

Angell: ...I was once just an ordinary Syndican.

Angell falls silent, then begins to share her past with you for the first time.

Angell: I was born into a humble family. We led a simple life in a small rented place. We weren't rich, but we didn't have to worry about food and clothing. Although we couldn't afford luxurious meals, my mother would often make sweet red bean soup.

Angell: One night, my parents were caught in a gang shootout on their way home. They were hit by stray bullets and died on the spot, covered in blood. I was still very young at that time.

Angell: With my parents gone and little savings, I couldn't afford the rent. I had no choice but to leave our home and look for ways to make a living.

Chief: So you joined the dark web to make a living?

Angell: Uh-huh.

Angell: In the early years, I had been everywhere in Syndicate and tried many different jobs. But it turned out I was only good at fighting, killing, and doing dirty work for other people.

Angell: For a long time, I was living hand to mouth, barely scraping by. And one day, I heard about the dark web and how people could make money there, so I registered an account and became a contractor.
