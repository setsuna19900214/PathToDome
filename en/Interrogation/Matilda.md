# Matilda Interrogation

## Matilda - Interrogation I

> You found out about Matilda's identity. However, with the hearing on the West District incident approaching, she is forced to make a choice. You decide to offer her some assistance.

You push open the door and walk into the reception room.

Upon hearing you enter, the man in front of you stands up, a big smile on his face. He extends his hand to you.

???: Thank you for taking the time to meet with me, Chief.

The man is wearing a crumpled trench coat, and his tie has come loose. He looks every bit the typical middle-aged man you'd see around Eastside.

He is courteous, but looks uneasy. Nevertheless, you shake his outstretched hand.

???: My name is Frank Miller; I'm a clerk from the City Council.

Frank: Ah, when I sent the email requesting a visit, I thought: The Chief of the MBCC was the most popular figure these days. My email would probably go unanswered, and I should count myself lucky if I received a rejection email.

Frank: I really lucked out. I can't believe that the Chief of the MBCC, who saved millions of lives in the West District, would agree to meet someone as insignificant as me. You do live up to the name "Heroic Chief."

The man's over-the-top tone and demeanor are making you feel a bit uncomfortable.

Chief: Thank you, Mr. Frank. That's what I should do.

You check the time on your terminal and decide to put a stop to his cringeworthy praises.

Chief: Tell me why you've come.

Frank: Sorry, sorry... What you and your Sinners have accomplished in the West District is really, really inspiring! Seeing you in person, I-I just got carried away...

The man adjusts his tie.

Frank: Long story short: thanks to you, the number of supporters of our policy has been increasing, and our work is going more smoothly now.

Chief: ...Whose policy? Whose work?

The man hears the doubt in your voice.

Frank: Ours...

Frank: The progressives in the City Council.

Frank: I'm here today to invite you to a hearing scheduled to take place one week from now...

You must have heard about the hearing. It's a public meeting scheduled by the City Council, in response to the recent events that have transpired in the West District. As soon as the news about the hearing was out, it attracted attention from all walks of life in Eastside.

Chief: So, you guys want me to... pick sides at this hearing regarding the Executive Committee's dereliction of duty, to declare support for the progressives...

Frank: No, no, we're not asking you to pick sides. Currently, the majority of the City Council members are, for lack of a better word, conservatives... We know we're no match for them; we wouldn't ask you to make an enemy out of them.

Frank: All you need to do is speak your mind and tell the truth when the time comes.

The man rubs his palms together, looking somewhat uneasy.

Frank: At this juncture, the liberation and welfare of the people of the West District is a sure thing.

Frank: We're not asking you to take our side. We're just hoping you'll stay on the side of the people of the West District. You're on their side, aren't you?

Frank: I heard that there's another MBCC member who played an active role in the recent events and fared brilliantly. The name is Matilda. If Miss Matilda could choose the side of the people of the West District as well, it would undoubtedly be a huge help!

After his passionate speech, the man gives you a meaningful smile.

Frank: Of course, you won't be fighting alone.

Frank: We've won over some of the media outlets that are eligible to attend the hearing, and they'll report the truth. We'll also help hold back pressure from the conservatives.

Frank: On top of that, we've had some funds ready, for investigation purposes. You can make use of them whenever you need.

The man produces a black card from his pocket and slides it over the tabletop.

Without saying a word, you slide the card back.

Surprise registers in the man's eyes. Finally, you speak.

Chief: I will attend the hearing.

Frank: I've heard so much about how noble the Chief of the MBCC is. Today, I've seen it with my own eyes.

Chief: As for Matilda...

You look the man in the eye.

Chief: I'm her superior, yes, but I still need to ask for her opinion.

Chief: She's a former member of the Executive Committee, after all. Whether she wants to choose sides at this point is her decision entirely.

Frank: Of course, of course.

The man can no longer keep joy from his face. He makes himself look slightly more presentable before extending his hand to you once again.

Frank: I have faith in Miss Matilda. She'll make the choice she believes to be right.

Matilda: So, you've given them your word?

Chief: I only promised to tell the truth.

Despite the fact that you haven't had much contact with Matilda since she arrived at the Bureau, her meticulous and efficient working style has left a strong impression on you.

Although she can come across as rigid and formal—a result of her strict adherence to rules, she's a very reliable adjutant professionally.

Chief: But I'm not going to speak for the benefit of the progressives, even though their offer is indeed tempting.

A large amount of money, political quid pro quo... You explain the progressives' offer to Matilda.

Throughout your explanation, the neatly dressed former Executive Committee adjutant listens without uttering a word.

When you're finished, she ponders for a moment before making her comments.

Matilda: If I may speak frankly, Chief, this invitation might not be what you think it is.

Matilda: It is... a trap.

**【Advance】**

- A trap?

**end**

Matilda: Yes, a trap.

Matilda: That the progressives in the City Council will make a move after the Executive Committee's debacle is to be expected. But, to put it bluntly, they don't have what it takes to pull off anything major.

Matilda: Political quid pro quo... well, that's possible, given that they hold 8% of the seats on the City Council.

Matilda: But the funds...

Matilda: I don't know the exact amount. But the card you just mentioned... if I'm not mistaken, it was issued by a private bank that has business dealings with many big figures in Eastside's political and business circles.

Matilda: They only provide one-on-one services, known for their ability to guarantee complete confidentiality for their clients. Whether your assets are gray or black, they can make them clean.

Chief: ...

Matilda: Unfortunately for the councilors from the progressive wing, their private assets might not be large enough to qualify them for cards from this bank.

Chief: Are you saying...

Matilda: Yes. I'm certain that this Frank is not a member of the progressive wing.

Matilda: These so-called progressives appeared on the scene several decades ago. But ten years ago, they were completely defeated, and they've never been on center stage since.

Matilda: Today, they exist on the periphery of the City Council, serving as a "necessary opposing voice." They owe their existence to the mercy of those in power.

Matilda: The Executive Committee's debacle represents an opportunity for them, one that they must seize. Unfortunately for them, this kind of game...

Matilda looks at you with a smile on her face.

Matilda: It's simply out of their league.

Chief: ...

Chief: Your understanding of the workings of the City Council far exceeds my expectations.

Matilda knows too much for an adjutant of a frontline Executive Officer.

Chief: If it weren't for you, I would still be kept in the dark.

Matilda: It's what I should do as your subordinate.

Matilda: You don't deal with them often. It's hard to know the truth without sufficient information.

Matilda: Add that to what this Frank did... The Speaker's faction has always been good at using these little tricks... This is why I'm so sure of my conclusion.

Chief: The Speaker's faction?

The conclusion, so casually made by Matilda, confuses you even more.

Chief: You're saying the Speaker, leader of the conservative wing of the City Council, wants me to truthfully present the situation in the West District, to make statements that are harmful to them?

Matilda picks up on your confusion and goes on to explain:

Matilda: Yes. Although it seems contradictory, the logic is not hard to grasp.

Matilda: Like I said earlier, this is a trap, but you're not the target, Chief.

Matilda: I am.

Matilda pauses for a moment. Looking you in the eye, she continues:

Matilda: Bernadette Cavendish... You must've heard of this name, I presume?

You have. You're not in the habit of seeking out news about the political scene, but you've heard this name mentioned here and there, through this channel or that.

The de facto leader of the Cavendish family, a star member of the City Council, a role model for the women in Eastside... and a popular candidate for the Speaker's seat in next year's election.

And then the full name of Matilda springs to your mind. You first came across the name while skimming through her file when she was transferred to the MBCC.

Chief: Matilda Cavendish...

Matilda: Yes, that's my full name. Councilor Bernadette, the current City Secretary...

Matilda: She is my aunt.

Things start to make sense now.

When the West District Reconstruction Committee was dissolved following its failure, many of its members faced dire consequences.

As a former member, Matilda was swiftly transferred to the MBCC, the safest place a Sinner can be.

And now, the consequences have come looking for her.

Chief: So, the Speaker's faction intends to use you against your aunt, right?

Matilda: Yes.

Matilda: The real threat to the Speaker has never been the progressives, but his overly active "subordinate" who has announced her candidacy.

Matilda: Few things pose a threat to Ms. Bernadette. Unfortunately, due to a combination of many uncontrollable factors, I seem to have become the only weak point that the Speaker can take advantage of.

Matilda looks up at you, her eyes filled with apology.

Matilda: Chief, I'm sorry for getting you involved...

**【Advance】**

- No need for apologies.

**end**

Chief: If you're worried about the possible adverse effect on Councilor Bernadette, I can make a joint statement with you at the hearing, cutting ties with all factions...

Before you can finish, Matilda shakes her head.

Matilda: It won't work.

Matilda: I'm a member of the Cavendish family. Once I stand behind the podium to say my piece, everything I have done, everything I have said... it will be used by the other conservatives to attack Ms. Bernadette.

Matilda: A relative of a conservative councilor openly expressing sympathy for the West District... This is likely to become the biggest stain on Ms. Bernadette's political career...

Matilda: I could claim that I did what I did in the West District because I was forced to. This would possibly minimize the damage to our family's interests, but...

Matilda: After all I have been through, I can't, in good conscience, bring myself to lie.

Matilda lowers her head. Her lips are moving, as if she has something more to say, but nothing comes out.

The fact that Matilda is in so much agony can only mean one thing...

She has decided to attend the hearing.

Chief: You're right. We've been through it all together...

**【Advance】**

- I have a suggestion.

**end**

Chief: I don't know much about politics, but I can play tricks too.

Chief: I can get you a medical report saying that you were critically injured during an operation in the West District and almost died.

Chief: Or I can tell them that you've been sent to a satellite town for an important task.

Chief: My point is, as long as you're not available, they can't use you to attack Councilor Bernadette...

Your attempt at humor works, at least for a moment. Matilda's furrowed brow relaxes slightly.

Matilda: Haha...

Matilda: Even if I disappeared from DisCity, it wouldn't really help the situation.

Matilda: The Speaker's faction has set their plan in motion. They're going to pull out all the stops to turn the spotlight on me, whether I attend the hearing or not.

Matilda: I won't let that stop me from speaking up for the West District. If not me, who else will?

You understand Matilda's choice.

You understand why she is choosing to speak up for the West District, even though she can't really change anything or weaken the conservatives' position in the City Council, and anything she says and does can be used against her family.

Since the very beginning, she has never considered not attending the hearing. She is anxious to convey everything she saw and learned in the West District to Eastside.

Her mind is made up. Matilda looks back at you with a determined look in her eyes.

Matilda: I won't run away. I must attend the hearing.

Matilda: Even if the Speaker's faction has considered everything, there's one thing they got wrong.

Matilda: I, the "pawn," am the only variable.

Chief: You have thought of a countermeasure?

Matilda: I wouldn't call it a countermeasure, but... I want to give it a try.

Matilda: Ms. Bernadette must've known about the Speaker's faction's move, but so far, the Cavendish family hasn't made any public announcement to sever ties with me.

Matilda: Which means that Ms. Bernadette believes that I'll make the right choice, even though it may not be a choice the family would want me to make. But I will never let my aunt and the rest of my family down.

Matilda: I'll do everything I can before the hearing to find a way to turn the situation around.

Matilda takes a deep breath. Her expression becomes even more determined.

Chief: All right... I understand.

Chief: By the way, is there anything I can help you with?

Matilda thinks for a moment, and then gives you a somewhat shy smile; she rarely smiles.

Matilda: If it's not too much to ask...

Matilda: Could I use your office for a while?

## Matilda - Interrogation II

> You had a long talk with Matilda. The influence of her aunt on Matilda is deeper than you had expected.

36 hours before the hearing, 4:29 A.M.

You've just wrapped up the day's work, ready to get some rest, but a source of bright light draws you to the second floor of the workspace.

The light comes from your office. You let Matilda use the office, where she has easy access to a large amount of documents and confidential information.

There haven't been many pressing matters that require your attention lately. To give Matilda space, you moved out of your office. Nevertheless, you're surprised to find Matilda up this late.

The door is ajar. You ease it open wider. Behind the mountain of files and disposable coffee cups on the table, you see the person hard at work.

The soft rustling of pen against paper is constant in the quiet, and she doesn't seem to notice your presence.

**【Advance】**

- Morning...

**end**

Matilda: Chief?

Matilda: You're still up?

Chief: Just got off work.

Chief: Are you still prepping for tomorrow's hearing?

Matilda: ...Yes.

Matilda sounds tired. Still, she's making an effort to keep lethargy out of her voice while answering you.

You glance at the corner of the table and notice the sheets of paper covered with color-coded notes. From something as small as a punctuation mark to something as large as a whole paragraph, nothing has escaped her meticulous pen. Collectively, they reflect the writer's thorough thought process.

However, it's obvious that this stack of paper is unwanted.

The writer holds herself to extremely high standards, and while that is true, it's the mental burden that plays a major role in her being so hard on herself.

Chief: Only a few pages of paper...

Matilda: Sorry, what did you say?

Your voice is low, and Matilda has a lapse of concentration and fails to catch what you've just said.

Chief: What I was trying to say is, we've been through a lot in the West District, but it can be summed up on just a few pieces of copy paper.

Chief: It's in fact a story worthy of the adjective "epic."

You remove a piece of paper from the unwanted stack and give it a quick scan.

Chief: A lot of numbers in here, a lot of zeros. Like how much drinking water the West District needs every day, or how many emergency supplies, or how much of this or that is needed to maintain the stability of the entire West District.

Chief: You must've put a lot of thought into deciding on what units to use; you're trying to explain the problems faced by the West District without embarrassing the people involved too much.

Chief: Unfortunately, numbers are cold. They arise from the collision of different interests; they are conditions added as an afterthought when the big shots are trying to reach an agreement.

Chief: Even the experiences we had together in the West District, the emotions these experiences evoked... They can tell a more touching tale than numbers ever will.

Matilda listens to your long speech. When you're finished, she puts down the pen she's holding.

Matilda: How I wish I could tell stories to them.

Matilda rises to her feet, reaches out reflexively for a paper cup, but catches herself.

Matilda: At this hour... I don't think it's a good idea to offer you coffee.

Chief: It's okay. Take a break. Let's have a chat.

Chief: Speaking of stories, I know nothing about your story before you joined the Executive Committee. Care to share it?

Matilda looks you in the eye, and sighs as if with resignation.

Matilda: Are you always this blunt when you "chat" with others?

Chief: Haha...

Matilda looks away from your smiling face and toward the window.

Matilda: My story is not a complicated one... My childhood was not that different from that of most Eastside children. If there's any difference, it's probably that my father's last name is Cavendish.

Matilda: I grew up under the protection of my parents. Until the incident in N.F. 104 changed everything...

Chief: N.F. 104?

Matilda: Yes.

Matilda: The political scene of Eastside was thrown into turmoil that year. That came about when Abel, a candidate for the Speaker's seat supported by the Cavendish family, was assassinated and died during a campaign speech.

Matilda: The Cavendish family poured all its resources into this attempt at ascending to the peak of power in the City Council, but almost ended up the way the Executive Committee did, narrowly avoiding the fate of being divided up by the family's political enemies.

Matilda: My parents lost their lives in the turmoil...

Chief: ...It must have been a very difficult time.

Matilda: For the family, yes.

Matilda: But relatively speaking, I was lucky...

Matilda: After my parents passed away, my aunt, Ms. Bernadette, adopted me.

**【Advance】**

- Were you two close?

**end**

Chief: Your guardian is the star councilor in the City Council, the leader of a political family... She couldn't have had much time for her small "family," right?

Matilda gives a soft laugh, her eyes sparkling.

Matilda: You're right. She didn't have much time to care for me. But that doesn't mean she failed as a guardian.

Matilda: She sent me to the best elite school in all of Eastside, hired the best teachers to give me private lessons on etiquette and teach me all the other skills useful in social situations...

Matilda: We didn't spend much time together, but whenever she was with me, she'd always let me know that she knew about my latest achievements, and she'd give me suggestions for what I should do next.

Matilda: I made many mistakes, but she never made me feel bad about them.

Matilda: She allowed my mistakes, my immaturity, my shortsightedness.

Matilda: At the same time, she made a point of telling me, "The most important thing is to find your true self when your mistakes lead you astray and locate the path that suits you best."

Chief: Find your true self, huh? Sounds like your aunt was a major influence in your life.

Chief: Sadly, your aunt's true self seems to have led her to a completely different path from yours.

Matilda's bright eyes dull somewhat at your remark.

She closes her eyes, as if reminiscing, or contemplating the true meaning of the term "true self."

After a long while, she opens her eyes and, slowly, she speaks:

Matilda: I didn't understand what true self really meant at first.

Matilda: I was just an ordinary person, with no outstanding talents. I couldn't hold a candle to those real geniuses at the elite school.

Matilda: From the textbooks, the teachers at the school, the propaganda from Eastside... I learned the rules of society.

Matilda: I thought that if I followed these rules and worked really hard, I could at least narrow the gap between Ms. Bernadette and me.

Matilda: It's like target practice... Do you know anything about target practice, Chief?

- Not really.

**end**

Matilda: The concept of target practice is simple. All you need to do is hit the bull's-eye, or get as close to it as you can.

Matilda: For the longest time, I stayed in the shooting range that was Eastside and shot targets other people put up for me...

Matilda: Ms. Bernadette, taught me all the techniques and rules, and even the "gun" in my hand was given by her.

Matilda: I used to think that I didn't need to think too much, that all I needed to do was shoot the targets, but now...

Matilda: I don't know when, but my target has changed, and the person who has been standing behind me and supporting me is now blocking my view of the bull's-eye I want so much to hit.

Chief: And you can't bring yourself to pull the trigger this time around?

Matilda: I wouldn't dare.

Matilda: Forgive me, I'm just too immature.

Chief: I don't think this is a matter of maturity.

Chief: You're just worried, about how that relative who means so much to you will treat you after your bullet hits the bull's-eye.

Chief: You're worried that there will no be turning back once you pull the trigger, right?

Matilda is silent for a long while, then nods with resignation.

Chief: But isn't your true self telling you to aim for this particular bull's-eye?

Chief: "The most important thing is to find your true self when your mistakes lead you astray and locate the path that suits you best."

Doubt is leaving Matilda's eyes.

Matilda: ...

Matilda: You're right, Chief. I...

Matilda: I get it now. Instead of being timid, I should just pull the trigger first.

Chief: Not my words. That's what Ms. Bernadette taught you, isn't it?

Matilda: You're right.

Chief: You have a great aunt.

Chief: Sensible, wise, patient with her descendants, and willing to guide.

Chief: Among the many Sinners in the MBCC, very few can claim to have a relative half as good as yours.

Matilda: ...

Chief: Maybe she's just been deceived by the one-sided voices in Eastside?

Chief: Tomorrow, at the hearing, when you go up there to speak the truth about the West District and awe everyone present, I believe she'll understand why you do what you do.

Matilda: ...

Chief: The people of the West District have changed you. I can't see why they can't change more people. Have faith in Ms. Bernadette.

Chief: If you win over a powerful ally like your aunt, the future of the West District will be even more secure.

Matilda looks out of the window.

Matilda: Perhaps...

Sensing Matilda's reluctance to talk more about her aunt, you drop the subject.

Chief: In any case, don't stress yourself out. Just go do what you want to do and what you should do. I'll support you in the background.

Matilda turns her gaze toward you. The smile she gives you this time is filled with warmth and determination.

Matilda: Mm... I know.

She thinks for a moment, then speaks again:

Matilda: Thank you for taking the time to chat with me about these things.

Matilda: I won't let you guys down.

Chief: You're welcome...

You yawn, feeling sleepy.

Chief: Go to bed as soon as you finish revising the draft. You don't want to speak at the hearing with dark circles under your eyes.

Matilda smiles and nods.

Matilda: Mm, you should get some rest too.

## Matilda - Interrogation III

> After the hearing, the situation in Eastside changed, and Matilda embarked on her own path.

Black Screen: █/█, 7:00 P.M., some venue in Eastside

Frank: You came.

Frank: And Miss Matilda is here too. It's been days; the heroes of the West District look as dazzling as ever.

You see a familiar face the moment you enter the venue.

The man is neatly dressed and full of vitality today, a world away from how he looked a week ago.

Chief: You clean up nice.

Frank: Well, it's a formal occasion after all. I thought I should dress up a bit.

Just then, a middle-aged man enters the venue. When Frank sees the man, he smiles and excuses himself.

Frank: Sorry, I've got some business to attend to. Excuse me.

Frank walks up to the man in long strides and whispers in his ear. As he does so, you notice them looking your way a few times, both wearing a self-pleased expression.

After their brief exchange, the middle-aged man walks over to the front row.

Chief: Do you know that man?

Matilda: Reagan, the current Deputy Speaker, valued by the Speaker for his fund-raising ability.

Chief: Heh, looks like they've decided to drop the act.

Matilda: Well, they have achieved their goal.

More people flood into the venue. You notice a man giving a nod of acknowledgment to Matilda.

Chief: Who is that?

Matilda: Kadis Cavendish, my distant cousin, currently Ms. Bernadette's right-hand man.

Chief: Some cousin. Didn't even come over and say hi...

Chief: So, is he the representative of the Cavendish family at this hearing?

Matilda: Yes.

Chief: Ms. Bernadette is not coming?

Matilda: ...

As more and more people flood into the venue, some murmurs reach your ears.

Noise: Hey, did you see that? Bernadette is a no-show. Is she admitting defeat to the Speaker?

Noise: What else could it be? Obviously, they're making that poor girl a scapegoat.

Noise: That's a shame. Looks like the old man's re-election is a done deal. I was hoping she would be elected and reshuffle the City Council.

Noise: Look on the bright side. At least this way, the Cavendish family is bound to take a hit. If Councilor Bernadette wants to save her own skin, she'll have to do more than let a girl take the blame.

Noise: You're right. Gotta make her cough up what she's swallowed over the past two years.

Noise: Heh, it's only been a few years since the Abel incident, and Bernadette still has her sights on the Speaker's position... I don't think the Cavendish family will be able to come back from this ever again.

Gradually, you notice, all eyes in the room are being trained on Matilda.

Feigned sympathy, ill-concealed hatred, and more often than not, pure schadenfreude... Some of them even begin to look at Matilda as if evaluating a commodity.

You have no doubt that the former Executive Committee adjutant sitting next to you can also sense those unpleasant stares.

**【Advance】**

- Encourage Matilda

**end**

You place your hand on Matilda's shoulder.

The girl turns to you, and the smile she gives you is pure and genuine.

You realize that she's been ready all along.

As an elderly judge walks up to take his place behind the podium, the gazes on Matilda are slowly drawn away. A silence quickly falls over the venue, punctuated only by the clicking of camera flashes and the occasional cough.

You learned about the identity of the judge beforehand from the intelligence department. He is the Speaker's elder brother, who was swiftly promoted to his current position after his predecessor died in an "accident."

Chief: Heh, in the end, neither the Speaker nor Ms. Bernadette showed up. But it seems that the Speaker's side is well-prepared.

Matilda: ...

The judge cuts short your mockery of him. He clears his throat and turns on the microphone.

Judge: Ahem, the purpose of this hearing is to clarify the events that have occurred in the West District over the past year and discuss the City Council's future plans and actions regarding the region.

Judge: Given that the Executive Committee has failed to achieve the expected goals in its reconstruction work in the West District, it is necessary and obligatory for all government agencies in Eastside to reflect on the overall policy concerning the West District.

The judge's eyes sweep around the venue, and only after confirming that the attention of all the attendees is on him, does he continue:

Judge: I declare, the hearing has now begun.

The judge has hardly put down the microphone when a young politician at the front stands up to speak.

The show begins.

Young official: Greetings, Judge. I'm Mensa, an official of the city's Treasury Department.

Young official: As we all know, the Treasury Department has been under tremendous pressure over the past year to support the City Council's series of measures in the West District.

Young official: Normally, I'd cite data and reports, but I've decided to forego that step, since this hearing is about problem-solving.

Young official: On behalf of my colleagues and the four million taxpayers in Eastside, I would like to ask the City Council the following questions:

The young councilor looks around the room and continues:

Young official: First question: The City Council claims that the Executive Committee was established to replace the ineffective Public Security Bureau. Specifically, it was established following the Keylan Square incident to bring the West District, commonly known as Syndicate, back on track.

Young official: But... In just one short year, billions have gone into the restoration of order in the West District, without anything to show for it. If anything, the entire region seems to have grown more chaotic.

Young official: May I ask what explanation the City Council, the most vigorous facilitator of the establishment of the Executive Committee, would give to the people of DisCity?

This standard line of questioning directed at the Speaker-led City Council, assertive and forceful, can certainly "represent" the voice of the majority of people who care about the future of DisCity.

However, you know that it's just a carefully orchestrated show.

Sure enough, the spokesman for the Speaker, Reagan, makes no move at all. The city official sitting next to him adjusts his glasses and stands up.

City Official: First of all, I would like to make one thing clear to you. The West District is a lawless zone that has long been controlled by illegal violent groups.

City Official: This has always been how the City Council characterizes the West District. It was in the past, it is now, and it seems that it will be for a long time to come.

City Official: The West District was in chaos before the City Council green-lighted the establishment of the Executive Committee. And the causes of the chaos were the incompetence of the local security precinct in the region and the ineffectiveness of the West District Order Management Regulations!

City Official: To deal with the criminals and rioters, we had to use unconventional means...

Young official: Sorry to interrupt.

Young official: Are you saying that the Executive Committee did nothing wrong, and that they should've stepped up their effort by bringing in more chariots and machine guns to... clean up the West District?

The young councilor frowns, seemingly reluctant to utter the words "clean up."

City Official: Your description of the Executive Committee's method is not accurate, but the basic idea is right.

City Official: In the early stages of their execution of the plan, the Executive Committee achieved great results. They eradicated gangs and other illegal organizations, and successfully restored some order to the West District.

City Official: And then serious dereliction of duty occurred within the Executive Committee.

City Official: Some Executive Committee officials lapped up the rhetoric of the so-called "progressives" and attempted to obstruct law enforcement. They advocated establishing "channels of communication" and solving problems in a "peaceful manner" by working with a group of rioters, gangsters, and ruffians.

City Official: That was undoubtedly a betrayal.

City Official: It was these despicable traitors who jeopardized the interests of Eastside; they betrayed the Executive Committee's internal information to a West District organization called Rustfire, which eventually led to the failure of the Executive Committee's operations in the West District.

The remark causes uproar in the crowd.

Some of them may be hearing it for the first time, and their surprise is real, but the vast majority are just acting surprised.

Young official: Sir, that's a very serious allegation you just made there. Is it the official opinion of the City Council?

City Official: Yes, it is.

City Official: What I just said is not unfounded, nor is it an attempt to shift the blame. It is what actually happened in the West District.
