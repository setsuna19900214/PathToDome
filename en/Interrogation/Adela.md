# Adela Interrogation

## Adela - Interrogation I

> What you didn't expect was that Adela, the culprit in the events of Sloane Street and many more, had even erased her own memories.
> You need to understand her motives, her starting point. To that end, you initiated a program of investigation.

█Number: FAC-M-136-I

█File: Sinner's Arrest Report

A Sinner was arrested by the MBCC Arrest Unit in the latest locale where the myth of Mad Shears appeared. The arrestee was barely conscious at the time of arrest and is presumed to be Adela, an S-Class Sinner at large.

Adela is a suspect in a series of attacks on Sloane Street. Although no video evidence is available, it's believed that she's capable of mental interference. She's extremely dangerous and must be detained immediately.

*Annex—Performance in Serving Term (PERF):

The Sinner has been detained. An interrogation by the MBCC chief is urgently needed. Furthermore, MBCC supervision of the Sinner should be strengthened to keep her dangerous behavior in check.

You walk into the interrogation room. Seated at the table is Adela the Sinner. She looks up at you with a smile on her face.

She looks harmless, with her gentle smile and slight build. Appearances can be deceptive, however. You know how dangerous she is.

**【Advance】**

- Let's get started, shall we?

**end**

You put the PERF report and evidence bag on the table and sit down across from Adela. You notice that she's looking at your face with interest.

**【Advance】**

- What are you looking at?

**end**

Adela: Oh, nothing. It's just that you look familiar.

Adela: I have a feeling that I've seen you before, but I can't place you. Have we met before?

**【Choose One】**

- On Sloane Street, perhaps?

**end**

You don't remember meeting Adela.

Chief: I guess this is our first meeting, Miss Mad Shears.

Adela: I was mistaken, then. Sorry!

You don't remember meeting Adela, but...

Chief: I'm not sure. Maybe we met on Sloane Street, where I was attacked by a mysterious being and lost the memories of that day. Have you been to Sloane Street, Miss Mad Shears?

Adela: Sloane Street? Yet another place I've never heard of.

Adela remains calm, unruffled by your accusatory tone. But she didn't contradict you when you called her Miss Mad Shears.

You're dealing with a seemingly cool-headed individual with a streak of madness.

Adela: You must have brought me here for a reason. Why don't you tell me how I can be of help, Chief?

You have so many questions about the Fog Alley attacks and Mad Shears herself.

None of the Fog Alley attacks was captured on film, and the victims and other people who had been on the scene, affected by the Sinner's power, couldn't remember what he or she looked like. The only thing the Arrest Unit had to go by was the reported sightings of Mad Shears. Despite the lack of information, they were able to apprehend Adela, who was losing control of her power at the time of arrest.

But you have a more urgent matter to deal with at the moment.

**【Advance】**

- Open the PERF report

**end**

Chief: In a matter of days, S-Class Sinner Adela attacked eleven people, including MBCC staffers and Sinners. Ten of them have suffered mental impairment to various degrees.

You glance at Adela out of the corner of your eye. She's still wearing that gentle smile of hers. You have a hard time reconciling the harmless-looking woman in front of you with Mad Shears in the urban myth, or with the dangerous Sinner in the PERF report.

Adela: You don't look well. They're important to you, aren't they? And you're worried about their well-being?

Adela: You don't have to worry. They're not in any danger. They'll sleep it off, and when they wake up, they'll be as good as new.

**【Choose One】**

- Their well-being is none of your concern.

- I'm not here to talk about them.

**end**

Chief: Here in the MBCC, everyone understands what they've signed up for. I've done everything in my power to protect them against Mania, and they're forever ready to face it when it's necessary.

Chief: What I want to know is why you attacked them.

Chief: I know you didn't do it to break out.

Many of the detained Sinners have tried to break out of the MBCC, but Adela hasn't been one of them. She didn't run away after her attacks. Instead, she sent the victims to the lounge area and stayed on the premises.

Chief: Why did you do it then?

Adela: Why? I was just trying to help them.

Adela: Their hair was too long. They carried it everywhere they went. It weighed them down.

Adela: It upset me to see them like that. So I cut the troublesome hair off to make them feel more comfortable. I didn't mean to cause any trouble. I'm sorry about that.

Adela sighs. She sounds sincere, even though her explanation is absurd.

**【Advance】**

- Hair?

**end**

Adela: Oh sorry, I forgot to explain it to you.

She brushes a stray lock of hair from her eyes, her smile never fading.

Adela: Your hair grows rapidly if you carry a lot of worries in your mind. The more worries you carry, the longer the hair.

Adela: I was just trying to help them get rid of their worries.

Chief: I have no idea what you're talking about.

Adela: Maybe only those who hold the shears can see the rapidly growing hair, but...

Her gaze is still on you, but her smile is gone.

Adela: I did make some mistakes in the past and got some people in trouble. But I won't make the same mistakes again.

Adela: I want to trim away other people's worries. It's always been my responsibility, and mine alone.

You don't fully understand what she's saying, but you've taken notice of a useful piece of information in her gibberish.

Chief: You said you want to trim away other people's worries?

Chief: Was that why you attacked those Sloane Street residents trapped in thick fog? To trim away their worries?

**【Advance】**

- Produce the report on the Sloane Street attacks

**end**

Chief: It's discovered that all the Sloane Street victims suffered from memory loss, as have the MBCC staffers who you attacked.

Chief: Are you still claiming you're innocent?

You look into her eyes, trying to see if they betray anything.

Adela: I have no idea what you're talking about. I don't remember going to Sloane Street. I don't know anything about that place.

You've interrogated many Sinners. You know a liar when you see one. The eyes of the woman in front of you betray nothing; she seems very genuine.

Maybe she is a highly accomplished liar? Or maybe she's successfully trained herself to remain calm in whatever situation?

You can't decide which. You need more clues.

You have Adela's suitcase brought into the interrogation room. It's an important piece of physical evidence collected at the time of her arrest.

The lock on this exquisite suitcase is intact. When Adela notices how it's not been forced open, she gives you a compliment, still wearing her trademark smile.

<ins>Adela: You're quite the gentleman.</ins><br><ins>Adela: You're quite the lady.</ins>

Chief: I don't deserve such a compliment. I didn't open it, not because I didn't want to, but because I didn't get around to it.

Chief: Mind if I open it, Miss Adela?

Adela: Do I get to say no?

Still smiling, Adela gives a small wave of her hand.

Adela: Be my guest.

When you open it, you're greeted by a faint scent of lavender. Everything inside is neatly organized: clothes, toiletries, papers. The owner must be a very organized lady, you think to yourself.

Adela: As you can see, nothing out of the ordinary...

The papers catch your attention. They might bear some useful information.

**【Advance】**

- Take the papers out of the suitcase

**end**

A business license for a hair salon in Ashby Village, receipts for furniture purchased in the Central Business District, a lease on an apartment on Sloane Street...

These papers—some old, some new, have been tucked away in one of the compartments.

Chief: Do you have anything to say about this?

Adela: You mean the papers? I have this old habit of saving receipts and stuff. They might come in handy. That's what I thought anyway.

Adela: Now I realize I might be wrong. They take up space in my suitcase, and I haven't found a use for them yet. I've never got around to throwing them away. Now I have a big pile of them.

**【Advance】**

- I'm not asking about your habit.

**end**

Chief: Ashby Village, the Central Business District, Sloane Street, which I mentioned just now...

Chief: Do you know what these places have in common? The answer is the myth of Mad Shears.

Adela: Really? I didn't know that.

Adela: None of the names you just mentioned rings a bell.

Chief: Don't play me for a fool. Your power is unique. No other Sinner in all of DisCity can do what you do.

Adela looks unperturbed. You've met your share of accomplished liars in the past, and you've always been able to spot a crack or two in their veneer. The same can't be said of the woman in front of you.

Or maybe she is telling the truth. If that's the case, then the only possible explanation...

**【Advance】**

- Did you erase your own memories?

**end**

Adela: We all have troublesome memories. I'm no exception.

Adela: Maybe I did go to those places. Maybe I did erase those people's troublesome memories, and mine as well. Either way, I have no recollection of any of it.

Adela remains unperturbed—disturbingly so, for some reason.

Chief: It makes no sense. Why would you do that to yourself?

A faint smile crosses her lips. And then she starts explaining.

Adela: Whenever I get the chance, I listen to my beloved customers' troubles and remove those troubles for them afterward, so that they can carry on with their lives worry-free. That's my calling.

Adela: After I free them of their unpleasant memories, including the memories of me, I do the same to myself.

**【Advance】**

- What do you gain from all this?

**end**

Adela closes her eyes. She opens them moments later and answers your question.

Adela: Inner peace.

Adela: I gain inner peace. I'm free of worries.

Chief: It's ridiculous.

Chief: Are you telling me that every time you experience something unpleasant, your solution, your only solution, is to erase the memory connected with that experience?

You're getting worked up. Adela doesn't react strongly. She leans forward ever slightly, a concerned look on her face.

Adela: You look upset. Did I cause it?

Adela: Do you need my help?

Her words of concern send a chill down your spine. The fire of anger that was burning inside you just a moment ago has been instantly doused.

You blurt out your refusal.

**【Advance】**

- No, thank you.

**end**

Chief: I don't need your *help*. I don't want to have my "unpleasant" memories erased.

Adela: Are you sure?

She smiles, somewhat ruefully.

Adela: What a shame.

The brief exchange gets your adrenalin pumping. You can feel the shackles vibrating inside you.

Fortunately, Adela doesn't make further moves to escalate the situation. Now there's a brief silence.

You take a moment to calm yourself down. You now realize that your line of questioning is getting you nowhere. How do you expect to extract any useful information from someone who erases their own memories?

**【Advance】**

- Go through the contents of the suitcase for other clues

**end**

Chief: ...

As you go through the contents of the suitcase, Adela looks at you with a ghost of a smile on her face. Both of you remain silent.

You find a bunch of keys in a hidden compartment.

The keys are covered with rust. There's a tag attached to the key ring. On it are a few vaguely identifiable words.

*12 Dorset Street, Apt. E*.

Chief: Do you remember this place?

You've asked the question in a somewhat offhand manner, only expecting "I don't remember" or "It doesn't ring a bell" as an answer from her.

Your interest is piqued when Adela doesn't answer you right away. You look up and see her expression has changed slightly.

But it only lasts a second. And then she delivers what's become something of a catchphrase for her.

Adela: Doesn't ring a bell. Sorry.

Why did she hesitate? Why does she keep the keys? The address must hold great significance for her, you think as you run your finger over the piece of paper, which has grown soft with age.

Chief: These are the only keys in your suitcase. They're rusty, but you didn't get rid of them. They must mean something to you.

Chief: Dorset Street is where the myth of Mad Shears originated. Maybe that's where you lived before you became Mad Shears.

Adela: What does it matter where I lived before I became Mad Shears?

Chief: I don't know yet. It's worth checking out, don't you think? As part of the investigation.

Chief: I hope you can accompany me on this trip. Your participation is essential.

Adela shakes her head, her smile now tinged with resignation.

## Adela - Interrogation II

> Witnessing Adela's past, the truth has surfaced. But things are far from over.

It's night. You and Adela are in a neighborhood crisscrossed by poorly-lit streets, searching for 12 Dorset Street.

Adela: Your plan is to visit an apartment that's probably laid vacant for more than ten years?

You nod.

Adela: Why did you take me on this trip, instead of one of your people? I'm Mad Shears. People speak about me with dread.

Adela: There won't be any witnesses here if you're attacked.

Chief: Gathering evidence is only one part of this investigation, and always has been. What I really want to know is what happened in your past that turned you into who you're today.

Chief: That's why I took you with me. It's a calculated risk.

Chief: Besides, Mad Shears doesn't attack people for no good reason. She only attacks those who have too many unpleasant memories, right? So I guess I'm not in mortal danger.

Adela smiles at your reply. The two of you continue to search for 12 Dorset Street in the dimness, and soon arrive at the destination.

In front of you is a dilapidated apartment building.

Chief: Here we are. Do you remember this place now?

You receive no response. You feel as if you were talking to yourself.

You come into the dark hallway. You click on your flashlight and shine it on the letters on the doors. Apt. E is at the end of the hallway.

You take out the keys found in the suitcase and insert them one by one into the lock. The lock is rusty, and so are the keys. You give each key a good wiggle before moving on to the next one.

Finally, by trial and error, you unlock the door and push it open.

Chief: Cough... Cough...

You're assaulted by a cloud of dust, so thick that you have to close your eyes for a second, so thick that the beam of your flashlight hardly penetrates it. And there's a musty smell lingering in the air.

Clack!

The bulb overhead buzzes into life. Dim light fills the room. You find your bearings and look to Adela. She is dusting her gloved hand with a handkerchief.

Chief: The electricity is still on. Who's been paying the utility bills?

Adela: Nobody. It's just a trick I used to save money, as many ordinary folks in Eastside do. I'm a bit surprised that the circuit is still working, though. We're in luck.

Adela leaves the room and returns shortly with some cleaning tools. She must have found them in the common area you just walked past.

Adela: Let me clear a space. It might take a while. Just bear with me.

Adela sets to work, with surprising efficiency. She takes out bags of trash, which end up next to the trash can downstairs. Soon, there isn't a speck of dust anywhere in the room.

It has taken her half an hour, and the room that sat neglected for years has been transformed. She has even put an antique-looking table and chairs, old as they might be, in the center of the room.

On the table is a faded tea set and a kettle of hot boiled water.

Adela: This is the best I could manage. Wish I had brought some loose tea leaves. We'll have to make do with only water; I don't want to bother the neighbors at this hour. Hope you don't mind.

Chief: I didn't know you had to be good at housekeeping to be a hairdresser.

Adela: Not every hairdresser.

Adela smiles.

Adela: It's usually just me in the shop. No employee. So I have to do everything myself.

Adela opens the window to let in the cool night air. It mitigates the musty smell in the room.

Chief: I feel like a guest invited here for tea or whatever beverage is on offer, and you're the thoughtful host.

Adela: (Chuckles) It's nice of you to say that. But I know what you're implying.

Adela gives a smile that doesn't reach the eyes.

Adela: I just did something I'm good at, to make it a bit more comfortable for you. I have no memory of any of the objects here. This is just like any old apartment to me.

Chief: Don't worry. I'm gonna help you remember. It's an essential part of my plan, after all.

Adela: Well, how are you gonna accomplish that?

Adela regards you with interest, her eyes twinkling with amusement and anticipation.

Chief: You can see other people's worries when you're holding your shears. That's your power, right?

Chief: My instinct tells me that you must have had many memories connected to this place. You erased them, because they made you sad. I want to see them, but I'll need your help to do that.

Adela: ...

Adela is reluctant. She looks slightly uneasy.

Chief: Didn't you say that ridding people of their worries is your calling? Well, it's my worry, not knowing what happened in this room so many years ago.

Adela: It's just... I just don't think you'd see anything pleasant.

Chief: I don't mind. I'm used to seeing unpleasant things.

Adela sighs and gives in.

Adela: All right then, if that's what you want.

Adela holds out her hand. A pair of shears, black with long blades, materialize in a pool of dim blue light.

**【Advance】**

- Grab the blades

**end**

You grab the handle of the blades, and in the process, feel Adela's skin. It's cold to the touch.

In the blink of an eye, the room changes.

Hair is everywhere, twisting and bending as if it has a life of its own. It's in every corner, it's floating, it's all you can see.

You don't scare easily. You've seen many horrific things in your life. Still, the sight before you sends your heart palpitating.

Chief: This is what you see?

Adela responds with a mirthless smile.

You take a moment to collect yourself. And then you summon up the courage to reach out your hand.

**【Advance】**

- Touch the hair

**end**

You see a girl. She's sleeping. Someone is pounding at the door. She wakes up with a start.

She gets out of bed and switches on the light. It's three in the morning, according to the clock on the wall. When she opens the door, a woman collapses into her arms.

The girl glances at the hook that's used as a key holder. No keys are there.

Girl: Did you lose the keys again?

The girl half drags, half carries the woman to the bathroom, where, upon entering, the woman sinks down by the toilet and vomits into the bowl.

The vile stench of vomit is overwhelming. It stinks out the apartment that the girl took great care to spruce up during the day.

Girl: Mom, you must stop drinking. Or you'll ruin your body.

Mother: What did you say?

The woman says in a cold, shrill voice.

Mother: Don't you dare lecture me. You'd better keep your mouth shut.

Mother: I'm your mother. You literally owe me your life.

Mother: If it weren't for you, I'd still be with the ballet company, and I wouldn't have to degrade myself just to keep food on the table.

Mother: If I don't do this job, you'll starve, you ungrateful brat. So don't you dare lecture me!

The girl has tuned her shrieking mother out by this point. She's thinking about her next-door neighbor.

This irritable overweight neighbor is bound to come knocking on their door tomorrow, demanding an explanation for the racket coming from their apartment at three in the morning.

For some reason, the girl is amused by the thought.

Mother: What's so funny? You don't approve of what I do, is that it?

Mother: Answer me. Tell me what you're unhappy about.

The woman keeps up her ranting and raving. The girl doesn't talk back. She looks up and gives her mother a gentle smile.

Girl: Sorry, Mom. It's my fault. You must be tired. Let me get you to bed...

It's a heart-wrenching memory, which inspires in you a sense of vicarious pain.

Adela looks at you, as if curious as to how you're reacting to what you've seen. You meet her gaze.

Chief: Can you recall anything about the girl?

Adela shakes her head.

Adela: I have accessed a great number of unhappy memories. Nothing special about this one. It could belong to anyone in Eastside, or even in DisCity.

Chief: It's all right. You have quite a collection of unhappy memories here. Let's check out some more.

Chief: Maybe some of them will help you remember.

**【Advance】**

- Touch the hair

**end**

The sun is about to set. The woman is still sleeping.

The girl is making oatmeal at the stove. She adds sugar to the oatmeal and brings the dish to the table. The aroma is delightful.

A while later, her mother shuffles out of the bedroom and slumps into a chair at the table.

After two servings of oatmeal, some color returns to her mother's face.

Mother: Hmm...

The girl clears the table, brings out her mother's mirror and cosmetics, and put them on the table. They can't afford a dressing table, so the dinner table doubles as one. Her mother starts putting on make-up, getting ready for work.

Mother: It's in moments like this that I feel it wasn't such a big mistake to have kept you.

The girl doesn't say anything in response. She goes over to the sink, turns on the faucet, and starts doing the dishes.

Mother: Anyway...

The woman seems to be in a good mood. She's not in the habit of initiating conversations.

Mother: Have you given thought to your future career?

Girl: Not really.

The girl doesn't want to discuss what kind of career she wants with her mother. She doesn't see the point.

Mother: What do you mean *not really*? You can't sit around on your ass indefinitely. You have to contribute.

Mother: You have my looks. You can be a movie star or a model. Don't waste the good looks.

Taking to her daughter doesn't seem to distract the woman from the task at hand.

Girl: Hairdresser.

The girl gives a random answer, partly out of a desire to shut her mother up about the subject.

Mother: What did you say?

Girl: Hairdresser, I want to become a hairdresser.

The girl repeats her answer, with more determination in her voice.

Mother: Hairdresser? Why? Hairdressing is not a lucrative job.

Girl: I want to live a happy life. And you don't need to earn a lot of money to live a happy life. I'll be able to make my customers happy as a hairdresser, and if they're happy, I'll be happy too.

Mother: Instead of daydreaming about how to make strangers happy, why don't you spend some time thinking about how to make your mother happy? And your mother is unhappy, because she has no money.

Girl: ...

The girl has nothing more to say to her mother. She knew the conversation wouldn't end well.

Mother: You're useless.

Having done her makeup, she rises to her feet and leaves for work.

The girl listens as her mother's footsteps recede and eventually disappear. She smooths down her hair, which hasn't been trimmed for some time and has grown somewhat unruly, picks up a curl of hair between her index and middle fingers of one hand, and mimes clipping it with the other.

Girl: Snip, snip, snip...

Girl: Worries, colorless memories... Make them all disappear.

Adela's expression doesn't change, but you can feel her hand trembling slightly through the shears.

You know she's not as composed as she makes herself appear to be. You press on, accessing another of her memories.

**【Advance】**

- Touch the hair

**end**

The girl's mother looks different. Radiant even. She has even taken out the brand shoulder bag which she saves for special occasions.

Girl: Are you not going to work?

Mother: I took the day off.

Mother: I have a date, with William.

The girl has heard her mother talk about the man more and more often over the past two months. And whenever she talks about him, she goes all smitten.

The girl has met William a couple of times. He's not as likable as her mother is making him out to be.

He's a patron of the place where her mother works. They've grown closer over time. Now they spend time together even on her days off.

The girl looks away from her mother's happy face, not interested in hearing about her mother's love life, and busies herself tidying up the room.

Mother: You don't want to know where we're gonna meet?

The girl ignores the question. Her mother volunteers the answer.

Mother: We're going to the Belphegor.

Girl: Oh...

It *is* an unusual rendezvous for her mother.

Not discouraged by her daughter's lack of enthusiasm, the woman continues to talk about her beloved.

Mother: I still feel like I'm in a dream.

Mother: William proposed to me yesterday. It still feels so surreal.

Girl: ...

The girl's heart lurches at the news. A question comes out unbidden.

Girl: Did you turn him down?

Mother: What?! Why would I want to do that?

Mother: If I had turned him down, where would I find another man as handsome, considerate, and rich?

Mother: And he doesn't think of you as a liability. He's even promised to send you to one of those fancy boarding schools after we get married. You should be happy to have him as a stepfather.

Girl: ...

The girl looks up, her neck stiff, her face expressionless.

Girl: I'm not going to any boarding school, fancy or not.

Her mother pauses for a second. And when she speaks again, her voice is piercing as usual.

Mother: Where else would you go?

Girl: You go live your happy life. I can take care of myself.

Mother: You're talking nonsense. You're my daughter. It's my responsibility to take care of you. I can't just...

The woman trails off, her eyes on her daughter's face.

Girl: You're the one being taken care of.

Smack!

The girl's cheek is burning with pain.

Mother: You want me to choose between you and him? You don't like him, so you're pushing him out of my life. Is that it?

Mother: Haven't I sacrificed enough for you? Do you want to ruin the rest of my life too, you ungrateful brat?

Her mother's shrill voice gives the girl a headache. Still, the girl responds with a soft voice, like she's talking to a baby.

Girl: You don't have to make a choice, Mom. Go to him.

Mother: What?

Looking at her *understanding* daughter, the woman is lost for words for a moment.

Anger fills her chest. The words that next come out of her mouth cut like a knife.

Mother: Get out! Get out of my house! I don't want to see your face ever again!

Without a moment of hesitation, the girl gets up and leaves the apartment.

When she steps out onto the street, the last of the light has faded, and a cold fog is rolling in to engulf the world around her.

She wanders around aimlessly. Seen through the fog, everything seems distorted—the trees, the buildings, the pedestrians. Her hair is growing fast, trailing behind her like a black ribbon.

Girl: Cut it off, and everything will be all right again...

She holds out her hand and feels something coolly metallic in her palm.

Snip, snip, snip...

You let go of the blades. The room returns to normal.

Adela's chest is heaving. It's obvious that seeing into her past must have been taxing on her.

She keeps her eyes closed, as if relishing those memories of pain and regret.

Chief: Those are your memories. That's how you got your power.

Adela: I erased them. Now they've come back to me.

She opens her eyes, which are now devoid of gentleness and composure. She gives you a weak smile.

Adela: Thank you for helping me recover these memories that I erased.

Chief: You erased your memories and your mother's, didn't you?

Adela: ...

Adela: Yes.

Chief: Why did you do that?

Adela: I had no choice.

Her voice is uncharacteristically impatient.

Adela: You would've done the same thing if you were me.

Chief: But...

Adela: My mother and I, we both got what we wanted.

Adela: She wanted happiness, and I wanted her to have it without having to feel guilty about leaving me behind. So I erased our shared memories of us arguing, of us hating and hurting each other.

Adela: And I got my peace.

Chief: You lost each other.

Adela: I didn't care. My mother wouldn't have cared either.

Adela looks at you.

Adela: I made the right choice.

Adela: And you don't have to feel sorry for us.

Her voice is cold and distant.

Adela: It's about time we ended this farce of an investigation.

Adela turns around and makes for the door.

**【Advance】**

- Stop Adela

**end**

You grab her by her wrist.

Adela turns to face you. She doesn't look angry, but when she speaks, it's in a cold voice.

Adela: What else do you want from me, Chief?

Chief: How you want to proceed from this is entirely your business. It's just that I haven't got the whole picture yet. I have something to show you, and I want to hear your thoughts afterward.

You produce a tape. It's a recording of an interview with a man who has an intimate knowledge of the tenants of Apt E.

Does Adela really not care about her mother?

## Adela - Interrogation III

> After Adela fell into a coma, you called for staff of the MBCC to bring you back.
> You recalled all the things that had happened in Sloane Street, the times you had spent together. This time, you will not forget.

Javis: Kelly? You mean Mary Jane Kelly?

Javis: That name sure brings back a lot of memories. She liked to sit where you're sitting now.

It's early. Only a couple of patrons are in the bar. The waiter answers your questions while polishing glasses with a cloth.

Javis: Poor woman...

Javis: Kelly was once a promising ballerina. But as is the case with many young women in Eastside, fate was not kind to her. She was involved with a seedy man, who abandoned her when he found out she was pregnant. She lost her job, and at one point, she was close to becoming homeless.

Javis: She kept the baby.

The waiter stops polishing the glass in his hand for a second and looks to the doorway.

Javis: I still remember the first time I met her. It was raining that day, and she didn't carry an umbrella. She rushed into the bar soaking wet. She was desperate for a job.

Javis: So she started working here. At first, she only danced and sang on the stage. Later on, some patrons would pay for her to sit with them.

Javis: Life was not easy for her, or for many of us for that matter. And Kelly was—uh, how should I put it, headstrong.
