# Cassian Interrogation

## Cassian - Interrogation I

> Intelligence before completion:
> After the catastrophic explosion aboard Euphoria, Cassian, the Sinner who served as the ship's bartender, went missing. After days of searching, you finally find him on a rooftop in Eastside, deeply disturbed.

> Intelligence after completion:
> After days of observation and a heartfelt conversation, you come to understand the blood feud that weighs on Cassian. Yet when his vengeance finally settles, he seems like nothing more than an empty shell. He has lived his entire life under the shadow of duty and hatred, never truly living for himself. "Selfhood" is to him an unattainable luxury.
> When the interrogation reaches a deadlock, you offer to help clear his family's name. This proposal finally begins to open the long-sealed door to his heart.
> You immediately spring into action, gathering the materials needed for his exoneration.

### Interrogation Transcript

The night is silent, like a drop of wine sealed in glass.

Cassian stands before the door and knocks. Two soft "thud, thud" sounds fall, followed by faint stirring from within. A moment later, the door opens.

Falke slouches against the table, a silk robe hanging loose on his shoulders. He has the look of an old beast, smug and sated after feasting on a prey. He glances at Cassian, his tone dismissive.

Falke: Room service? I didn't order anything.

Cassian: My apologies for the intrusion. This is a complimentary offering from Euphoria for our most distinguished guests.

With Falke's nod of approval, Cassian steps inside, his head bowed, and sets a silver tray down on the table. It holds a drink of his own concoction. He uncorks the bottle and pours a glass. The amber liquid swirls, its rich aroma almost overpowering the faint scent of rot in the air.

Cassian: It's your usual blend. I've taken the liberty of making some minor adjustments. I added... something better suited for memories.

Falke: Oh?

He lifts the glass, takes a sip, and raises an eyebrow.

Falke: Not bad at all. When did you come aboard? I don't think I've seen you before.

Cassian: I'm a recent hire.

Cassian's voice stays calm and steady in the dim, hazy lights of the VIP suite. He slowly raises his head, revealing the horrendous scars on his burned face.

Cassian: But I think you have seen me before. You've simply forgotten.

Falke freezes, his expression cracking.

Falke: You are...

Before he can finish, Cassian runs a finger along the rim of the glass. A wisp of vapor rises, and the bouquet twists into invisible blades, piercing deep into Falke's mind. The man's face contorts. The glass slips from his fingers and shatters on the floor.

Falke: What is this... you... you're—!

He finally recognizes the face, the eyes of the boy who once pleaded at his feet. A pained groan escapes Falke's lips as his consciousness splinters under the psychic assault.

Cassian watches coldly, the liquor dancing on his fingertips. All he can see are the shadows of his own past, stretching across an endless night.

On his first day aboard the ship, he'd played the piano and mixed drinks with every ounce of skill he had, all to secure a position on Euphoria.

Recruiter: That scar's an eyesore. Skills are mediocre at best. The only things you've got going for you are these hands... pale, slender fingers, well-proportioned.

He ran his fingers over Cassian's knuckles, examining them like merchandise at an auction. Suddenly, his tone shifted, and he clicked his tongue in mock pity.

Recruiter: Such a shame... They're not soft enough.

Without warning, the man's hand clenched. He snapped Cassian's little finger with a sickening crack.

Cassian let out an agonizing scream. He struggled, but the guards behind him held him fast. Thumb, index finger, middle finger, ring finger. Then the other hand. The excruciating pain seemed to have no end.

Finally, they dumped his limp body in the garbage-strewn rear cabin, like a broken doll.

Recruiter: Don't give me that look. I'm just putting you where you belong. Sleep tight, Mr. Cassian. May you be worth something in your next life.

And earlier, when his family was crushed by false accusations, Cassian had knelt before Falke's expensive leather shoes in his father's old formal suit.

Falke: Begging me? What good will that do? Fate decided that you and your family would serve me. It also decided how you would make your exit.

Falke: If you must hate something, hate yourselves. You weren't born as hawks. No matter how high you climb, you're still doomed to fall to your death.

Falke: Try not to be born in the gutter next time. Now, say your prayers. Serve me one last time.

Then came the screams, the wails, the roaring fire. Through the smoke and ash, his mother's scalding hands touched his burning, bleeding face. "Live," she whispered, before shoving him away, vanishing forever into the void of memory.

Cassian clenches his fist. The amber liquor rises into the air, writhing and contorting before plunging into Falke's eyes, his ears, his nostrils, his mouth.

Falke howls, toppling over his chair. He convulses madly on the floor, his throat producing only torn grunts as he plummets, struggles, drowns in illusions.

Cassian stands motionless, his eyes calm, as though he's looking at someone long dead. He steps forward, crouches, and leans close to Falke's ear.

Cassian: Do you remember? You told me to pray and serve you one last time. So here I am. This judgment... this is the last service you'll have from me.

Cassian gets up. He pulls the IV needle from his arm and disconnects the monitors. He takes his clothes from the locker, changes, and quietly slips out of the room.

His last memories of Euphoria are vague. The last thing he remembers is steering the boat carrying the three of them away from the quicksand before exhaustion claimed him. How he got out of WhiteSands, how he ended up back in Eastside... he has no idea.

But none of that matters anymore.

Cassian walks the streets of Eastside like a ghost drifting among the living. Pedestrians hurry past, all of them strangers, indifferent to the story of someone whose shoulder they just brushed.

Only the massive electronic billboard overhead is still tirelessly cycling through the news...

Cassian stares at that familiar face.

The hypocrite who once stood in the light and preached about "justice" now lies inert in a hospital bed, riddled with tubes. People speak of him with a mixture of shock and pity.

Cassian: I won.

He mutters to himself, his words drowned in the city's din.

He remembers himself from years ago, staggering down these same streets, bruised and broken and barely clinging to life, his tattered coat doing little to shelter his skinny frame.

He'd stood beneath this very screen, watching that same man give a smiling interview about "stabilizing the situation" and rooting out "the corrupt parasites." In that moment, hatred had burned like a fire in his blood.

Cassian had imagined his vengeance countless times, fantasizing about dragging that man from his pedestal, watching him weep and beg for mercy.

But now that the fire has burned out, he feels nothing but emptiness.

It's like a piece of his heart is missing, leaving him too numb even for pain. He doesn't know where to go, or what to do next.

His feet carry him, unbidden, to the roof of a skyscraper. He leans against the railing, his gaze empty as he looks down at the city with its rivers of traffic and tides of neon lights.

Cassian: Is it over...? What... should I do now?

He murmurs to no one in particular. He doesn't know if it's a statement or a question. For him, right now, the difference between staying and leaving has become blurred.

Every evening, he pours a glass of liquor he never drinks. He just holds it by the rim and sits by the window, staring into space from dusk until late into the night.

Cassian: You've been watching me. It's all right. I've known for a while.

You're taken aback, but he merely offers a faint smile before lowering his eyes again.

Cassian: There's no need to be so guarded. I won't do anything you don't want me to. I won't cause you any trouble.

His tone is polite, gentle, even considerate. But beneath it, you hear a submission so absolute it feels like the stillness of death. It's not the result of rational self-restraint, but the instinctive response of someone who has completely given up on himself.

Chief: ...We need to talk.

You say it after a moment's silence. He nods, rises to his feet, and silently follows you into the interrogation room.

The interrogation room is sterile, the lights cold. Cassian sits across from you, eyes downcast, quiet as a statue robbed of its soul. You don't cut to the chase. Instead, you flip through a case file and speak in a casual tone, as if to make small talk.

Chief: Quite eventful lately, isn't it? After that night on Euphoria, Falke had a complete mental breakdown. They managed to save his life, but his prognosis isn't looking good.

Chief: This is no small matter. His doctor thinks it's trauma from the explosion, but I can't shake the feeling there's more to it than that.

You pause, looking up at him.

Chief: You were on Euphoria that night, Cassian. What do you know?

He calmly raises his head to meet your gaze. There's no evasion in his eyes, no anger. Not even the slightest flicker of emotion.

Cassian: I did it.

Chief: ...You're admitting it, just like that?

Your hands pause over the file, your eyes betraying your astonishment. You're taken aback by his frankness, but he simply nods, his tone calm and forthright.

Cassian: Yes, I used my ability to cause Falke's mental breakdown.

Cassian: You can lock me up right now. Prosecute me according to your regulations.

Cassian: The case is closed, Chief. You don't need to waste any more time on this. I won't cover up what I've done.

His voice is devoid of emotion, as if he has already passed judgment on himself and is ready to accept any verdict. As if he's actively seeking punishment. But you shake your head, your gaze settling on his gaunt face.

He's just a man who's lost everything, struggling to survive in the wreckage. His methods may have been extreme, nearly costing him his own life, but this isn't the time to be assigning blame.

Chief: I'm not here to press charges. I'm more interested in your help investigating Falke's other crimes than in judging you for what you've done.

For the first time, a flicker of surprise crosses his face.

Chief: The reason Falke survived, despite his mental breakdown, is that one of his powerful old friends took him along during the escape. But that same friend threw him to the wolves the moment they were back in the city.

Chief: Falke's finished. His so-called allies are scrambling to distance themselves, pointing fingers, cutting ties, kicking him while he's down. It's quite the show.

Chief: Given the stakes, we've joined the investigation. But there are still gaps to fill, crucial information that only insiders can provide. Someone like you.

You pause, studying his tired but composed face.

Chief: None of this surprises you, does it?

Cassian lets out a short, bitter laugh, his eyes full of scorn.

Cassian: Not at all. That's how they are. They lick the boots of whoever's in power and walk over anyone who falls from grace. One day you're the guest of honor, the next you're a worthless pawn.

Cassian: It's exactly what happened to my family. He lifted us to the heavens overnight, then kicked us straight into hell. Now it's his turn. What goes around, comes around.

The contempt in his voice is sharp as a razor's edge. But beneath it, you hear something else: a wound that will never heal, the harsh echo of a cruel era still weighing on his shoulders.

His cynicism isn't baseless. It was forged in blood and fire.

His tone is so light it's almost cruel, and it makes your chest tighten.

You look into his eyes, which seem incapable of reflecting a future, and find yourself at a loss for words. You finally realize that what he truly lost wasn't his family or his dignity, but himself.

You speak softly:

Cassian: Perhaps.

He says it with a slight curve of his lips, almost like a smile.
