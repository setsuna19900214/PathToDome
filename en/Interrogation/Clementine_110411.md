# Clementine Interrogation

## Clementine - Interrogation I

> Clementine's demeanor remains between cooperative and careful probing. Her responses are swift and precise, revealing little emotion. What concerns you more is the recording though—she's wavering, but the patience of the other party is wearing thin. Time is running out.
> When standing at the cliff's edge, courage is required whether you take a step back or press forward.

Thunder rumbles within the dark clouds. Rain pours over Eastside, sending the city into a dreary haze.

Police lights flicker beneath the shadow of the trees before the manor. Reporters have completely surrounded the place, cameras in hand. At the center of it all, a middle-aged maid is being escorted into a police car.

Through the jostling crowd, you catch the eye of a frazzled-looking sheriff.

Sheriff: You're late, Chief. The maid's already confessed.

Chief: I read the report on the way. I'm only here to confirm the situation. This makes three cases now, doesn't it?

Sheriff: That's correct... Shipping tycoon Porto, banker Leighton, and now young Humbert. All of them died under bizarre circumstances, yet in each case, someone came forward immediately with a confession.

Strange cases have been cropping up across Eastside recently. Last month, shipping tycoon Porto suffered brain death due to a malfunctioning nutrition pod, and the resuscitation failed. Banker Leighton, on the other hand, died of a heart attack after mistakenly ingesting NSAIDs.

As for this case, which occurred early this morning, the Humbert estate owner's only son was found dead in his bathroom. A garter stocking was pulled tight around his neck.

Chief: The ones who confessed... do they all have grudges against the victims?

Sheriff: Porto's gardener had a daughter who was used as a test subject for his nutrition pods. Leighton's chauffeur had a son who was driven to jump off a building over debt. As for today's case...

Sheriff: Years ago, young Humbert drove after drinking and killed a college freshman in a hit-and-run. Due to damaged surveillance footage and the reported vehicle being stolen, the police never pressed charges.

Chief: And the victim was that maid's only daughter.

Sheriff: Precisely. She confessed to the crime without hesitation. Strangely enough, though, she refuses to reveal how she did it, no matter what we ask.

Technically speaking, none of these cases fall under MBCC jurisdiction, but...

Something feels off. The victims in all these cases were wealthy elites with long histories of committing vile acts, none of which were ever brought to justice. The perpetrators were all people they had wronged, and somehow, each one devised an incredibly sophisticated plan to bypass security and got close to their targets...

As if they were following a script someone wrote for them.

You step into the bathroom. It seems there should be crystal chandeliers hanging from each corner of the ceiling, but right now, one lies shattered, and another spot is empty. Freshly starched shirts and aprons are scattered across the floor along with some undergarments.

Honestly, at first glance, this could easily pass for the scene of an accident. But then, you catch a faint glint in your peripheral vision as you examine the area once more.

Chief: This is...

Strange collectibles can be found on the desk—butterfly specimens, acorns, and several worn dice. A well-thumbed book bound in dark red leather lies facing down. A slender hand picks it up, fingertips trailing across the words on its pages.

??: A chandelier, fishing lines, and a perfectly timed accident... Not a bad piece of work, wouldn't you say?

The wind stirs the curtains. An uninvited shadow appears in the room.

This, however, doesn't surprise her. She slowly closes the book and returns it to the shelf, then reaches for a crystal wine glass. She then leisurely pours wine into the empty glass, all while smiling in the direction of the shadow.

In the interrogation room, Clementine sits with perfect composure, not showing even the slightest hint of discomfort. Two cups of hot tea have been set on the table between you. Through the rising steam, a faint smile plays on her lips.

Clementine: If that's the case, the cases should have been closed right then and there.

Clementine: It's just a coincidence, of course. There wouldn't have been enough time. Even I need considerable effort to craft such sophisticated crimes in my novels.

Clementine: But if you insist on pinning this on me...

Clementine: Perhaps Redwood gave them hope. They realized that injustice is only addressed when it becomes a scandal. Taking such desperate measures isn't so strange given their circumstances, wouldn't you say?

For a fleeting moment, you catch what looks like a sly glint in Clementine's eye. However, in the next instant, her expression returns to one of detached indifference.

Clementine: Why couldn't it be a miracle of love? Maybe that gave them the power to achieve vengeance against all odds?

Chief: You can dismiss everything I've said so far as speculation, but how do you explain this?

You pull out a slightly creased card and hold it over the teacup on the table. After a moment, a string of numbers appears on its surface.

Chief: The maid had a card sewn into her collar. If it hadn't been exposed to the steam, no one would have ever found this hidden message.

Clementine: Public opinion can be manipulated. The truth can be buried. And even if a conviction is secured... retribution would fall on those poor souls who dared to resist, long before the perpetrator walks free.

She leans back in her chair and tilts her head up slightly. The steam clears, revealing a hollow, emotionless gaze.

Clementine: DisCity is vast, and there are more souls holding grievances than anyone can count. Not everyone can make it to your office door, much less survive long enough to see justice done.

You're left speechless. Everything she said is true.

Seeing this, Clementine's voice softens, regaining its gentle warmth.

Clementine: I'm sorry. I didn't say all that to condemn you.

Clementine: Quite the contrary, in fact. I very much admire your sincerity and integrity. You possess something beautiful—something I stopped believing I'd ever see again long ago... Your very existence brings hope to those still struggling in the dark.

Clementine reaches out, her fingertips brushing across your cheek tenderly.

Clementine: But not everyone seeks the light.

Clementine: That's just how this world is. There are good people like you who charge ahead stubbornly, no matter how battered and bruised you get... and then there are utterly irredeemable bastards like Carl.

Clementine: But most ordinary people are caught in between. They're too kind to avoid being trampled, but too powerless to fight back.

Clementine: There are people you and the law cannot help... so I'll help them instead.

Clementine: Hatred can only be dispelled by blood. Just passing judgment on the villains isn't enough, considering how much they've lost.

Clementine: *Their lives were already ruined.*

Clementine: After everything they've been through, do you really think they can just go on with life as if nothing had happened?

Clementine's voice sounds distant. She's talking about others, but you hear something deeper in her words—a profound grief.

Clementine: They have nothing left but hatred and rage.

Clementine: Those who come to me are all like that. That's why they're willing to step into my stories, take up the blade, and become its protagonist.

Clementine: For them... this is the only way to live.

Chief: ...And then what happens?

Clementine: Hm?

Chief: Criminals need to serve their time and atone, but life goes on. What happens after they're released? How are they supposed to live the rest of their lives?

*"What choices will they have then?"* The words almost slip out, but you stop yourself.

Clementine: What happens after? The fact that even an outsider like you, Chief, can feel the anguish of the characters in this story... I think that shows it has served its purpose well enough.

Clementine: As for me...

Clementine's smile turns distant. She's sitting right across from you, yet she feels impossibly far away.

Clementine: Imprisonment, punishment, whatever it is, go ahead and follow your procedures. Don't worry. I knew the risks when I made my bet.

The interrogation ends. Clementine admitted to assisting the suspects in all three cases, yet you feel no satisfaction whatsoever.

You open the comms.

Nightingale: Chief, did the interrogation go smoothly? You look troubled.

Chief: The interrogation went about as I expected. Clementine is willing to accept the consequences, but she's not truly repentant. Given her current state, we can't let her leave the Bureau, much less hand her over to the Public Security Bureau.

Chief: Nightingale, keep a close eye on Clementine's condition. Pay special attention to anyone she might be in contact with, and monitor her behavior when she interacts with the other Sinners.

After giving Nightingale instructions, you sit alone in your office, anxiety still gnawing at you. You open the recording again—the one you've listened to countless times already.

??: Ma'am, you've been drawing quite a bit of attention lately. This doesn't seem like your usual approach.

Clementine: You mean before I "died"?

Clementine: Give me a little more time. A sudden death takes its toll, even a staged one.

??: Time... of course. We've always valued talented partners, but we also value those who know when to exercise restraint. That is the foundation of a lasting partnership.

??: We simply expect satisfactory results. Don't keep her waiting too long.

Clementine: ...

You've lost count of how many times you've listened to this conversation, yet some of the words and tones in it still leave you deeply unsettled.

The person in the recording must be the old friend Clementine mentioned—the Sillage Society, an organization that operates in the shadows of the city-state. They were the ones who gave Clementine Mania, the power to exact her revenge, and now they're inviting her to plunge into the abyss alongside them.

The society is trying to recruit Clementine, yet she's hesitating for once.

She vanished, only to reappear out of nowhere, and then she used these acts of revenge as a message to show that she's still alive. But why? Is it a cry for help? Or is she really just doing what she claims... enacting justice in her own way?

Clementine: *Their lives were already ruined.*

Clementine: *They have nothing left but hatred and rage.*

Clementine's novels are still on your desk. Both the popular and obscure ones. Were the people in her stories really just characters meant to serve a tale of vengeance?

You turn to the case file at your side. The victim list for the Redwood arson is painted in stark red, while the survivor column holds only a handful of names. And even those were buried in WhiteSands years ago, leaving Clementine as the only one left.

Perhaps the person whose life was destroyed, who has nothing left but hatred and rage... is Clementine herself.

You can't let her fall to the darkness. You have to do something.

You can only hope it's not too late.

## Clementine - Interrogation II

> Amid the orphanage ruins, the two of you speak openly. She's far too clever not to understand your intentions. But then an assassin appears, communications with security are severed, and Clementine's Mania erupts. Two weeks later

Somewhere on the outskirts of Eastside

The car leaves the city, winding through overgrown paths before finally coming to a stop in front of a dilapidated building.

You step out and open the door for her, but she remains seated. Despite her attempt to hide it, the surprise on her face as she takes in the view outside doesn't escape your notice.

Clementine: Is this your idea of punishment?

Chief: The crimes of Carl's family are still being looked into. There might be new leads here that the search team overlooked.

Clementine: The MBCC certainly has an unconventional approach to discipline. Haven't I already told you everything you needed to know?

Chief: It wouldn't hurt to go over it again, would it? Think of it as field research for your new book. I heard you've been gathering material lately.

Clementine steps out of the car. For a brief moment, nostalgia flickers in her eyes. Before long though, her composure returns, that practiced smile sliding back into place.

Clementine: My, keeping tabs on me, are you? Did that child called Wynn tell you that? Are you helping her press me for my manuscript?

Clementine: A seasoned author works at her own pace. You don't need to worry about my progress, Chief. Besides, aren't we here on business?

She won't speak unless asked, and when you probe, she acts surprised. Clementine keeps everything locked away inside, all while maintaining that unbothered facade.

Without another word, she turns away and walks toward the ravaged building alone.

You hurry after her, signaling the security detail behind you to spread out. If there are any traces of the Sillage Society, they'll alert you immediately.

Clementine closes her eyes. Years of rain have carved streaks down the charred walls, like tears running down a face. She gently traces them with her fingers.

Clementine: The smell of smoke... is gone. Aside from the fire, will anyone even remember what happened here?

Chief: If you're willing to talk about it, I'll make sure to remember. Anything goes.

You seize the opportunity, but she merely gives you a quiet smile and a knowing glance, as if she'd casually cast a line and baited out your true intentions just like that.

You make a quick recovery, keeping your expression serious.

Chief: This is also for the investigation. You were there, so you'd know more about Redwood Orphanage than any of us.

You expect her to dodge the question again, or perhaps throw you another puzzle and watch you struggle for answers. However, all she does is pause for a moment before walking straight toward the desolate, debris-strewn foyer.

Clementine's steps are light as she spreads her arms wide, just like a gracious host would when welcoming a guest into a home she's lived in for years.

Clementine: This was the reading room. Annie would play with her teddy bear here. The shelves were filled with fairy tales donated by kind souls, and there was a bean bag chair in the corner.

Clementine: The filling was buckwheat. On sunny days like this, we'd take it outside to air it out. It smelled wonderful—like a wheat field at harvest.

Clementine takes your hand and guides it to a series of notches carved into the doorframe at varying heights. There are markings beside them, but they've long since faded. You glance at her questioningly.

Clementine: This is where we'd measure everyone's height. It was an annual tradition, and the children always looked forward to it.

You trace your fingers along the marks. The highest line on the doorframe barely reaches your chest.
