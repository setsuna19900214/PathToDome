# Dudu Interrogation

## Dudu - Interrogation I

> Dudu demonstrates her thorough knowledge of the Drifter Camp during the interrogation. Despite refusing her request to release her back to the Drifter Camp, you promise to share some of the information you pry out of the camp. In exchange, Dudu agrees to obey the Bureau's rules.
> Finally, the problem troubling you for days is solved.

In just one night, a stack of complaint letters as thick as half a coin piles up on the desk.

There are all sorts of Sinners in the Bureau. Those who like to play the guitar, those who like to dance, those who like to raise flowers or animals... And usually, you respect their innocuous little hobbies.

But this newcomer Dudu really puts you in a bind. She loves to play the same song on her suona over and over again. At first, everyone was entertained by something new, but after a while, no one can stand it anymore.

You've talked to her several times about this, but she's sly and can't be persuaded. She'll simply agree to your demands and then keep doing what she always does. Recently, there are even signs of her behavior worsening...

Her blaring suona kept you up all night last night. Judging from these complaints, you weren't the only one.

A burst of footsteps snaps you out of your thoughts. Nightingale has come to tell you that both the venue and Dudu are ready for you.

You shake your head and stand up, taking along the stack of complaints.

Chief: Let's go.

Dudu: Hey boss, you're finally here? Sleep well last night?

Dudu starts blabbering eagerly as soon as you sit down across from her.

This little girl is always full of life and sociable, yet there always seems to be some wicked intentions lurking beneath her toothy grin.

She leans forward, observing you carefully.

Dudu: Oh no, the dark circles under your eyes tell me you didn't. In that case... I'll play you a song on my suona to cheer you up? Since you're the boss, I'll give you a 20% discount for listening...

As she speaks, she takes out the incriminating "weapon" as if revealing a treasure, the precious suona that she forbids anyone to touch. Once, when someone touched it out of curiosity, Dudu almost pounced on him in a fit of rage.

**【Choose One】**

- Interrupt her

- Listen patiently

**end**

Chief: No, thanks.

Dudu: Huh? Did I charge you too much? Don't worry, the price is negotiable. A 50% discount, then...

**【Advance】**

- Interrupt her again

**end**

Chief: That's enough. Check these out.

You cut her off, and push the stack of complaint letters towards her.

She looks around, then suddenly lowers her voice mysteriously.

Dudu: Cough cough. I'll give you a 50% discount, but don't tell the others. It'll be a secret between us.

She looks at you eagerly, but doesn't get a reply.

Dudu: Uhh... Why aren't you saying anything?

Chief: Are you done? Then let's get to the topic.

Dudu: The topic? You're not here to listen to my music?

You don't reply, but just push the complaint letters towards her.

Chief: Look at these.

Dudu's gaze follows your hands and lands on the pile of letters. Her eyes widen incredulously.

Dudu: These are...?

You pick out a few at random, clear your throat and start reading them out loud.

Chief: "Chief, I haven't been able to shut my eyes for three whole days because of that damn suona. The dark circles under my eyes are so thick that the alarm goes off when the sensors scan my face."

...

Chief: "Chief, I feel like I'm in the countryside again, only the roosters back home don't crow as much as she does."

...

Chief: "I'm going insane. If I hear that song one more time, I'll put poison on her mouthpiece at night. I'll really do it! It's me or the suona... One of us has to go!"

Chief: ...

After reading out these complaints, you can't help but worry about everyone's mental health. Dudu's smile fades.

Chief: I remember warning you multiple times not to play that song all day and night. If this continues, I'll have no choice but to confiscate your suona.

Sure enough, the words "confiscate your suona" instantly makes her panic and yell at you.

Dudu: Who gives you the right to confiscate my suona?!

Dudu: How is me playing the suona any different from the combat training y'all do? You know what'll happen if I don't practice and get rusty? My livelihood depends on it!

Dudu: Plus, my teacher taught me this piece back in the day. It's the one I play best!

Dudu: I was like this back in the Drifter Camp. I'd go play whenever there was a wedding or funeral, and if there were none, I'd just play by the roadside.

Dudu: Everyone in the Drifter Camp loves hearing me play. The generous ones even give me money. I really don't get why you folks at the Bureau hate it so much.

Her lips tremble, as if she had just suffered a grave injustice.

Dudu: Since y'all find me so annoying, just let me go home! This place cramps my style anyways...

**【Advance】**

- Go home?

**end**

Chief: You mean to the Drifter Camp?

Dudu: Of course. Where else?

Chief: If I remember correctly, aren't you an Outlander? You couldn't have been in the Drifter Camp for more than a few years.

Dudu: So? The Drifter Camp is the place that truly accepts me. It's my home.

Dudu snorts, unimpressed.

Dudu: So what if I haven't been there that long? I know the Drifter Camp as well as any local!

Dudu: Let me put it this way. Even if you drop me into the Drifter Camp blindfolded, I can tell which street I'm on by the smell alone.

Chief: Are you being serious?

Though her tone is calm and convincing, you can't help but wonder if she's mixed the real with the imaginary.

Dudu: Of course I'm being serious!

Dudu: Who's the biggest spender, who you should never cross, which part of the river is best for catching fish, which forest grove is best for a date, who sells the yummiest stinky tofu, whose cat catches the most mice, who's the best matchmaker in the camp... I know the answers to all these questions!

She pauses, her eyes shifting about. Heaven knows what tricks are popping into her head.

Dudu: Hmm... If you don't believe me, I'll give you a consultation for free.

Chief: For free? Knowing you, shouldn't you be extorting some sort of fee?

You raise your brows in surprise.

Dudu: Pfft, I make my money from honest work, okay? How can you call it "extortion"... Anyways, back to the subject. I'm doing you a big favor this time. Ask me anything you want. If I can't answer it, I'll swap the syllables of my name back to front!

...But wouldn't that still be Dudu?

Your mouth twitches. You calm yourself down, trying your best not to let her ruin your composure.

This little girl had tricked you many times before, so you're always extra vigilant when dealing with her. Even now, you're keenly aware that she's goading you to ask her about the Drifter Camp.

In that case... It's better to just go along and see what tricks she has up her sleeve.

**【Advance】**

- Ask casually

**end**

Chief: Then... Did anything major happen in the Drifter Camp recently?

Dudu: Anything major...

Dudu rubs her chin, thinks for a moment, then flashes a confident smile.

Dudu: Of course there has!

Dudu: A goose escaped from the market and chased the blacksmith's youngest son for three miles straight. This trauma has left him with a deep fear of birds, and now he pees his pants at the honk of a goose.

Dudu: The egg seller's daughter plucked a bunch of chicken feathers to make a shuttlecock, but she spooked her family's hens so much that they stopped laying eggs. Her mother beat her up for this.

Dudu: Our building manager's son is getting married next month. He's ecstatic and preparing a huge wedding. I must go and play him a song or two.

Dudu: Ah, there's more! The old lady who lives next door to me hasn't been in the best health. She felt that she didn't have much time left, so she asked me to help arrange her funeral...

Even though it was just a casual question, you can't help but cut off her incessant blabbering. These petty trivialities couldn't be farther from "anything major."

Chief: I asked if "anything major" happened. Maybe you can pick some important things to talk about.

Dudu: These things aren't important? They may seem meaningless to you, but for us in the Drifter Camp, these things make up our daily lives.

Dudu: We're not like you. We don't have any lofty ideals. Survival is our only pursuit. So yeah, those things are pretty major to us.

Her stubborn, willful gaze puts you out of words.

Chief: ...Sorry, you're right. I didn't think of it that way. I shouldn't have said that.

A look of astonishment flashes across her face. She obviously didn't expect you to apologize. After regaining her focus, she rubs her nose and coughs awkwardly.

Dudu: Cough... Okay, okay. I forgive you.

That little episode makes you curious about the source of her news, so you take the lead in breaking the ice.

Chief: Where did you get all that news from?

Dudu: Ah, well that requires some technique.

Perhaps you're just imagining it, but you feel that Dudu has become more relaxed and sincere towards you.

Dudu: The best places in the Drifter Camp to get news are: the riverside where ladies wash clothes in the evening, the night market stalls where people grab food after work, the game tables of senior citizens, and the roadside where the Homeless beg.

Dudu: Each of these places has it pros and cons.

Dudu: The laundry ladies aren't very vigilant. They start chatting nonstop whenever they bump into someone, but it's usually just neighborhood gossip that won't interest you.

Dudu: You can get a wider variety of news from where the workers gather. These aren't limited to just neighborhood gossip. After all, they have diverse sources. But these middle-aged men love to brag, so a lot of it is hearsay.

Dudu: As for the elderly, they've lived a long time and have seen a lot, but often their minds are a bit muddled, so it's easy for them to forget the details of past events.

Dudu: The Homeless are everywhere in the Drifter Camp. Nothing escapes their eyes and ears. Getting news from them is often the most efficient choice, and their news is the most accurate. But these guys are very greedy, and will probably try to rip you off.

You listen quietly and intently to Dudu. It seems that she really knows the Drifter Camp like the back of her hand, and has a lot of experience gathering information.

Dudu: So how about it, boss? Now do you believe how well I know the camp?

Chief: I believe you.

Dudu: In that case, I have another brilliant idea. Want to hear it?

Sure enough, she can't wait to let the cat out of the bag. This is exactly what you've been waiting for.

**【Advance】**

- Let's hear it.

**end**

Dudu: Why don't you send me to the Drifter Camp for a while to gather news for you?

Dudu: I know that the MBCC is now in contact with the Drifter Camp. You probably need a lot of intelligence. As someone who is intimately familiar with the place and won't raise suspicion from the locals, I'm your best choice.

You listen to her premeditated proposal and smirk.

Chief: So after leading me down the garden path, this is what you really wanted to say?

Though a bit annoyed that you didn't take the bait, she continues to push for her cause.

Dudu: Well, that way, not only will I get to keep my suona and livelihood, you also don't have to worry about looking after me, and no one's life will be disturbed. Isn't it a win-win?

Chief: …Sorry, the MBCC has no precedent for releasing detained Sinners. We also have dedicated agents for gathering intelligence.

Dudu: What... So it's still a no after all that. I wasted my breath for nothing.

She mumbles quietly to herself, her fawning grin vanishing from her face. But your heart softens upon seeing her so upset.

**【Advance】**

- How about this...

**end**

Chief: Though I can't let you go back, I can ask the intelligence agents to regularly share information with you about the camp.

Dudu: For real? Are you being serious? You're not pulling my leg?

Dudu looks at you in surprise, lifting her head as if she has heard something unbelievable.

Chief: Of course I'm being serious. I'm not that cold-hearted.

Having gotten an affirmative answer, Dudu beams with joy, her eyes squinting from her fawning smile.

Dudu: Having some information to hear is better than nothing! No wonder you're the Chief of the MBCC! You're so wise and caring towards the masses!

**【Advance】**

- But I have some conditions.

**end**

Dudu: What are they?

Chief: From today onwards, you must abide by the Bureau's work and rest schedule. You may only play the suona at specified times and places, and you must not disturb other people's rest.

Chief: If you violate any of these conditions, the information sharing will cease immediately.

She salutes you goofily with a playful smile.

Dudu: Roger that! Don't worry boss, I promise I'll be good!

You tell Nightingale to take Dudu back. Watching her skip happily behind Nightingale, you finally let out a sigh of relief.

A wave of exhaustion sweeps over you. After reaching a deal with this little troublemaker, you can finally live a few days in peace.

## Dudu - Interrogation II

> The so-called lead about *Parma* is indeed just a pretext Dudu has made up for going back to the Drifter Camp to visit a severely ill old lady. But guided by Dudu, you interact with the residents there and observe their customs and habits up close, making quite a few useful discoveries.
> However, just when you're about to go home, you learn from the local elderly that the old lady's mind has been occupied by something named *Palama*. The truth about everything seems to hide in an abandoned building destroyed in a disaster.

However, you have only enjoyed a few days of hard-earned peace and quiet in the office, and it was once again shattered by a high-decibel shout.

?: Ch-chief! Something major has happened!

The office door swings open with a "bang." Dudu puts one hand on the door frame, gasping for breath as she tries to speak. Judging from the sweat streaming down her face, she ran all the way here.

Chief: Calm down. Tell me slowly what you want to say.

Dudu gathers her breath to talk, but what she says next comes as a complete surprise...

Dudu: Someone from the Drifter Camp has gotten ahold of intelligence about Parma!

Chief: Parma?! But I didn't see anything about it in the intelligence agent's report.

Dudu: Pfft, how long has he been there? How can he know as much as I do? To be honest, I only realized it when I was chatting with him today.

Dudu: It's my fault for not realizing it before... Anyway, we need to hurry and go as soon as possible! You'll understand when you get there!

You look at Dudu suspiciously. You can't tell if that rare look of seriousness on her face means she's trying to trick you again, or if she's really figured out something important.

But considering that anything related to Parma is no trivial matter, you'd rather be safe than sorry. You immediately assemble a team to go investigate the Drifter Camp.

Following Dudu's instructions, you lead a team of five to an old residential building in the southeast corner of the Drifter Camp.

You squint at the ivy-covered walls, which are mottled from years of wind and rain. You then glance at the senior citizens outside the building, who are chatting and fanning themselves under the shade. Your expression becomes a bit perplexed.

**【Advance】**

- Are you sure this is the place?

**end**

Dudu nods furiously.

Dudu: A hundred and ten percent! I live right next door!

Chief: ...Okay.

You nod and begin to give instructions to the guards around you.

Chief: You hide in the flower bed over there, and you guard the door. The rest of you, come up with me.

Dudu: Uhh... Well...

Dudu stammers hesitantly. You assume that she's scared, so you pat her on the shoulder.

Chief: It's okay, don't be afraid. Just stay behind me when the time comes.

Dudu: Ahhh... Actually... Hey!

Before Dudu can finish her sentence, you start leading the force inside. She has no choice but to stomp her feet and follow you.

Chief: 3...

Chief: 2...

Chief: 1...

You give a look to the guards around you. They aim their guns, kick open the door, and rush inside all in one go.

The moment you break open the door, you get bathed in large swathes of sunlight. Yet the scene before your eyes is totally different from what you had imagined...

Instead of a secret hideout filled with contraband, weapons and black-clad gangsters, it's just a plain old-fashioned home.

The decor is old and plain, but also neat and tidy, as if someone frequently cleans it. The extremely simple layout makes the tiny space feel unusually vast and empty. One can get a sweeping view of the entire home from the door, with absolutely no room to hide.

Your eyes all land on the only living being in the room. Standing alone before the window is...

An old lady?

You and the guards gawk at each other for a while. You then finally turn to Dudu.

**【Advance】**

- ...What is this?

**end**

Dudu: Hehe... Actually, I wanted to tell you just now that there's no need to make such a big commotion...

Dudu scratches her head, grinning widely.

Dudu: But you were all serious-faced and dispatching everyone here and there, so I couldn't butt in at all.

You take a deep breath.

Chief: So the person who you claim has the important information... is her?

Dudu: Yes!

You carefully observe the old lady, who's gazing out the window with blank eyes. She's old and frail, with a lame foot, and doesn't seem to be in her right mind. Even though a bunch of gun-toting strangers just broke into her house, she shows no reaction whatsoever.

You can't connect this common old lady in front of you with Parma at all.

Chief: You can't joke about such important things.

Dudu: I'm not joking at all.

She walks over to help the old lady sit down on the bed. You also go over, and watch as Dudu starts to peel an orange from the coffee table.

Dudu: Granny, why did you go to the window again? How many times have I told you not to stand that long? Your legs can't handle it.

Old Lady: ...Who is it? You... I don't know you...

The old lady stares blankly at Dudu for a while. Then, a wary look appears on her face. She slurs her words and has trouble speaking, but Dudu understands her effortlessly.

Dudu lets out a helpless sigh. She stuffs half the orange into her mouth, then puts the other half into the old lady's hand.

Dudu: Don't you remember me? I'm Dudu. I live next door.

Old Lady: You... Pa... ma... Give me back... Pa... ma...

Frowning, you listen to her mumble the word "Pa... ma" over and over again, but you just can't make out what the syllable in the middle is.

Dudu takes a glance at you, then starts to gently question the old lady.

Dudu: You keep saying "Parma, Parma." Who is Parma?

Old Lady: Pa... ma... important...

Old Lady: The old elm tree... climb the tree...

Dudu: ...What?

Old Lady: In the chicken coop...

Old Lady: I still... wait...

Chief: ...?

Totally befuddled, you cast a puzzled look at Dudu. The old lady suddenly becomes agitated for no reason. Stomping her cane with all her might, she starts to shoo you away.

Old Lady: Get out! You're not welcome here! Leave! Leave!

Dudu: How come you... You're so full of energy. It doesn't seem like anything bad happened... Sigh, okay okay, we're leaving! Quit stomping your cane!

After shooing all of you to the door, she hobbles back to the window, leaving you to face her frail, stooped but stubborn back.

Dudu: I really don't know what there is to look all day...

**【Advance】**

- Ask Dudu.

**end**

Chief: What did she mean by "elm tree" and "chicken coop?"

Dudu: Who knows? She has some mental problems and can't speak clearly. It seems to be caused by a tumor in her head. She was already like this before I moved here. The last time she was clear-headed was two or three months ago, when she asked me to arrange her funeral after she dies.

Dudu scratches her head, then looks at you as if seeking praise.

Dudu: You heard her, didn't you? I wasn't lying. She really knows about Parma.

Chief: But was she really saying "Parma"? I could only make out "pa" at the beginning and "ma" at the end, but the syllable in the middle sounded more like...

You rub your chin and ponder, but get interrupted by Dudu's nervous laughter.

Dudu: Ahaha, boss, your imagination is too wild. Of course she's saying "Parma." What else could she be saying?

Dudu: Let's go see what clues we can find from the elm tree and chicken coop she was talking about...

She pulls your arm and walks out as she speaks, trying to change the subject. Seeing her pompous and guilty reaction, you get a pretty good sense of   what's going on.

After all the trouble just now, you're pretty sure that the delirious old lady has nothing to do with Parma, but you still let Dudu drag you along and continue the investigation.

First, it's a good chance to observe how people live in the Drifter Camp, and second, you want to see what tricks Dudu has up her sleeve.

You send the other team members back to the Bureau, and delve deeper into the Drifter Camp with Dudu on your own.

Dudu first climbs onto an old elm tree in front of the vegetable market and pretends to search around. As you expect, she finds nothing, then scampers away with the excuse of "searching the chicken coop."

While she's gone, you try to strike up conversations with the residents here. Seeing that you came with Dudu, they don't seem to be on guard against you.

**【Advance】**

- Ask the Blacksmith.

**end**

Chief: Hi, excuse me, do you know Parma?

The blacksmith stops cleaning his furnace and looks at you, puzzled.

Blacksmith: Parma? You mean as in Parmesan cheese?

You're speechless for a moment.

Chief: You know... The one that the granny who lives in the southeast corner is always mumbling about...?

The blacksmith seems to have an epiphany and lets out a hearty laugh.

Blacksmith: Oh, so it's "Parma," is it? I could never tell what she was mumbling about. Haha, my ears are no match against you young folks'.

**【Advance】**

- Do you know that lady well?

**end**

Blacksmith: Oh, I know her all right. She was a well-known shrew in our Drifter Camp back in the day. Even the most vicious gangsters avoided her when they saw her. In a fight, she can curse with the strength of ten people, and yell at you so hard that you get a heart attack. It was terrifying!

This information catches you by surprise. Although you could tell from the old lady's face that she's not a kind and gentle character, you didn't expect her to have such a notorious reputation.

The blacksmith lets out a long sigh, then changes the subject.

Blacksmith: But ever since she got that tumor in her head a few years ago, she's completely lost it. Now she's dazed all day, and can't even take care of herself.

Blacksmith: So now, Dudu does all her cleaning and plumbing. She does all this hard work for free, but still has to endure that crazy woman's tantrums. It's really tough for Dudu.

**【Advance】**

- Dudu would do things like that?!

**end**

You can't help but blurt out your doubt. The blacksmith looks at you long and hard.

Blacksmith: You must've met Dudu not long ago, so you still don't know her well, eh?

Blacksmith: The little tyke's got a lot of bad habits, that's for sure. She's cunning, greedy, annoying, a freeloader, full of bad ideas, and loves to fool others... But she's definitely not a bad or selfish person.

Blacksmith: Didn't that crazy woman's condition worsen over the past two days? Dudu must have been worried after hearing the news, so she hurried back.

Blacksmith: Although Dudu's an Outlander, we all know that she thinks of the Drifter Camp as her home and us as her family. She does all those chores voluntarily, and while she complains, she never stops her actions of care and love. Isn't that what family does?

Really...

The Dudu described by the blacksmith is so different from the Dudu you know. Then... which one is the real her?

As you lower your head to ponder, an energetic voice rings out.

Dudu: Boss! I'm back!

Dudu stands before you with messy hair and chicken feathers all over her body, as if afraid that others won't know where she went. You suppress your laugh as you help pick off the feathers and tidy her hair.

**【Advance】**

- Found anything?

**end**

You already know the answer, but you still ask her as a formality.

Dudu: Sigh, what a bummer. I didn't find Parma. Just eggs.

Yet there's no sign of disappointment on her face. She weighs the eggs in her hands a few times, then slips them into her pocket.

Dudu: But it wasn't a wasted trip. I can get an extra meal when we go back.

The little boy who has been curiously listening to you chat stares at the scattered chicken feathers in fear, then shifts his shocked gaze to Dudu.

You suddenly remember that Dudu told you about a poor kid in the camp who got chased by a goose and became terrified of birds. This is probably him.

Blacksmith's Son: You got pecked, too, Dudu?

The little boy asks timidly, but Dudu flashes him a bright smile.

Dudu: How's that possible? This is from my epic battle with it.

Blacksmith's Son: Then did you win?

Dudu: Do you even need to ask? Of course I won!

Blacksmith's Son: You're so cool, Dudu! How did you win?

Dudu: Ta-da! My invincible suona! Whether it's a chicken, duck or goose, one song will scare 'em away!

Dudu proudly takes out her suona, and the little boy looks at her with adoring eyes.

Blacksmith's Son: Wow!

Dudu: Want to hear it?

Blacksmith's Son: Yeah!

Dudu: Then I'll play you a song...

Blacksmith: Stop! We don't want to be extorted again!

Just as Dudu raises her suona to play, the blacksmith stops her in annoyance. Dudu doesn't get angry. She simply puts away her suona and makes a face at the little boy.

Dudu: I'll stop, otherwise your dad will beat me. But I can teach you how to play the suona, so that you don't have to be scared of geese anymore.

Blacksmith: Shush! Stop fooling my kid! I'll really beat you if you don't leave!

The blacksmith starts shooing her away with the rag in his hand. Dudu sticks out her tongue at him, then drags you away.

She pulls you nimbly through the narrow alleys of the Drifter Camp. You gaze at the light and shadows cast upon her hair by the golden afternoon sun, feeling suddenly dazed.

In this moment, she's like a fairy who has strayed into the mortal world, forever lively, free, happy and unrestrained.

Next, you guys come to a vegetable stall.

Vegetable Seller: Parma? Never heard of it.

Dudu: Then can you tell my boss about the old lady who lives alone in the southeast corner?

She takes one look at Dudu and waves her hand impatiently.

Vegetable Seller: I don't care who's with you. Go go go. Don't get in the way of my business.

A grinning Dudu fishes out your wallet from your pocket and generously pulls out a couple of large bills for the seller.

Dudu: Keep the change, ma'am. A few random veggies will do.

Chief: Hey...

You reach out in alarm to stop Dudu, but the vegetable seller swiftly grabs the money and stuffs it into her pocket. She flashes you a fawning smile, reversing her attitude completely.

Vegetable Seller: Your boss wants to know about that old hag, right?

Dudu: Yes!

You look at Dudu in exasperation, but she winks at you without any remorse, as if to say, "This is how we do things in the Drifter Camp."

Vegetable Seller: That piece of work, tsk tsk... Back when she was still clear-headed, she'd haggle with me for half an hour for a green onion or two. I was really scared of her.

Vegetable Seller: This must be her karma for being so cruel and callous when she was young. Now she can only die alone.

As she says these unkind words, she shoves a big bag of vegetables into your arms.

Vegetable Seller: That's all I know, boss. Thank you, come again.

However, when you turn to leave, you hear her telling her daughter to cook some extra food for the evening to give to the old lady.

Chief: Doesn't the vegetable seller despise the old lady?

You ask Dudu in confusion, but Dudu responds matter-of-factly.

Dudu: Yeah. Everyone in the Drifter Camp despises her. So?

Chief: Then why did she ask her daughter to give her food?

Dudu sighs.

Dudu: We can't just watch the old lady starve to death. She's childless and has no one to take care of her to begin with. Now that she's lost her mind, she can't do anything.

Dudu: That's why people like us have to step up and take the responsibility. The vegetable seller would bring her food regularly, while the blacksmith would go to her house every now and then to see if anything needs fixing. As for me, I just help with some cleaning and chores.

...That's why her home is so clean.

Dudu: To tell you the truth, no one likes this kind of thankless work. It's dirty, tiring and unpaid, and sometimes she yells at me for no reason. There are times when my temper flares up and I swear to quit, but then I feel sorry for her again when I see her staring blankly out the window and mumbling to herself.

Dudu: If even we don't take care of her, she'll really end up dead. After all, we're the only ones she can rely on.

Deep in thought, you pace through the narrow alleys of the Drifter Camp with a big bag of vegetables in your arms. The sun overhead has already set.

Although you didn't get any information about Parma today, it was by no means a fruitless trip.

The Drifter Camp you witnessed is completely different from what you had imagined. Even the jabbering girl beside you seems more vivid and adorable now.

The sincerity between the camp residents makes you feel unprecedentedly relaxed. Here, no one has to put on a facade or watch their backs. People can be their truest selves, and express their truest thoughts with the frankest words.

Everyone here is struggling for survival, but they still love life. They are doing their best to live every day.

They despise the old lady, yet voluntarily take care of her. One should not judge someone's nature by what they say, but by what they do. Their plebeian vulgarities cannot conceal their warm and kind hearts.

Chief: It's getting late. Let's head back to the Bureau.

Dudu looks at the vegetables in your arms, her eyes shifting about. You wonder what tricks she's come up with this time.

Dudu: Wait a minute! I know!
