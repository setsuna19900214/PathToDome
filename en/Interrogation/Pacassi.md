# Pacassi Interrogation

## Pacassi - Interrogation I

> From start to finish, Pacassi has never lost control of her power. Even when someone rubbed salt into her wound or even when she felt emotional, the detector on the wall never reached a critical level.
> Perhaps you should try a different approach the next time you interact with her.

Through the glass flap on the door, you see the young girl in the interrogation room.

She sits there quietly without the slightest hint of warmth in her eyes.

You feel the thick protective suit on you, and glance at the myriad of surveillance devices in the interrogation room. After confirming that it's safe, you push the door open and switch on the lights.

Chief: Hi, Pacassi.

Pacassi lifts her head slightly, on which is her pale, apathetic face.

Chief: MBCC and the Ring Research Institute have reached an agreement to transfer you here. You will live here as a Sinner detained by MBCC from now on.

Chief: Of course, given that you will be living in the Bureau from now on, I'll have to confirm some important security matters with you.

Pacassi: I understand.

Pacassi's voice is cold and distant.

Pacassi: Please start the interrogation.

You glance at the nuclear detection module on the wall. The old device, provided by the Ring Research Institute, is almost static. The numbers on the screen fluctuate between 0.3 and 0.5—which means you're safe, for now.

You pick up the files in your hand and unwrap the bag labeled with a nuclear radiation warning sign, thinking about your conversation with Nightingale earlier today.

Nightingale frowns next to the desk. When she sees you coming in, she puts down the file in her hand and stands up with a stern face.

Since the Ring Research Institute transferred Pacassi over, MBCC has been preparing for Pacassi's detainment, while Nightingale sorts out some relevant information.

Nightingale: Below are the original descriptions.

Nightingale: "Although the subject has shown no intent to attack, radiation levels continue to accumulate when she stays in one place for a long time."

Nightingale: "When the subject experiences emotional fluctuations, the radiation level increases abnormally and can reach up to 1986.4 Roentgen."

Nightingale: "Tissue cut from the subject continues to emit intense radiation, and decontamination is extremely costly."

Chief: She's not a Sinner from the DisCity, is she? How does she have such a dangerous ability?

Nightingale shakes her head.

Nightingale: It's not specified in the file. It is only known that eight years ago, an unusual energy reaction was detected somewhere 2,000 kilometers north of the DisCity, where the Ring Research Institute monitored.

Nightingale: Documents show that an abandoned nuclear power plant was located there.

Chief: A nuclear power plant?

Nightingale: It's an energy facility in the olden days. It used technology destroyed and eliminated by the city-states—which had devastating power too.

Nightingale: ...When the rescue team arrived, the human settlement, Mayek, was already leveled to the ground. Pacassi was the only survivor.

Nightingale: Some researchers suspected that Pacassi was responsible for the nuclear accident, but there was no evidence.

Chief: And they never asked Pacassi about it?

Nightingale: There's no relevant information. The Research Institute didn't have a professional interrogation department after all, and they would't do anything beyond their duties.

Nightingale: In fact, they only studied Pacassi's abilities for the first couple of years. For the few years after that, Pacassi had been locked in a radiation isolation room.

Nightingale: The Research Institute didn't care about this Sinner who had lost her research value, nor was it obligated to find out the truth behind the incident in Mayek.

Nightingale: They just dismissed Pacassi as a nuisance and sent her to the Bureau.

Now, this "nuisance," the suspect behind Mayek's destruction, is sitting before you.

You waste no time, flipping through the file Nightingale has summarized for you, and formally begin the interrogation.

**【Advance】**

- Ask Pacassi about her ability

**end**

Chief: Tell me about your ability.

Pacassi looks at you in the eyes and replies,

Pacassi: A radiation field with a maximum radius of about 100 meters and a maximum level of about 2,000 Roentgen.

Pacassi: It can be activated by will, or reactively released by external triggers.

Chief: Looks like you know your ability very well.

Pacassi: I just memorized what they said in their research.

Her voice sounds calm while her body remains unmoved.

Chief: How are you feeling?

Pacassi: Great.

Pacassi: I don't need to eat or rest. My strength and endurance have improved a lot. And the radiation doesn't affect me.

Pacassi: I feel great.

**【Advance】**

- No negative impacts at all?

**end**

Pacassi shakes her head while her eyes subconsciously glance at her mechanical arm.

Chief: Does your arm hurt?

The dark metal sheen of the paint looks out of place on the young girl.

Pacassi: ...Okay.

Pacassi: The nerves connected to the prosthesis can hurt.

Chief: Did the Ring Research Institute install the prosthesis for you?

You are a little curious. After all, it isn't very common to see such exquisite bionic device, even in the DisCity.

If Pacassi had lost her arm in a small remote town before gaining the Sinner's ability, it would have been a massive inconvenience in her life.

Pacassi: They added the bionic fibers, but the metal structure inside was installed in the town.

Pacassi: It's been too long. The nerves were already connected to it, so it couldn't be replaced anymore.

Chief: Did it happen before the explos... before you became a Sinner?

Pacassi: Yes.

Pacassi: It was an accident in a pipe repair.

Pacassi: I got used to the prosthesis very quickly. The pain was tolerable, and it didn't affect work.

Chief: You had to work after you lost your arm? What did you do?

Pacassi: Same as before.

Pacassi: Check and repair the pipes of the nuclear power plant three times a week, weed the fields, and help the mayor process and deliver documents.

Pacassi: That's all.

Chief: That's too much work for a child.

Pacassi: Chief.

Pacassi stares into your eyes, her voice clearly sounding more emotional.

Pacassi: The DisCity is different from us and most places in the world.

Pacassi: The DisCity has abundant energy and Hypercubes to avoid natural disasters.

Pacassi: But Mayek didn't... We couldn't afford to feed idlers.

Chief: ...I'm sorry.

Pacassi: I didn't mean to blame you.

Chief: I know, but I must apologize.

Pacassi: ...

Pacassi speaks no more. The atmosphere becomes peaceful again.

Only then do you realize that not once did the device on the wall send out an alarm during the whole process of questioning just now.

This isn't quite consistent with what the Ring Research Institute claimed.

You lay out more documents before Pacassi, and a few of the images look particularly appalling.

Large, ruined steel and concrete buildings, piles of shattered concrete blocks and debris, and a crumbling ghost town covered with vines...

This is Mayek, Pacassi's hometown. Or rather, the ruins of her hometown.

Chief: Pacassi, there's an important issue I must figure out in this interrogation.

Chief: The truth behind Mayek's destruction.

Pacassi lowers her head.

Pacassi: It was an accident.

**【Advance】**

- Was it planned? Or was it a mere accident?

**end**

Pacassi: Both.

Chief: Can you elaborate more?

Pacassi: ...The nuclear power plant in Mayek had been close to its limits for as long as I could remember.

Pacassi: We were struggling to keep it running, but there's no way we could stop it from deteriorating and becoming more dangerous.

Chief: Was there no other solution?

Pacassi: Mayek was in this continent's northernmost part. Half of the year was winter.

Pacassi: There was no way we could survive without the energy generated from the nuclear power plant.

**【Advance】**

- You could leave Mayek.

**end**

Pacassi: Many did, including my parents.

Pacassi: They went to the south, to the city of dreams where they didn't have to worry about the cold and hunger... They abandoned us.

Pacassi: But there were always people who wanted to stay.

Pacassi raises her head.

Pacassi: We knew about the nuclear war that destroyed half the world after Nightfall and the risk of accidents with nuclear power plants.

Pacassi: But we had no choice if we wanted to survive in our hometown.

Chief: But still, your town...

Pacassi: Fewer and fewer people stayed. Eventually, we didn't even have enough staff to operate the power plant.

Pacassi: There was more and more work on everyone's shoulders. A lot of them couldn't take it anymore.

Pacassi: Before the accident, there were only two people left in the town.

Chief: You and the mayor, Longyear?

Pacassi: Yes.

Chief: You said Mayek's destruction wasn't entirely a pure accident.

Chief: If it wasn't you, was it Longyear the mayor?

Pacassi looks slightly stunned.

Pacassi: No... He just...

Pacassi: It was my fault.

The fact that Pacassi tries to hide it makes you more certain of your speculation.

In the early years of Nightfall, a nuclear war lasting for years wiped out most of human civilization. The survivors signed the Nuclear Restriction Pact, which banned all nuclear technology, including nuclear power plants.

Chief: You should know the implications of this pact, Pacassi.

Pacassi purses her lips without giving a response.

Chief: All I want is the truth, a reason for you to stay in the Bureau without being feared...

Chief: Whatever you've gone through in Mayek is all in the past. Mayek doesn't exist anymore. There's nothing more for you to protect.

Pacassi: That's not true!

Pacassi suddenly raises her voice.

Pacassi: I'm still alive. Mayek hasn't disappeared!

Pacassi: It's Longyear's life. It's our home... They were the ones who took me in when I was abandoned...

Pacassi: Yet I couldn't save him...

Pacassi looks up, her eyes full of tears.

Pacassi: I deserted him. I deserted Mayek...

Pacassi lowers her head after finishing the words in trembles. With her going through such emotional turmoil, you subconsciously glance at the surveillance device on the wall.

To your surprise, the numbers on the device are as calm as a late autumn lake.

**【Advance】**

- Comfort Pacassi

**end**

Looking at the young girl weeping in front of you, you can't help but reaching out.

Chief: Pacassi...

Chief: Don't worry. The Bureau is your new shelter. We won't abandon you, and we won't use you for experiments...

Chief: You can consider it your... home, if you will.

You try to comfort her, yet your words seem so empty.

Pacassi: I don't have a home anymore. Not since that winter when Mayek and Longyear was gone.

Pacassi's voice is cold and resisting.

Looking at the grieving young girl before you, you realize any words of comfort are simply pointless right now.

Perhaps you should leave her alone for now.

## Pacassi - Interrogation II

> Somewhere thousands of kilometers away from the DisCity, there was once a tragedy with irreversible consequences.
> That tragedy left a scar in the girl's mind that would never heal. She would sink deeper and deeper, drowning in a vortex of remorse and sorrow.
> Perhaps you should do something about it.

You walk in the sports field in the Bureau. The Sinners are free to move about in the designated area during this time of day, and today is no exception.

The afternoon sun is bright and warm. You look over to the bench by the fence and find Pacassi there as expected.

**【Advance】**

- Go sit next to her

**end**

Pacassi budges up without closing the book in her hand.

Chief: What are you reading?

Pacassi: The History of the DisCity, 6th edition.

You have heard of it, a well-known historiographical work that has been reprinted many times.

Chief: What made you want to read this?

Pacassi: I want to know about the history of the DisCity.

Pacassi: But more than that, I want to know how you guys view the world...

Chief: So what do you think of the Bureau now?

Pacassi: It's great here. I don't have to be in all sorts of experiments and can even have time to myself.

Pacassi: I'm really happy with my life now.

She never goes out of control, always finishes the work given to her, and never makes any mistakes, even in battles, which isn't her strong suit... Pacassi has always been reassuringly stable.

But she also hardly interacts with others. She's always alone, like an outsider.

This isn't what you want to see.

Chief: Pacassi, I have some questions for you.

Pacassi: Aren't we going to the interrogation room?

Chief: No, you've already proven yourself.

Chief: Instead of as a Chief and a Sinner, I hope we can talk as friends.

Pacassi looks at you, her head slightly tilted, and closes her book.

Pacassi: What would you like to ask?

**【Advance】**

- Take out the Mayek directory

**end**

Pacassi takes the directory from you readily.

Chief: It's just some personal questions I have. I want to know more about Mayek.

Chief: I want to know about you guys' past.

Pacassi caresses the leather cover gently.

Pacassi: Mayek was once a prosperous city, with an ice-free port which was rare in the north, and an independent nuclear power plant.

Pacassi: Mayek had a population of half a million at its peak.

Pacassi: But that was all before Nightfall.

Pacassi flips through the directory. Each page was filled with names that were then crossed out.

Pacassi: Actually, for the first few decades, there wasn't a significant decline in the population of Mayek, thanks to the nuclear power plant.

Pacassi: But since thirty years ago, the town's population had dropped below ten thousand.

Chief: Five hundred thousand to ten thousand... What happened?

Pacassi: Thirty years ago, the nuclear power plant stopped running for the first time.

Pacassi: I had read Longyear's notes. It was just a problem with the cooling tower's pipes, but they couldn't figure it out at that time no matter how much they tried.

Pacassi: To prevent the reactor core from overheating, possibly causing an irreversible accident, they shut it down.

Pacassi: When the power plant was up and running again, the town had lost three thousand people.

Chief: Did they leave the town? Or...

Pacassi: It was December. No one could leave alive.

Pacassi: People thought they were merely stuck in the winter, which they could survive through with the nuclear power plant.

Pacassi: But it wasn't until that day did they realize that the winter was longer than they expected.

The warm sunlight pours on the pages, making the names in bright red particularly glaring.

Pacassi: Since then, the nuclear power plant had more and more frequent accidents, which could last from half a day to as long as a month.

Pacassi: Longyear was elected as the new mayor, because he fixed the cooling tower in the first accident. But what he couldn't fix was the distress in the residents' hearts.

Pacassi: People had enough of living in fear, enough of mashed potatoes with ice shreds. They heard about the DisCity in the south, far more prosperous than the town they were in.

Pacassi heaves a soft breath.

Pacassi: This kind of emotion reached its peak when I was four.

Chief: What happened?

Pacassi: Same as every time. Power outage.

Pacassi: A major power outage, lasting from summer to early winter.

Pacassi flips a few pages in the directory and points to a name.

Pacassi: Leroy was a great chef. Everyone loved his borscht. Even I still remember the taste of it.

Pacassi: He left Mayek that year. And there has been no restaurant in town ever since.

Pacassi continues to flip to the back of the directory.

Pacassi: Nastia was an elementary school teacher. Her daughter went missing the year before.

Pacassi: She searched for her daughter all winter.

Chief: Did she find her?

Pacassi: Yes.

Pacassi: At the end of summer, she found her under melted snow.

Chief: ...

Pacassi: The three-month power outage forced everyone to plan for the worst.

Pacassi: That was when my parents left me.

Chief: Why didn't they take you with them?

Pacassi shakes her head.

Pacassi: I don't remember.

Pacassi: All I remember is that Longyear took me to his house later.

Pacassi: He gave me a place to live and made me fruit soup. Longyear told me he would fix the power plant, and everything would be fine.

Pacassi: But that year, when he was fixing the power plant, he lost his legs and his only son...

- That was insistent of him

**end**

Pacassi: Yes.

Pacassi: Perhaps...

Pacassi: But, at least he didn't abandon me, like he never abandoned Mayek.

Chief: What happened then?

Pacassi: After that, I took over his son's work, and became his assistant.

Pacassi: On top of the assigned work, I also helped him with chores and maintenance reports for the power plant.

Chief: What happened to the nuclear power plant after that?

Pacassi lowers her head.

Pacassi: Accidents happened more and more frequently, and there were fewer and fewer people in the town.

Pacassi: In the last two years, the power plant never started again. The remaining residents survived by demolishing abandoned houses for fuel.

Pacassi: And Longyear's mental health became more and more... unstable.

Pacassi: Everyone realized that perhaps all is lost for Mayek.

Chief: But you stayed till the end.

Pacassi: We later saw a glimpse of hope.

Pacassi lifts her head and looks at you.

Pacassi: One day, an oddly-dressed traveling merchant came to town.

Pacassi: He said he was from the DisCity and offered us a type of stone for sale—a stone that he claimed to provide protection from natural disasters.

Chief: Hypercubes?

Pacassi: Yes.

Pacassi: I bought the Hypercubes and the complementary device with spare precious metals in the nuclear power plant, and kept it secret from Longyear.

Pacassi: After we started up the device, we were able to repair the power plant in only two days. For the next two months, the plant ran stably.

Pacassi: Until Longyear found out about it...

Chief: What did he do?

Pacassi: He smashed the device, called us all traitors, and told us to get out.

Pacassi: At that time, there were only a dozen people or so left in the town.

Chief: Why did he do that?

Pacassi: Maybe he was just too tired... Or maybe he realized what he had been insisting for the whole time was totally pointless...

Pacassi: No one understood him. Everyone said he was out of his mind. He destroyed our last hope, and everyone left that summer.

Chief: So, in the last days, it was just you and Longyear?

Pacassi shakes her head.

Pacassi: In the last days, Longyear wouldn't talk to me. For the whole summer, he just wandered around the town without uttering a word.

Pacassi: Eventually, we were the only two left in the town. He showed up at my door and said to me,

Pacassi: "Why are you still here? Have you no shame? Get the hell out! Out of my town!"

- And you...

Pacassi: At that time, I just headed south with my mind totally blank.

Pacassi: It was only in the next evening when it started snowing that I realized I couldn't leave Longyear there alone.

Chief: ...

Pacassi: When I raced back to the town, I found the power plant running, with thick steam rising from the cooling tower.

Pacassi: It was as warm as summer around the power plant, so warm, even the snow melted.

Pacassi: I searched everywhere. In the end I found Longyear asleep in the duty room in his wheelchair...

Chief: That was when the power plant...

Pacassi: I don't remember the details of what happened afterwards.

Pacassi: I just remember something rushing into my body and I didn't feel cold anymore.

Pacassi: When I woke up again, I was already in the DisCity.

Pacassi: Longyear, the nuclear plant, Mayek... they were all gone. I'm the only one that survived.

Chief: He protected you, Pacassi, even when he kicked you out of the town. He wanted you to find your future.

Pacassi: ...I don't need a future.

Pacassi: Everyone was leaving for the future, leaving the past behind.

Pacassi: No one remembered Mayek and Longyear.

Pacassi: Who else will remember them if I don't?

Pacassi: I don't want this to happen.

Ring—Ring—

A rapid bell rings, indicating that the free time for the Sinners is over.

Pacassi hands the directory back to you, picks up the book on the side, and stands up.

Pacassi: Thank you. Thank you so much for all you've done.

Pacassi: You are so kind. You listened to my dull stories, gave me books I wanted to read, and treated all Sinners equally... But I'm not like them... I don't belong here.

Pacassi: I should go now.

Pacassi heads toward the nearby building hurriedly. The afternoon sun shines on her face.

Yet you can't feel the slightest warmth.

## Pacassi - Interrogation III

> Mayek's story is over, but Pacassi's story has only just begun.
> Perhaps she can finally break free from her "past" and move on with her life.

Pacassi looks at herself in the floor-to-ceiling mirror in the mall and speaks to you somewhat embarrassedly.

Pacassi: This jacket...

Pacassi: Constrains my movement. And the fabric is too thin...

**end**

Pacassi: Thank you...

Pacassi: But...

Chief: You should buy some more summer outfits too. It's not good to always wear such thick layers.

Pacassi: I don't mind heat that much...

Chief: How about trying something else?

Pacassi: ...

In this Eastside's busiest commercial street, Pacassi, who got dragged out by you, seems slightly uncomfortable being faced with a dazzling array of items.

Pacassi: I'm... really not used to this.

Chief: You need to relax.

Pacassi: Life at the Bureau is very relaxed already.

Pacassi speaks as she watches the bustling passersby and vehicles, and flinches back slightly timidly.

Chief: If you don't like it here, why don't we go somewhere else?

Pacassi: ...I don't have any place I want to go.

**【Advance】**

- There's a place you will be interested in.

**end**

...

You can see Pacassi feeling slightly helpless.

You hold Pacassi's hands as you walk along the garden street. It's a nice day, and the sun sprinkles through the leaves onto the ground.

You walk down the long road, and a huge billboard comes into view.

Pacassi: Is this... Nastia?

On the billboard is an old lady, surrounded by children, with a heartfelt smile on her face.

Chief: Do you remember what she looked like? You were very young when she left Mayek, right?

Pacassi: Yeah, I do remember.

**【Advance】**

- Want to go see her?

**end**

Pacassi: ...

Pacassi: Alright.

Pacassi: Borsc... Beet soup and bread, please.

Waiter: Is that all, Miss?

Chief: Any recommendations?

Waiter: I strongly recommend our strawberry parfait for the two of you.

Pacassi: Parfait is... ice cream, right?

Waiter: That's right.

Pacassi: I'm alright, thanks.

You order a few more dishes, and the waiter hurries back to the kitchen.

It's noon time, and the restaurant is packed with customers. Thankfully, the restaurant is very speedy in serving food. It doesn't take long before the dishes are brought to the table.

Pacassi breaks the bread into small pieces and soaks them in the soup before putting it in her mouth—a way of eating rarely seen in the DisCity.

Chief: How is it? Do you like it?

Pacassi: Okay.

Chief: Does it taste familiar to you?

Pacassi: ...uh-huh.

Pacassi nods.

Chief: This restaurant is owned by Leroy.

Pacassi puts down the silverware as her eyes widen.

As she looks around at the restaurant packed with customers, Pacassi smiles in relief.

Pacassi: Looks like he's doing great.

Pacassi: I'm... a Sinner.
