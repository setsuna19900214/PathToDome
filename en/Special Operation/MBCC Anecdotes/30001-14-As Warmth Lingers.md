# MBCC Anecdotes｜As Warmth Lingers

## ANGELL-000 Vow

Time off at the Bureau hits out of the blue every single time.

One day you are buried under a mountain of case files, and the very next, a time-off notice pops up on your comm device.

[Time-Off Notice]

Recipient: ${PlayerName}

Per automated duty log calculations, your on-duty hours exceed the recommended upper limit for 41 consecutive reporting periods.

You have accumulated 512 hours of unused compensatory time off, 480 of which will expire at the end of this month.

You are hereby scheduled for five days of mandatory time off, effective tomorrow at 00:00. No deferrals will be granted.

Note: You previously requested a deferral on the same grounds during the last reporting period. Further requests will not be accepted.

This is a system-generated notification. Do not reply.

After grinding continuously for so long, actually getting a few days off leaves you completely stumped on what to do next.

You zone out at the notice for a while before slowly turning your mind to vacation possibilities.

Sleep it off for a couple of days straight? That feels like burning daylight, and plus, you've probably slept enough for a lifetime inside that coffin anyway.

Binge-game with Hella and the others for a few days? Sure, why not. But there's nothing good to play lately, and you'd rather pass on another session of Project: Dark.

...Disguise yourself, head to an Eastside board game spot, and play as long as you want? No, playing by yourself with no friends around is just too sad.

One idea after another floods your thoughts. There are far too many things left undone and far too many things you want to try, leaving you stuck in total indecision.

Chief: Never mind. Coffee first, decisions later.

Chief: ...Wait a minute. I'm not working overtime today! Time to get a real drink instead!

You quickly set the ceramic coffee mug aside and grab the insulated cup off the edge of your desk, one you haven't touched in ages. You used to pour yourself a hot drink in this whenever you had downtime, and it's high time it saw some action again.

Right as you're enthusiastically planning your brew, you spot a fine, reddish ring lining the inside wall.

Chief: Weird... what was ever poured in here to leave that kind of trace?

Knitting your brows, you start sifting through your memories to figure it out.

Chief: Nonsense. I'm right here. You can always tell me when you have a craving.

Chief: How about I drop by the safe house sometime and teach you in person? That should be much safer.

...Oh, right. That was from last time when you made red bean soup for her when she was practically starving, and you poured a little into the cup to give it a taste first.

With some rare free time on your hands now, you definitely can't break your promise.

## ANGELL-001 Attempt

On the very first day of your leave, you head out of the Bureau early and take an hour-long drive to the farmers' market run by a satellite town in DisCity, gathering every single ingredient for the red bean soup one by one.

Sure, the Supply Office has all the supplies you could ever need. But you know Faye's overactive imagination and gossip network all too well. You can't let her catch wind of this, or the entire Bureau will be spreading ridiculous rumors about you by tomorrow.

So a few hours later, you stand in front of a door that feels both familiar and strange, arms laden with plastic bags, and knock three times.

There is no answer from inside, but you aren't worried. The safe house might have a new address, but the secret knocking pattern you two agreed on should still work just fine.

About a minute later, you catch the sound of the lock turning. Click—click—

The door swings open. The owner of the apartment gives you a single look from inside before turning back inside, leaving the door wide open for you to walk in.

The apartment is just as you remember. That familiar smell of instant noodles lingers in the air, and the goldfish bowl remains in its corner, filled with water so clean it puts the rest of the place to shame.

Several cardboard boxes are piled up by the wall, their tape intact. Looks like she only unpacked what she couldn't live without after moving in.

You shut the door behind you and walk further inside. With the curtains tightly drawn, the place might be different, but the layout is identical to the old one.

Angell: You're just in time. I'm hungry.

The woman on the couch doesn't bother asking why you showed up. Her eyes look past you, locking straight onto the plastic grocery bags you're holding.

**【Advance】**

- Looks like you haven't been eating well lately...

**end**

...Well, at least it's a step up from the last time, when she almost starved herself into passing out.

You clear a spot on the cluttered stovetop, set down the plastic bags, and turn to see a sticky note stuck to the fridge. It's the red bean soup recipe you wrote for Angell back then. You turn toward Angell to ask her.

Chief: So, ever figured out how to make that red bean soup?

Angell: ...Kind of.

She hesitates for a second, but still gets off the couch and walks over to the kitchen.

Seeing her approach step by step, your mind can't help but wander back to that night.

Chief: ...I haven't eaten for two days. I'm really hungry.

Angell: I'm hungry too.

Angell: Do you cook?

It was the crack of dawn on your third day trapped in this apartment, and hunger pushed you into the kitchen in the middle of the night to grab a knife, only for her to catch you red-handed. To save your own skin, you made up a lie that you could cook. Back then, though, instant noodles were the absolute limit of your skills, and half the time you still served them undercooked.

To this day, you have no idea if she actually bought your story. But after all the practice you put in during that period, red bean soup has turned into one of your specialty dishes.

Only now, it's Angell's turn to learn.

It's also the perfect chance to see what she actually means by "kind of."

Angell: Rinse... the beans? Don't you just throw them in the water and boil them?

Angell tilts her head, looking completely clueless. You immediately double-take at the note on the fridge, and sure enough, you never actually wrote down the preparation steps.

Alright then. Even though you already knew what to expect from someone who once asked you if instant noodles could expire, her question still leaves you completely stumped for a second.

Chief: Well... uh... there's dirt on them. And... um... soaking them lets you pick out the bad ones.

Angell: So there are bad ones.

She bends down and picks out a few red beans from the bowl to examine them closely, looking for all the world like she's trying to tell two similar-caliber bullets apart.

With all the prep work out of the way, you two can finally get around to making the red bean soup.

Angell's hands don't shake at all, but she holds that ladle like a blade. Watching the sharp snap of her wrist, you can tell the soup is milliseconds away from flying right out of the pot.

Now you understand what her idea of "kind of" actually looks like. You step up to her without a word.

Angell: ...Something wrong?

Sensing you coming closer, Angell turns her head to glance at you.

Chief: ...

Without offering an explanation, you reach out from behind to grab her wrist, slowly guiding her hand around to turn it over.

Her hand locks up under yours for an instant, then goes slack again, letting you take full control. Whenever you choose to let go, it makes no difference to her.

It isn't until you realize the pot has been simmering loudly on the stove for quite a while that you hurry to let go of her hand.

**【Choose One】**

- ...We'll stop the form-fixing for now.

- Whatever you do, don't handle a ladle like that again.

**end**

Angell: Mm.

Ten Minutes Later

Predictably, the first batch of red bean soup is a total burn.

A black layer sits welded to the bottom of the pot, turning what should be a sweet treat into a smoky, bitter mess. You switch off the stove, and the two of you lock eyes over the steaming pot.

Looks like letting Angell handle it on her own was a bit too much to expect after all.

Chief: Let me take you through it step-by-step first.

Angell: ...Okay.

Angell automatically yields her spot at the stove, letting you take over. You leave the burnt pot to soak in hot water, grab a clean one, and run through the checklist for her:

Everything left out of the original note—prepping the beans, starting on high heat before dropping to low, adding sugar only when the beans burst open—you break down step by step.

Angell takes it all in, so you hand the stove back over and let her give it another shot.

Ten Minutes Later

Pot number two under her command doesn't quite make it either. The red beans are undercooked, some even outright raw, so you don't dare chew with full force. At least the pot didn't scorch this time.

Chief: ...Honestly, a huge improvement.

Angell: Sorry. Besides fighting... I guess I'm not good for much else.

She scoops up half a spoonful, takes a small taste, and goes quiet. Standing by the stove, her hand holding the ladle slowly drops to her side.

Catching the quiet slump in Angell's mood, you default to the routine of every day spent in this apartment: you take the ladle from her hand and flick the stove on.

A rich sweetness slowly drifts out from the pot, erasing the burnt smell lingering in the air.

Angell leans against the fridge, holding the bowl of red bean soup you handed her, drinking it one spoonful at a time. Halfway through the bowl, she suddenly stops, zoning out into a complete daze.

Chief: What's on your mind?

Angell: Nothing. Just... it feels good, being able to have this again.

Angell: We had it all the time when I was little. After that... I bought it plenty of times, but only yours tastes like the way I remember it.

Angell: I never had the time back then. Now... I have plenty of time. I want to learn to recreate that taste myself.

Chief: You can't rush these things. You take it one step at a time.

Angell stays silent. She lowers her head for another sip, then glances up at the pot you scrubbed clean and reset on the stove.

And so, teaching Angell to make red bean soup officially becomes your go-to ritual for the vacation.

## ANGELL-002 Ordinary Person

It starts as red bean soup lessons, but it doesn't end there.

Today, when you walk through the door, red beans aren't what you smell in the air.

She is standing right at the stove, with two completely unrecognizable lumps sitting in the pot. Spread out on the table is a convenience store flyer, with recipe steps scribbled across the back in her handwriting.

Angell: Tomato and egg stir-fry.

You take a seat at the table and eat both of those mystery pieces down to the last bite. The taste is pretty strange—hard to put into words, really—except that the whole thing is so ridiculously salty it leaves a bitter kick.

Chief: Cough... Next time you add salt, maybe don't go so heavy-handed.

Angell: Got it.

Right between your painful coughing fits and desperate gulps of water, she pulls the flyer back toward her and writes a few more lines on the back.

——

A vibration buzzes in your pocket. You pull out your comm device and check the screen. It's an automated notice from the Bureau.

Remaining time-off: 72 hours.

You swipe it away immediately and tuck it back into your pocket. Angell catches a glimpse of the notice but doesn't say a word, just dropping her head to edit the remaining recipe steps in silence.

The place is quietly undergoing a makeover. There's even a pair of guest slippers waiting near the entrance now, meant for you.

You notice the coffee table looks much cleaner. The blade that used to sit on display is gone, replaced by a pair of mugs set side by side.

You don't bring up where the weapon went. Naturally, Angell keeps quiet about it too.

The goldfish bowl has been moved out of the gloomy corner and onto the cabinet, saving you from crouching to feed it.

You drop a small pinch of flakes into the water. The goldfish flicks its red fin twice, looking pretty pleased with the food.

Angell: Take it.

On the third day of your visit, right as you're slipping out of your shoes at the door, Angell reaches over and hands you something.

Angell: Saves you from waiting outside every time.

Unlike the key you returned to her back then, this is a spare key she went out and had cut herself.

The days move fast. Just as you carry the groceries into the kitchen like any other day and open the fridge, you hit a scene completely unlike day one.

Stocked inside is a bag of red beans, rock sugar, and a small packet of dried tangerine peel. You've never bought this much red bean soup stock in one go.

You quickly check the outside of the fridge door and realize a whole batch of new sticky notes has been added to it.

They don't just lay out the steps for red bean soup anymore; there are even notes scribbled down for a few other dishes.

"${PlayerName} said to soak the red beans long enough so they actually cook through."

"Add seasoning to taste... but how much is to taste? ...Probably around three spoonfuls? No, wait, maybe four..."

"Cut into thick thin slices... thin usually means 2mm? But a thick thin... is that 4mm? Not sure, I'll just ask ${PlayerName} tomorrow."

The click of the door lock turning sounds from behind you. You turn your head to see Angell standing in the doorway with her black cloth bag slung over her shoulder, watching you in silence.

Angell: You saw them?

Chief: With this many notes, it's a little hard to miss. Sneaking in some secret practice while I was away?

Angell: Mm. None of it turned out very well, though.

**【Advance】**

- How did you find so much time to cook anyway?

**end**

She walks over and calmly takes the note right out of your hand, sticking it back onto the fridge door. She presses down firmly on the peeling corners so they stop curling up.

Angell: Because lately... I haven't really been taking contracts.

She speaks while carefully smoothing down the paper.

**【Advance】**

- Why? The Bureau doesn't restrict your freedom, does it?

**end**

Angell: My own choice.

Angell: Because... taking contracts means less time with you. Besides... I still have plenty of cash saved up.

She turns to look at you.

Angell: I told you before... that kind of life wasn't something I wanted. I just happened to be good at it.

You pick up the ladle from the stove and press the handle into her grip.

**【Advance】**

- In that sense, you're actually pretty normal.

**end**

Chief: Normal people have a long list of things they're terrible at, too.

Angell stares down at the ladle in her hand, completely spacing out.

Angell: ...Normal.

She mutters the word under her breath, barely audible, but her grip on the ladle doesn't loosen.

Angell: The beans have been soaking since last night. Keep teaching me.

## ANGELL-003 Red Bean Soup

That day's red bean soup lesson ends up stretching all the way into the night.

By the second pot, things are already turning out pretty good, but it's still nowhere near good enough to meet Angell's standards.

You check your watch. It's almost midnight. With that, you stand up and get ready to grab your coat.

Chief: It's getting late. I'd better head back. Tomorrow we can definitely—

Angell: Wait.

Angell's voice calls out from behind you. You pause in your tracks. She's still standing in the kitchen doorway, clutching that wooden ladle in her hand.

Angell: Going back alone this late... is not safe.

Angell: Last time you got attacked, you were out by yourself at night, just like this.

You know full well she's right. As for whether the neighborhood around this place is safe, nobody knows better than she does.

**【Choose One】**

- ...Good point. Probably not the safest idea.

- Oh... right...

**end**

Chief: Guess I'm crashing here tonight, then.

Knowing you can't win this argument, you take your coat off, hang it right back up, and head over to the couch like it's second nature. This is where you slept back when you were being held hostage, after all.

...!

The second you sit down, the couch makes a groaning protest, and you sink straight into it.

You scramble out of the caved-in couch and stand there with Angell, who hurried over at the noise, both of you staring down at the massive crater left behind.

Angell: ...

Angell: There's a mattress pad under the cabinet.

Angell: Sleep on the floor tonight. It's not cold.

Angell turns around and vanishes into the bedroom right after speaking. Accepting your fate, you drag out that mattress pad and roll it out on the floor right beside her bed.

Chief: ...Okay.

When the lights go off, the room drops into silence. Quiet enough for you to pick up the faint, broken bleed of music from her headphones.

But during your time held hostage in this place, you heard this very tune night after night.

Chief: Still playing that same song?

In the darkness, Angell doesn't say anything.

A long time passes, and right when you're about to drift off, you think you hear her whisper something under her breath.

Angell: ...With these on, I don't have to listen to anything else.

You're way too exhausted to push any further. After running around all day, sleep hits you fast.

But Angell is still wide awake. Leaning back against the headboard with her headphones resting around her neck, she just sits there in the dark, watching you sleep beside her bed.

Angell: ...

Her Mania picks up on killing intent with razor precision. She's far too accustomed to violent intent. The moment anyone so much as forms a malicious thought, her physical body acts on reflex.

That reaction is how Angell has survived to this day. Over all these years, her internal tripwire has never loosened for a single second.

But over the past few days, she has felt herself slipping into a dull haze.

Someone else is sleeping in this room right now, right beside her, close enough that every soft breath comes through loud and clear.

This is the very thing she fears the most.

Angell: I...

Angell watches your sleeping face, then slowly leans down, strands of hair slipping out from behind her ear as she moves.

She reaches out a hand, fingertips hovering right beside your face, a fraction of an inch away from feeling your warmth.

Angell: ...

After a long silence, Angell slowly pulls her hand back and leans back against the headboard again.

Angell: Living like a normal person... just for a few days, it shouldn't hurt.

Angell: What do normal people do, anyway?

Mother: Angell! We're having red bean soup this morning!

Young Angell: Yay! Mom's red bean soup is my favorite!

Angell: Right... tomorrow morning, there should be something waiting.

She gets out of bed and slips quickly into the kitchen. Checking the clock, she sees there are still a few hours before sunrise. It's more than enough time.

Angell: This time, I won't mess it up.

A sweet aroma nudges you awake.

Morning light creeps in through the gaps in the curtains, and the room is filled with a familiar scent.

By the time you push open the door and step out, Angell has already been waiting at the table for a while.

Angell: Try it.

She ladles out a bowl of red bean soup and slowly slides it over to you.

It's always been you serving her soup, making this the very first time the tables are turned.

You pick up the bowl and take a sip. The red beans are cooked until soft and tender, and the sweetness hits just the right spot.

Angell keeps her eyes on you the entire time. Only after you take a second sip does she look down and serve herself a bowl.

Angell: ...So this is what it's supposed to taste like.

Angell: Everything I was ever taught... was meant for breaking things.

Angell: This is the first time I've ever made something whole.

You hold the bowl, staying completely silent.

Out of nowhere, you catch yourself wishing time would slow down just a little bit more.

## ANGELL-004 Sunshine

Angell: We're out of red beans. I want to buy more.

Angell: ...Normal people go grocery shopping on their own, right?

She drops the line while stacking the bowls, as offhand as saying it's time to take out the trash.

You set down what's in your hands and look at her in mild shock. The one time you dragged her outside in the past, she was completely on edge, resisting even a trip to the movie theater.

But right now, Angell is actually bringing it up herself, saying she wants to head out and buy groceries.

Chief: Sure, I'll come with you.

She heads to her room to change and steps out with her black tote bag slung over her shoulder.

The bag isn't fully zipped. Beyond the blade she carries everywhere, you notice an added thermos inside.

The daytime market bustles with foot traffic, a chaotic hum of calling vendors and bartering customers. Her eyes dart toward passersby every so often, but she spends most of her time inspecting the vegetables.

Finally, she stops right in front of a stall selling whole grains.

Angell: How much for the red beans?

Stall Owner: Ten DisCoins a pound! Top-grade red beans, best in the district!

She reaches into the bottom of the sack, scoops out a handful, and flattens them over her palm, checking each grain individually. At last, she picks out a few dull-colored ones and holds them up for you to see, a gesture bordering on a proud display.

Angell: These ones are bad.

Stall Owner: Whoa, sharp eyes on you, kid! You're looking closer than I do!

Watching her from the side, you recall the first time she washed red beans, examining a few tiny seeds in her hand like a riddle. But now, she's definitely gotten a lot more used to it.

The red beans are bought in minutes. Angell lifts the bag to test its weight, but instead of turning back, she pivots toward the other side of the market.

Chief: Wrong turn. Home's the other way.

Angell: ...There's somewhere I want to stop by.

She doesn't stop until you've walked past a few streets. In front of you is a cleared, abandoned lot. Three sides are walled off, the faint outline of an old foundation remains on the ground, and weeds poke out sparsely through the cracks.

Angell: This was the place.

You stare at it for a moment, and slowly, it clicks. You start to realize what this place used to be.

**【Advance】**

- ...Is this where that diner was?

**end**

Angell brought this place up to you once before. There used to be a little eatery here that she frequented all the time, and she'd always order the red bean soup.

Angell: Yeah. The door was right over there.

Angell points down at her feet, where the concrete seems a little lighter than the rest of the ground, looking like something used to sit right there.

Angell: The owner didn't know me. He just knew I ordered red bean soup every single time, and he'd always throw in an extra scoop of beans for me.

Angell: Sitting here, sometimes I'd forget what I did for a living. It felt like... I was just a regular customer coming in for a bowl of red bean soup.

Angell: I couldn't really taste much back then, but I still remembered this place... If only those thugs weren't around.

Angell pulls the thermos from her bag, unscrews the lid, and slowly pours the red bean soup onto that patch of bare ground.

Halfway through, she pauses.

Angell: This is for you. Thank you for having me back then. I'll take good care of the goldfish.

The rest of it, she pours out little by little as well.

Angell: ...And this is for the old me.

The red bean soup sinks into the soil, sending up a faint wisp of steam that vanishes almost immediately.

By the time you both head back, evening has already settled in.

The setting sun drags your shadows out long behind you. Angell carries that bag of red beans, walking even more slowly than she usually does.

So you slow down your pace, matching her step for step.

## ANGELL-005 As Warmth Lingers

Your leave hits its deadline today. Right on cue, the terminal in front of you starts ringing constantly, as if taking revenge for being ignored all week.

You dismiss the alerts out of habit, but your eyes catch the fridge. The original note you left Angell is still taped there, now surrounded by a whole collection she added later.

It's completely useless by now. You reach up to tear it down, but the moment your hand touches the paper, Angell speaks up from behind you.

Angell: Leave it.

Your hand pauses, and you turn around to look at her.

Angell: ...You gave it to me. It's mine now.

On the night before you leave, she cooks another pot of red bean soup.

When the soup hits the table, you take one sip and just keep going, finishing it spoonful after spoonful. Better than any compliment, cleaning the bowl shows her exactly how you feel.

Of course, she doesn't ask if the soup tastes good. Looking down at the empty bowl, she already knows the answer.

Early the next morning, you change into your shoes at the entryway. Angell leans against the far wall of the corridor, arms crossed, watching you.

Chief: I'm off. Hard to say when I'll be able to drop by again.

Angell: Hard to say if I'll still be here next time, either.

You pull the door open, letting the morning sunlight flood in and spread out across the floor.

Footsteps echo from behind you. You turn around to find Angell walking up, pressing her familiar thermos into your hand.

Angell: Red bean soup. For the road.

Chief: ...Thanks.

The thermos is freshly filled and slightly hot to the touch. You tuck it inside your coat, holding it securely.

**【Advance】**

- Remember when I said I hoped you'd try living like a normal person?

**end**

She stands with her arms crossed, waiting for you to finish.

Chief: You know... an ordinary life is pretty much just like this.

Angell: ...Is it?

Angell pauses for a moment, then lets out a soft, rare laugh.

Angell: Thanks... but I won't change who I am just for this.

Angell: Still, having one more thing I can actually do on my own... that's good enough for me.

You turn to leave, but something else crosses your mind.

**【Advance】**

- Oh, about that key...

**end**

Angell: Keep it. How else are you going to get in for red bean soup next time?

Angell gives you a subtle nod and steps back into the safe house.

You wave at her, then turn and walk away. As the door clicks shut behind you, the quiet settles right back in.

Sunlight slants in through the gap in the curtains—the ones she pulled open herself—and falls across the fish bowl.

A pair of headphones rests on the couch's armrest, playing that familiar line: There is no hope, there is no soar.

Angell stands by the window, watching your back grow smaller and smaller until you finally disappear around the corner.

Angell: ...Stay safe out there.

Her voice is barely a breath, lost to the quiet room.

Angell: I don't know when you'll be back.

Angell lowers her eyes, looking down at her own hand.

A faint trace of warmth still lingers in her palm. Slowly, she curls her fingers inward, holding onto what little heat remains.

Angell: But this kind of warmth... is just right.
