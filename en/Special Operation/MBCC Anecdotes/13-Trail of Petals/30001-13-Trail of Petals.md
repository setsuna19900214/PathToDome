# MBCC Anecdotes｜Trail of Petals

## SID-1801 Colorless Garden

Bureau Corridor

A hush finally settles over the Bureau's corridor after a long day of activity. As your work is wrapping up, you exhale the day's tension and set your sights on the Recreation Area for some well-earned rest.

Chief: Finally managed to carve out a few days off. Now, how to spend them... Something low-key? A nature escape? Honestly, just crashing at home for three days straight sounds like a solid recharge plan. I'm definitely keeping that on the table.

At that moment, a soft voice curls around the corner at the corridor's end, almost as if it has heard your unspoken thoughts and is answering back.

???: Spring is in full bloom. Why not let your heart take a little trip? Good evening, dear listeners, and welcome to your personal late-night sanctuary. With the holidays upon us, have you made any travel plans yet?

Chief: Hm?

Eleven: Perhaps a quiet stroll through the city streets, letting the rhythm of everyday life wash over you. Or a walk through the hills, where you can breathe in the scent of fresh earth and open air. Or maybe just nestling in at home with your loved ones. All of them are beautiful ways to slow down and savor a rare, unhurried moment.

Eleven: Our new feature, "Come Away With Me," has been blessed with so many heartfelt letters from devoted listeners. You've shared such thoughtful tips and hidden gems with us. Today, I'd love to introduce a nature spot that truly deserves a place on your list.

Eleven: This little treasure sits just on the city's edge. It's not only a breathtaking garden in its own right, but also the real-life inspiration behind *The Enchanted Garden*, the illustrated fairy tale book that's taken Eastside by storm.

Eleven: I'm sure many of you have already read the book. Whether you fell in love with the charming little animals, the winding paths, the wildflowers, or those shafts of light dancing through the leaves, you'll find every last detail waiting for you here, alive and real.

Eleven: So... that's the end of it. What do you all think?

Eleven lowers the script with a touch of reluctance, her read-through complete, and turns her gaze to the little ones she's roped in as test listeners for her new feature.

EMP: That was sooo good! Bravo!!!

Hella: Whoa! Eleven, you sound so pretty and, like, super fancy! Hey, can you write my essay for tomorrow's exam? If I have to copy off EMP, I'm totally screwed...

Hecate: Your voice is gentle. It pulls you in... I think your listeners will really like it.

Hypatia: It was lovely. Though... does DisCity actually have a place like that? Since I arrived, I've only ever seen the urban landscape. Nothing quite like what you described.

EMP: Oh, you asked the right person! This picture book went crazy viral online, and the location totally blew up with it! So many people have already been there. Sooo... what if we sneak out tomorrow and go check it out ourselves?

Hecate: We can't... There's an exam at the Syndicate Academy tomorrow, and I need to make sure Hella studies. If she fails again, they'll call Chief in. We shouldn't make more trouble for Chief.

Hella: Ugh, it's just a dumb exam! I get the same grade no matter what anyway! You know who I am? I'm the mighty Hella! You really think some stupid test can hold me down? I'm skipping it, end of sto—OW!

Her grand declaration barely out of her mouth, a flick lands squarely on her forehead. Hella winces from the sting and looks up, only to find the culprit—you—staring back at her with a perfectly blank expression.

Chief: Slacking off on your studies and now you want to skip exams too?

Hella: I-I just wanna go out and do something fun, okay?! The weather's so nice outside. Of course I wanna go see it!

At the sight of the group tumbling into a mess of laughter, a giggle escapes Hypa despite herself, and she glances over at Eleven.

Hypatia: And what about you? If you really had the chance, would you want to go see it?

Eleven: ...I would. Talking about these places on air, sharing them with everyone, but never having seen them with my own eyes... I always worry the feeling behind the words isn't quite real.

Chief: If it bothers you that much, why not go see it for yourself?

Everyone turns to look at you.

Chief: Come on. Eleven, Hypa, you too. Let me show you what DisCity looks like this time of year.

Hella: Then... I'm coming too!

Chief: Don't you have an exam tomorrow? What's this, O mighty Lady Hella, planning to bring home another big fat zero?

Hella puffs up her cheeks, brimming with sulky displeasure.

Hella: You big dummy! Won't even let me have one day off for a trip. You're no fun at all!

You raise an eyebrow at her.

Chief: Oh, I was going to say... if you guys pass tomorrow, I'd bring you along as a reward. But since I'm such a big no-fun dummy, never mind then...

Hella: Huh—wha—wait!! What did you just say?! Hold on, hold on, hold on—don't walk away! You mean it?! Hey! A grown-up can't just say something and take it back—!

But... the renowned garden that greets you upon arrival is a far cry from anything you had pictured.

Gone is the jewel-bright lake from the fairy tale book. In its place sprawls a bog of blackened water, its stench thick and clinging as its viscous pollution oozes into every corner of the forest clearing.

Plants lie dead in wide, rotting patches; animals have fled in droves... You meet the eyes of the two Sinners, each face mirroring the same grim realization: Mania.

Chief: What on earth happened here?

Eleven: I remember... some of the listener letters mentioned that *The Enchanted Garden* used to have a very special quality to it. Quiet, and warm. People really loved that feeling.

Eleven: But recently, some listeners have been saying the picture book has changed. The art has grown rough, and the stories feel hollow... Now it all makes sense. The scenery here was being corrupted all along.

Eleven: No wonder the listeners were in such low spirits those days. They must have been affected by Mania as well. But I... I didn't know how to comfort them back then.

Eleven lets out a sigh. Hypatia, however, wears a look of earnest contemplation, as if something in Eleven's words has sparked a flicker of inspiration.

Hypatia: Perhaps... this is connected to the garden's current state. It could be that people's negative emotions were projected outward, triggering a Mania overflow. One that not only corrupted the picture book, but bled further into reality.

You're still mulling over her analysis when a startled cry from Hypatia breaks the silence.

Hypatia: Chief, look! Over there—

You follow her gaze. The undergrowth is being steadily corroded by waves of Mania corruption surging forth, and the source spewing all that contamination outward is a fairy-tale picture book, radiating an eerie, unsettling aura.

???: Quack—

Chief: What is that?!

Was that... a duck quacking? You eye the book with suspicion. Just as you crouch down to examine it more closely, a pink ball comes rocketing out from its pages at tremendous speed and hits you square in the forehead.

The world spins wildly, and then everything goes dark.

As consciousness slowly seeps back, the crisp sound of quacking reaches your ears. A jolt runs through you. You can actually understand what the duck is saying?

???: ...Quack... Quack quack quack... (Oh no, did I just knock all three of them out?)

*This has to be a dream... how else could I understand a duck?* You close your eyes and try to slip back into the dream.

???: Quack quack quack quack... Quack quack quack... (Why won't they wake up... Maybe I should try bonking them again...)

Before the duckling can take another shot at your forehead, you struggle to your feet.

Chief: It's okay. It's okay. I'm perfectly capable of waking up on my own, thank you.

You brush the dust from your clothes, rise to your feet, and take in your surroundings. The world has transformed into the pages of a living picture book.

Overhead stretches a sky of soft blue drawing paper, dotted with cotton-ball clouds. The trees are lush with the saturated greens of watercolor brushstrokes, and the windmills carry the folded creases of origami... but at your feet, the flowers lie wilted and shriveled, a stark note of decay in an otherwise painted paradise.

Before you stands, prim and proper, a... duck fairy? Its wings flutter as it quacks at you with great insistence. Your companions groan back to consciousness beside you, and before long, the three of you are locked in a bewildered staring contest with a very determined duck.

Eleven: Where are we? Why do we look like cartoon characters? And this little duck...

Hypatia: Our whole style has changed. We look just like cartoon characters from a book... Could it be that we've fallen into that corrupted picture book?

Hypatia: Was it this duck's doing?

Realizing it's under suspicion, the duck hurriedly lets out a flurry of quacks to proclaim its innocence.

Chief: Easy now. I don't think it means any harm. It's just talking a mile a minute. We can barely catch a word... Slow down a little. Something about... the garden? And... picture book?

Thrilled that you can actually understand it, the duck grows even more excited. It strains to drag out a hefty book and, using its flat little bill, flips it open before you page by page.

*Once upon a time, there was a picture book called The Enchanted Garden. Every child who read it and gazed upon its illustrations couldn't help but say: "I wish I could play in that garden someday!" Quack!*

*And from those very wishes, quack—the little duck fairy was born. The more kindhearted wishes people made, the stronger its magic grew. It watched over the world inside the book, and it watched over the real garden too, helping every living thing bloom brighter and more beautiful. Quack!*

*But one day, a book was tainted by something called Mania. The corruption seeped slowly into the fairy tale world. Plants withered and died in great numbers, and worst of all, quack... the little duck fairy's magic began to fade, little by little.*

*It could no longer nurture the flowers, nor bring forth the spring. The garden would never again be what it once was, with every bloom unfurled in full splendor. The readers who came found only disappointment, and little by little, the Mania, driven by that disappointment and malice, fed on their despair and grew ever stronger. Quack...*

*If the Mania at the heart of the fairy tale world cannot be purged, the corruption will spill over and destroy the garden in reality as well. And people may... lose the garden's spring forever. Quack.*

Chief: So that's why you knocked us into the book? You want us to help you purge the corruption from the fairy tale world?

The duck fairy bobs its head in an eager flurry of nods, its beady little eyes shimmering with a watery, pleading light as they meet yours.

Chief: Alright. For the sake of the real world, I'll do everything I can to help you purge the Mania from this storybook.

Duck Fairy: Quack! (Thank you!!!)

## SID-1802 Magic Named Spring

Duck Fairy: Quack quack, quack quack quack... Quack quack quack!!! (The corruption is gone, the flowers are blooming... our fairy tale book is back to the way it was!!! It's like magic!)

The little duck fairy raises a wand wrapped by fresh flowers, beats its wings, and lifts into the air. With a gentle wave of the wand, you sense something unusual gathering in the air around it.

Duck Fairy: Quack quack, quack, quack quack, quack quack quack! (Thank you, humans. And now, it's time to show you what the duck fairy's magic can do!)

With a light tap against the paper-like sky, the duck fairy traces a vast magic circle out of thin air. A warm radiance washes over you and your companions, and your consciousness begins to blur at the edges.

You know it's time to say goodbye.

Your consciousness slowly returns. The whispers of grass and trees ride in on the breeze, mingling with the gentle sway of wind chimes and the light, airy calls of birds.

Sunlight spills across the lake, setting the water aglitter. Eleven stretches lazily, her eyes still closed, then she opens them, and the sight before her stops her short. Taken aback, she raises a hand to her lips.

Eleven: ...It's breathtaking. Like some kind of spring magic.

Eleven gazes out at the breathtaking lake and the spring scenery, and something seems to stir in her memory.

Eleven: I must have dozed off just now, and I had the most wonderful dream... a dream about magic.

Eleven: You were there, and so was little Hypa. We went to a fairy tale world together and helped the people there save the flowers, bring back the spring... The spring there looked just like this.

Chief: What a coincidence... sounds like we had the same dream. Did you have a good time there?

Eleven: Very much so. Making flowers bloom with your own hands... it felt just like having magic.

You can't help but smile.

Chief: That's because you do have magic. You've always had it.

Eleven: Me?

Chief: You do. If you share the story of how this garden was brought back to life with your listeners, who knows? It just might work a little magic of its own and lift their spirits.

Eleven's eyes go wide.

Eleven: I see... The listeners would be overjoyed to hear this. I'm sure it'll make their hard-earned holiday all the brighter. Right, I'll add this part to the script at once.

Perhaps it's the magic at work. Eleven, who has been walking around with a furrowed brow these past few days, now has a smile softening her features. Just then, you hear Hypatia's voice.

Hypatia: Has a certain Chief perhaps forgotten? There's still a whole group of people waiting on that magic of yours.

You turn around to find Hypatia holding up her terminal toward you. On the other end of the call, a noisy little mouse with purple fur is squeaking and bouncing about as if the world were on fire.

Hella: Hey! Chief, they just handed back the exam papers, and I passed! I'm already here, Chief, so where are you guys? Hypa, come get me! Hypa—

Her bubbly voice fills the air, and you find yourself meeting Hypatia's gaze. A smile passes between you, and in each other's eyes you find the same quiet reassurance: if the one on the other end of that call is happy, then everything you did today was worth it.

You give a soft cough to clear your throat.

Chief: You might not believe this, but I found magic here...

Hella: What? ...Magic?

You shoot Hypa a meaningful look, then deliberately keep Hella in suspense with a teasing pause.

Chief: As for what kind of magic, well... you'll just have to come see for yourself.

Hella: What the heck?! You totally dodged my question! You're the worst! Hypa, you too! Ugh, I'm done with you both... Just you wait, I'm on my way!

Hella's indignant voice crackles through the call, and you and Hypatia can't help but burst into laughter.

Hypatia: Eleven really got it right with that line... "Spring is in full bloom. Why not let your heart take a little trip?" Walking quietly by the water here... it feels like something inside you has been washed clean.

Hypatia: Come on, let's go get her! The flowers are in full bloom. Piling into the car with friends for a spring outing is something I've always wanted to try.

Sunlight ripples across your shoulders like liquid gold, and a breeze stirs the storybook left beside the bench.

With a soft, papery sigh, its fragile pages flutter open and come to rest at the very end, where the storybook's words offered to spring await.

*Spring returns to the land. The withered fields grow green again, blossoms push through the soil, and hope takes root in every heart. They all hold this truth close: spring will always come.*

*And everything they share in the season of spring... that, they call magic.*
