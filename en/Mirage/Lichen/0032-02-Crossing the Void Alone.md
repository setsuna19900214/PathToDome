# Crossing the Void Alone

The immortals gaze upon the sea of suffering from the clouds, while all souls drift like boats unmoored.

Crossing the Void Alone II

## AZ-10001 Crossing the Void Alone

### Lichen 2- Start

Tinkle...

High on the mountain, inside a crumbling temple, the young girl stirs before the statue of the Celestial Master. She rubs her eyes, still thick with sleep, and calls out, her voice soft and unguarded:

Girl: Shifu...?

Nothing but emptiness. No voice returns her call.

She blinks, more awake now, and pushes herself up from the meditation cushion. As she rises, her eyes catch on something by her feet—the Pellucid Bell, knocked over in her sleep. So that was the chime that woke her.

Dusk had settled while she dreamed. The young girl stands still for a long moment. Then, without a word, she bends and picks up the bell. She wipes the dust from its surface, tucks it carefully into her robe, and turns toward the path leading down.

### Lichen 2- Finish

The vegetable patch at the foot of the mountain marks the end of her journey.

After the spring flood, no one has come to clear the debris and rocks. Vehicles can go no farther than this.

Most of the Daoists have already been sent home. Only Daowei and one senior disciple remain, seeing things through to the end. They have set up a makeshift shelter by the parking area, loading boxes onto the truck.

Three days of hauling things up and down the mountain with the girl pitching in the whole time, and by the third evening, they have finally moved nearly everything.

The sun has fully set. The young Daoist lights the incandescent bulb inside the shelter, its faint buzz drawing swarms of insects into its glow. Nearby, Daowei takes inventory of the items loaded onto the truck.

Hearing her footsteps, Daowei looks up and asks offhandedly:

Daowei: Everything checked? Nothing left behind?

Girl: Mm.

Daowei: What took you so long?

Girl: I went up to the peak.

Daowei pauses for a moment, then lowers her head and goes back to work without another word.

The girl doesn't stand idle either. She joins the senior disciple by the truck, helping to load whatever she can carry. Though young and not particularly strong, every pair of hands helps, no matter how small.

They work in silence for a while, the only sounds the shuffle of feet and the thud of boxes. Then, suddenly, the girl speaks:

Girl: Say, if Shifu comes back one day and sees the whole temple emptied out... What kind of face do you think she'd make?

At this, the senior disciple hurries to set down his box. He turns to face the girl, his expression drawn tight. His mouth opens, then closes. For a long moment, he struggles, but no words come.

Young Daoist Priest: ...

Daowei: Call the police... That's what anyone would do.

Girl: There isn't a single police station for miles around.

Daowei: Then what else could she do? Sit on the ground and cry? Perform a divination right there to figure out where everything went?

Girl: That actually sounds exactly like her!

She sets down the bedding she's been carrying and laughs.

Girl: But you know what I think? I think Shifu probably ascended to immortality. She'll come back, breathe one breath, and poof—the whole temple turns into a celestial dwelling. Auspicious clouds everywhere. White cranes circling...

Daowei: Which Daoist scripture describes an immortal spell that convenient?

Girl: I'm just guessing! Besides, if mortals could understand the ways of immortals that easily, wouldn't that be kind of... boring?

Daowei: What you're describing doesn't sound like any proper Daoist deity...

Girl: So? You better believe it! Everyone online says Shifu is a banished immortal. Sent down by Heaven to warn us about the apocalypse...

Daowei: ...That's not what they said back then.

The moment the words leave Daowei's lips, the air turns cold.

Because it is true. Daoxuan's actions back then had been anything but immortal-like.

Pellucid Bell in hand, she had rushed into the crowd and proclaimed her doomsday prophecy for all to hear. When they tried to restrain her, she thrashed like one possessed, babbling wildly that this was a warning, that they had to listen.

She had looked every bit the hysteric.

Outsiders called her a disgrace to Daoism. The head priest, unable to withstand the public outcry, expelled her from the sect. Even after she returned to the mountain, she showed no signs of recovery. Within days, she was gone.

And then the star fell. The world descended into chaos. It took over a year of devastation before people finally came to their senses—before they began to revere the very woman they had cast out, calling her now "prophet."

Young Daoist Priest: Well... times change. And Shifu always did have real talent. Who knows, maybe she really did... you know. Attain immortality.

Seeing them both fall silent, the young priest steps in to smooth things over.

Young Daoist Priest: Anyway! Everything's loaded. We should get moving... Looks like rain again.

At the mention of rain, a shared unease settles over them. There have been three floods already this year, three too many. No one dares take chances anymore.

Daowei: Let's go.

Doors slam shut. The engine coughs to life, and the farm pickup truck lurches forward, belching black smoke as it begins its journey.

Years have passed since the day she slept atop the Celestial Master statue, but to Daowei and the others, Li Xianling is still just a child.

When she sees her growing drowsy from the bumpy ride, Daowei pulls out a blanket and drapes it carefully over her. The girl breathes in, catching the lingering scent of temple incense. In that familiar smell, a realization cuts through her haze: once they leave, she might never come back.

The thought jolts her awake. She rolls down the window and leans her head out, letting the wind whip at her hair as she gazes back at the mountains and the land receding behind her, as if she could hold onto them with her eyes alone.

Young Daoist Priest: ...Actually. I believe it too. That Shifu will come back.

The girl settles back into her seat. In all her years, she has never heard such words from her taciturn senior.

Young Daoist Priest: Immortals... they always show compassion for the living. That's what they say. They wouldn't just stand by while the world falls apart. And Shifu, she...

Young Daoist Priest: None of us has ever forgotten her. Not really...

The mountain road is treacherous at night. Throughout his words, her senior keeps his eyes fixed ahead, never once glancing her way. Yet she knows that this is no empty comfort, no mere words meant to soothe.

Since Daoxuan vanished, it is the first time she has felt something settle quietly inside her.

Soon, drowsiness rides in on that fragile calm and washes over her once more. This time, she surrenders to it and lets herself drift.

### Lichen 2-Dialogue1

Though autumn is young, winter arrives uninvited. The young girl clutches her cyan robe at the collar, but the mountain mist finds its way in, leaching the heat from her bones.

She remembers the day her shifu read the heavens and spoke of the "white ray through the sun." Every omen since has proven true—above among the stars, and below among the dust of men.

Now chaos has truly come, just as foretold.

Fallen leaves lie thick and unswept on the stone steps. The young girl pays them no mind, hurrying down the mountain without a glance.

### Lichen 2-Dialogue2

Darkness fills the Shangqing Palace now. No eternal flames flicker before the altar; they died long ago. The main hall stands hollow and bare, stripped of everything useful.

The meditation cushions where they once sat. The candle holders that caught the wax. The gauze curtains that softened the light. All gone. And the young boy too. His father, the young girl's grandfather, took him months ago, seeking safety in the city's larger temple.

In the darkness, only a few things remain: a pile of discarded clutter in the corner, the donation box still standing at the hall's center, and the statue of the Grandmaster with its features swallowed by shadow.

The young girl picks through the clutter, sifting patiently until her fingers find a tattered Daoist robe, worn thin but whole. She pulls it over her shoulders.

At least now she won't freeze on the way down.

### Lichen 2-Dialogue3

She steps through the temple gate. Far below, at the mountain's foot, faint glimmers of light flicker in the growing dark.

Girl: They must be starting to worry...

She starts down, then pauses. The stairway unfolds before her, endless stone steps vanishing into the dark below. Without worshippers to warm it, the path lies cold and still, shade pooling in every corner. An eerie thing, alone.

Girl: I'll take the shorter path.

### Lichen 2-Dialogue4

The Immortal's Grotto stands unchanged, exactly as it was a year ago.

Outside, the cold bites deep, but here within the stone walls, a surprising warmth lingers. Yet the silence presses close: no birdsong, no animal calls, nothing alive but only the faint, steady whisper of trickling water somewhere in the depths.

When her shifu first vanished, the Daoists combed the mountain end to end, time and again, yet every last one of them passed over the Immortal's Grotto without a word.

Only Li Xianling, the senior disciple under her direct tutelage, knew the truth: that Master Daoxuan, for all her ethereal grace, had a habit of stealing away to the grotto whenever something weighed on her heart.

For over a month, the young girl came here every single day. She would wait, hoping her shifu might emerge from some hidden corner like an immortal stepping out of legend, declaring that she had at last unraveled the secrets of heaven. And then they would walk back to the temple together, and everything would be as it was—peaceful, whole.

But that day never came. When the calamity struck, the mountains grew treacherous. Daowei, ever fretful, forbade her from wandering far. And so the grotto saw her no more.

And truth be told, this place is hardly pleasant. Visiting now and then for a change of scenery is one thing, but anyone who chooses to live here day in, day out would have to be out of their mind. Even her shifu, that most devoted of Daoists, who has given herself entirely to the pursuit of immortality, would never have made this cave her permanent retreat.

Clearly no place for an officially ordained deity. She might as well have ascended to the heavens sooner, for at least up there, she could enjoy a carefree existence.

Lost in thought, the young girl's foot catches on something—a hard branch, perhaps—and she stumbles, nearly falling.

Girl: Weird... Why in the world would there be a branch all the way in here?

She peers down into the darkness, but whatever it is refuses to reveal itself. After a moment, she gives up and moves on.

### Lichen 2-Dialogue5

After leaving the Immortal's Grotto, the young girl walks for one more mile until a familiar shape emerges. It's the Earth Guardian shrine, small and weathered, just as she remembers.

Girl: Phew. Okay, good. At least I'm not lost.

Her ankle still throbs from twisting it back in the grotto. She brushes the dust from a flat stone beside the shrine and lowers herself onto it, wincing slightly.

As she rubs the ache from her foot, she turns to look at the old "acquaintance" within.

The small shrine stands empty of devotion. No offerings sit before the altar. Even the red silk ribbon that had once been tied around the clay figurine's neck is gone.

Girl: Business is slow, huh, old man?

She reaches into her satchel as she talks, rummaging absently, then stops herself, thinking better of it.

Girl: ...Nah. What's the point?

Girl: You know, the folks from Houshan Village used to stuff you full of offerings all the time. And what did you ever do for them? Just sat there pocketing the benefits.

Girl: This is why you've gotta be picky about which gods you worship...

Having rested enough, the young girl rises to her feet.

Girl: Anyway. You'll survive.

### Lichen 2-Dialogue6

She follows the mountain trail out of the woods and finds herself standing amid a stretch of ruins.

This is all that remains of Houshan Village.

In the second year after the prophecy of the white ray through the sun, spring arrived not with gentle rains but with a furious downpour. By the next day, flash floods came roaring down the mountain without warning. The Daoists had rushed down the night before, rousing villagers from their beds, and thanks to them, no lives were lost.

But the water took their homes anyway.

She passes the rubble that had once been the "Southern Heavenly Gate," the very spot where "Immortal Lady Li" had loved. The young girl trudges on, her steps uneven as she picks her way through what remains of Houshan Village.

Then, passing a particular house—or what is left of it—she stops.

Girl: ...

Granny Liu never saw the flood. She had passed away the winter before.

Her funeral was a grand affair, where gongs and drums echoed across the village, and children and grandchildren gathered to send her off. In the end, she kept her dignity. In the end, she got her due.

Dog: Whine...

Girl: ...Buddy?

Following the sound of whimpering, she finds a filthy, emaciated dog crouched beside the ruined house.

The creature is so gaunt it hardly looks like a dog at all, ribs pressing against matted fur, its original color long since swallowed by dirt and neglect. And yet, the young girl knows it at once.

Girl: What are you doing here? Didn't Uncle Wang take you with them to the city?

Dog: Whiiine...

Girl: Why am I even asking you... It's not like a dog can answer.

Buddy whimpers softly, a far cry from the vigorous barking of before. Its eyes roll once, then it flops down with a heavy sigh, listless and spent. The young girl might as well be invisible.

Girl: Ugh, just my luck. I can't stand seeing anyone... or any dog... suffer.

The young girl reaches into her bundle and pulls out an unleavened flatbread, tearing it in half. She hesitates for a moment, then changes her mind and tosses both pieces to the dog.

Buddy wastes no time on pleasantries. It falls upon the flatbread with a whimper, tearing into it as if the bread had personally wronged it.

She watches for a while as Buddy wolfs down its meal. The large flatbread is too big for the creature to finish quickly. But she can't keep Daowei and the others waiting any longer.

Girl: Goodbye, Buddy.

As she speaks, the young girl casts one last look at Houshan Village.

Girl: Goodbye... Goodbye.
