# Crossing the Void Alone

The immortals gaze upon the sea of suffering from the clouds, while all souls drift like boats unmoored.

I shall break apart these mortal bones, forge an imaginary vessel to cross this sea.

## AZ-10001 Crossing the Void Alone

### Lichen 4- Start

A full year has passed since "the immortal's revelation" swept through the land.

It happened all at once. People from across the nation, strangers to one another, all reported the same dream. In it, Celestial Master Daoxuan appeared and pointed west, toward Maria City, where the Perishing Star fell. There, she told them, they would find a way to quell the tide of calamities.

Scholars called it mass hysteria, a sensation unworthy of serious consideration. But such explanations failed to convince the public.

The proof lay in what followed: reverence for Celestial Master Daoxuan only deepened, spreading like roots through dry earth. What began as scattered folk belief gradually cohered into something larger—organized movements, gathering momentum.

And Lichen, as the Celestial Master's direct disciple, has become the spiritual leader for most who followed.

### Lichen 4- Finish

Assistant: Master Lichen... is this really necessary? All of this?

At the dock, the last crate groans as it's hoisted aboard. Lichen watches it swing through the air, then nods without hesitation.

Lichen: The seas are unpredictable these days. Better to have too much than too little.

Lichen: Besides, once I reach the West and meet with the foreign collaborators, who knows if we'll even see eye to eye? The more I bring, the more cards I hold.

Assistant: You know that's not what I'm asking.

Lichen: ...

Lichen lifts her gaze to the sky.

Lichen: You're asking if it's worth it. Selling everything. Burning the whole house down to put out a world on fire?

Assistant: Mm. When you might never return. For a dream, is it really worth...?

Lichen: How would I know unless I go see for myself?

Lichen looks at her assistant, a young woman about her own age, and gives her head a gentle tap.

Lichen: You're just like your brother, fussing over everything. Chew on every little worry until it's worn thin.

Lichen: Those who cling to silver and silk never find the Dao. Shifu's spirit shows up every now and then. Have you ever seen her ask anyone for coins?

Assistant: ...

Lichen's words leave the assistant stunned, tangled in her own logic. She has no comeback, none at all.

How can someone so stubborn over trifles, so relentless in haggling, become so terrifyingly "carefree" when the stakes are this high? The assistant stares at her, thoroughly lost.

Daowei: Li—! Xian—! Ling—!

Their talk is broken by a shrill shout. Following the sound, they spot two figures making their way over: a Daoist priest of about forty and a priestess a decade older, both laden with bags, waving as they draw near.

Daowei: LI XIANLING!

Lichen: Senior Brother...

Lichen glances at her senior brother, walking just ahead. He catches her look and responds with a helpless purse of his lips.

Knowing that he must have failed to stop the priestess, Lichen sighs inwardly. When she faces Daowei, her smile is brave, but her voice carries the weight of acceptance.

Lichen: Priestess Daowei, please, there are people around. Could you not call me by my birth name? I do have a Daoist name, you know...

Daowei: Daoist name? What Daoist name? You're not even ordained!

Daowei, ever the impatient one, glances at the ship before resuming her scolding:

Daowei: Honestly, you! Making a decision this big without a word to me or your senior brother...

Lichen: I wrote you a letter—

Daowei: By the time your letter arrived, the whole world already knew you were setting sail!

Lichen: ...

Daowei: Never a moment's peace, not since you were this high. Disgracing the ancestors, defying the masters... is there anything you wouldn't dare do?

Lichen: That's a bit much... When have I ever disgraced the ancestors?

Daowei: Remind me, who climbed up the Celestial Master's statue and took a nap on his head?

Daowei: No, this won't do. I'm coming with you. If there's no one to keep an eye on you, who knows what nonsense you'll get up to next...

Lichen: Please don't! If something happened to you, how would I explain it to Shifu?

Daowei: Don't talk like that! "Explain it to Shifu"—as if anything's going to happen—

At these words, the group exchanges glances and falls silent. After a moment, Lichen sidles up to Daowei and asks with a smile:

Lichen: So you don't believe Shifu ascended to immortality either?

Daowei: Believe or not... I just can't picture that cranky old woman sitting up there as equals with the Grandmasters...

Lichen: Then... do you believe immortals exist at all?

Daowei: ...

The Daoist priestess scrunches her face and shrinks back, all her usual fire extinguished. For a long moment, she says nothing. When at last she speaks, her voice comes soft.

Daowei: I am a practitioner of the Dao. Of course, I believe immortals exist.

Daowei: According to the scriptures... the immortals I know, they roam freely between heaven and earth. They don't trouble themselves with mortal affairs...

Daowei turns to look back at the dock, and at the mortals below, toiling and struggling simply to get by. Then, she lets out a sigh.

Daowei: But what use are immortals like that to people like them?

Lichen: That's why I'm going to find one who is useful. And bring them back.

Daowei: ...You?!

Lichen: I refuse to believe every immortal is a cold, unfeeling lump of jade. Heaven and earth are vast, with countless beings, countless paths. Surely at least one or two of them are willing to watch over mortals?

For a hundred years, the mountain temple burned incense. If immortals were real, all that smoke, heavy with sorrows and wishes, should have built them a stairway to earth by now.

But the temple fell silent in the end. Its incense and candles guttered out. Not a single Daoist remained on those vast green slopes. Only the Immortal's Grotto was left—an empty shell with no immortal inside—still whimpering in the wind.

Lichen: If I search the whole world and still can't find one...

Lichen: Then I'll make one. Right there, on the spot. And I'll teach them, from the very beginning, to always think of mortals first.

Daowei: Y-y-you... you are completely out of your mind!

Lichen: What does it matter? The world's already on fire. Could my little spark really make things worse?

Daowei: With those half-baked tricks of yours, you dare speak of making immortals? Why don't you just say you'll split heaven and earth apart while you're at it?

Lichen: Then you'll have to lend me the axe first.

Daowei: Heavens above, you're going to be the death of me!

For one sharp instant, the Daoist priestess burns with frustration. Then her shoulders sag. She snatches up her luggage and stalks toward the ship in a huff, the fire already gone.

Lichen: What are you doing?

Daowei: I still can't stop worrying. I'm coming with you!

Lichen: Priestess Daowei! This isn't a pleasure cruise! Have you ever read of anyone in the ancient texts bringing a little old lady along on their voyage to seek immortals...

Lichen: Besides! That one is coming too! If you don't trust me, surely you can trust him?

Daowei: Who? ...Oh! He's coming?!

Lichen: He is. The foremost young prodigy of the orthodox Daoist sect. Direct disciple of the head priest himself. Steady as a mountain, dependable as sunrise, been that way since he was a child.

Daowei: Well... that's true. It's been years since I last saw him. I wonder what he looks like now...

The little old lady lowers her luggage to the ground. Lichen and her assistant hurry over immediately, unwilling to let her lift another thing.

Lichen: Book a room for Priestess Daowei and your brother. Have them settle in here for now.

Lichen: There are still a few days before we set off. Plenty of time to catch up while we wait for him to arrive...

As the sun dips below the horizon, Lichen finishes settling the group and returns to the dock alone.

Lichen: This is how mortals are...

Lichen: Thinking of life. Thinking of those we love. The ones still here. The ones already gone...

The sea breeze brushes against her skin as she draws out the Pellucid Bell and gives it a gentle ring. Its ethereal chime drifts into the air, soft and resonant, and in its wake, her heart grows as calm as the sea stretching out before her.

Lichen: Shifu... I often wonder. Do you think of us, from the other side? The way we think of you?

Lichen: You must do... Otherwise, you wouldn't have run yourself ragged over that last divination like a woman possessed. And you wouldn't still be visiting us in dreams, all these years after...

With her back to the sea, Lichen perches on a stone block and watches the dock come and go—figures moving through the last light of day.

Lichen: When we were children, you taught us two things above all: hold compassion for all beings, and let the Dao follow its nature. The one who attains the Dao, you said, should follow the natural order and protect every living thing...

Lichen: But how exactly does the Dao we've been seeking save anyone?

Lichen: It hasn't made me an immortal who can summon food from thin air. It hasn't made me invulnerable, like the heroes on television everyone loves...

She strokes the Pellucid Bell and murmurs, her voice so soft that only she can hear:

Lichen: So then... I'll use what I have.

Lichen: I'll use my own strength... to reshape Dao.

### Lichen 4-Dialogue1

Lichen moves the antique furniture out of the reception room piece by piece—heirlooms passed down through generations. She wipes the dust from each surface herself, then photographs every item against a white backdrop.

Years of history lie settled in the wood grain. Each nick along the table corners seems to speak to her: This is the dignity befitting the Abode of the Immortals.

### Lichen 4-Dialogue2

The bookshelves grow emptier by the day. Lichen sorts her stacks of rare texts with care: canonical scriptures here, miscellaneous arts there, records of the strange in their own pile. Even the hand-copied talisman patterns, long tucked between yellowing pages, are retrieved and set aside.

These books have traveled with her through years of calamity, through wastelands where no signal reached, through slungs thick with miasma. More than once, they have saved her life. But now, they deserve a better home.

### Lichen 4-Dialogue3

In her office, Lichen signs and stamps, transfers and liquidates. Every asset under her name is accounted for, down to the last copper coin.

### Lichen 4-Dialogue4

When the final transfer document is filed, her assistant places the completed accounts before her and steps back.

Assistant: The supplies are ready, Master Lichen. The ship's been arranged, but...

Lichen nods and hands over a car key.

Lichen: Then let's go. I want to see the dock myself. This car's getting traded in soon anyway.

The budget is still short. Lichen spreads the checklist across the table where red circles mark item after item: a ship, fuel, backup communications, water purification equipment... Nothing that can be cut.

Her gaze drifts across the room, lingering on the few prized possessions she hasn't yet parted with. Her fingertips tap lightly against the table, a quiet rhythm against the silence.
