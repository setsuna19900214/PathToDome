# Crossing the Void Alone

The immortals gaze upon the sea of suffering from the clouds, while all souls drift like boats unmoored.

## AZ-10001 Crossing the Void Alone

### Lichen 3- Start

??: ...This just in... days of torrential rain... levee breach... causing... major damage across... several regions...

The television flickers, its news broadcast cutting in and out. At the desk, a woman in a Daoist robe stirs. Her head had been resting on her folded arms; now she blinks groggily, surfacing from sleep.

Female Daoist: After all these years... why a dream about my childhood, and why now?

The Daoist priestess has little time to dwell on it. Her attention is seized by the alarming images flashing across the television screen.

Television: Current number of victims...... over one hundred thousand... property damage estimated at...... rescue teams have been dispatched to......

The Daoist priestess hurries over to the television and gives it a few firm smacks.

The result is predictable. What little picture and sound had lingered vanished entirely, leaving nothing but static snow swirling across the expensive LCD screen.

Female Daoist: ...

In these times, catastrophes worsen with each passing year. Communication signals, once reliable, grow fitful and thin.

Humanity's living space dwindles day by day. Survivors crowd into the few remaining major cities, huddling together like islands cut off from one another in a vast and rising sea.

The television shows no sign of recovering. The Daoist priestess gives up and switches it off in silence.

Just then, a voice calls out from beyond the door.

???: Master Lichen? Master Lichen?

It's her personal assistant. The Daoist priestess quickly tidies her hair that's still slightly disheveled from her nap and clears her throat.

Female Daoist: Come in.

The door swings open, and a female assistant around her age peeks inside.

Assistant: Master Lichen, Practitioner Lin has arrived.

Female Daoist: Ah... right. I almost forgot.

Female Daoist: Have her wait just a moment. I'll be there shortly.

Assistant: Got it.

The moment the assistant closes the door, the Daoist priestess rises from her seat. Practitioner Lin is a major patron, so this meeting is not to be taken lightly.

At the very least, she must maintain the air of an immortal's disciple.

### Lichen 3- Finish

After seeing Lin off, the Daoist slumps back into her chair. All traces of her ethereal demeanor drain away in an instant.

For a long while, she sits staring at the calligraphy hanging in the reception room. It is penned by none other than the presiding Daoist priest of her sect. The man has always refused to grant her an ordination certificate, yet when asked to write this piece, he did not hesitate.

Four bold words stare back at her:

ABODE

OF

THE

IMMORTALS

Female Daoist: Hehe...

The Daoist lets out a snicker.

Her years drifting through the secular world have taught her one thing: there are likely no immortals in this world.

In these end times, only endless calamities and rampant demons remain. If immortals truly existed, how could they stand idly by while the world falls to such ruin?

She has heard rumors—colossal beings capable of moving mountains and filling seas have appeared deep within the calamity zones and across the seas. The ignorant mistake them for deities.

But what good is a deity who offers no protection? They are nothing but demons and monsters.

At this thought, she calls out for her assistant. The young woman enters promptly from outside.

Assistant: Your orders, Master Lichen?

Female Daoist: Take this. Purchase food and winter clothing in the name of the Celestial Master Relief Charity. Refugees from the neighboring city will be here within days. We need to be ready.

Assistant: ...This much?

Female Daoist: Every life we save is worth it. Besides... money has a way of finding its way back to those who spend it well.

Assistant: Understood.

Female Daoist: One more thing. Go to the storeroom and bring out one of the Celestial Master statues. For the next forty-nine days, I'll be in the shantytown at the city's edge for relief work. No visitors...

Once the instructions are given, the assistant leaves without another word. This is exactly why the woman likes her. Just like her brother, she speaks little but works efficiently.

After she leaves, the Daoist quickly grows drowsy. Understandable, really, as today has been exhausting.

She collapses onto the sofa, her gaze drifting to her shifu's statue directly ahead. As she stares at it, the words begin to spill out—a quiet complaint, aimed at stone that cannot answer.

Female Daoist: Look at me. Quite the sight, aren't I?

Female Daoist: You had it easy, didn't you? Leave behind a few cryptic words, ascend to immortality, and just... drift off. While the rest of us are left here, drowning in the mess.

Female Daoist: And what can I do? Play at charity. Run myself ragged. Patch up a few wounds while the city burns. Saving... what? A dozen lives? A hundred? Against thousands lost?

Female Daoist: Shifu... If you're really an immortal now... can't you come back? Just for a moment? You were always the one who cared. The one who couldn't stand to see anyone suffer. Did ascending make you forget?

Female Daoist: At least... at least show me a way...

After staring at the statue for a long while, drowsiness creeps over her again. Putting on a show for a client is more exhausting than climbing a mountain from base to peak.

She curls into her chair, chin dipping toward her chest. Sleep creeps in, and with it, a strange mist that seems to rise from the floor.

—Then the statue opens her eyes.

The gilded clay figure blazes to life, her gaze fierce as burning torches. Slowly, deliberately, a finger emerges from the wide sleeve and points...

West.

### Lichen 3-Dialogue1

The Daoist priestess lifts the Pellucid Bell from her desk, which is the only memento her shifu has left behind, now renowned as the relic of an immortal.

As a keepsake, it holds her memories. As a prop, it burnishes her image. As a tool, it helps her make a living. Its importance cannot be overstated.

For this very reason, she never lets it leave her side.

### Lichen 3-Dialogue2

The bookshelf holds rare texts she has collected over the years.

After leaving the temple, she spent some time in formal education before joining rescue teams, traveling the world while conducting what she called field research.

Along the way, she made a point of gathering records of mystical Daoist arts from every region. Some are orthodox texts of the mystic traditions. Others are folk magic from the mountains and wilderness. The rest are simply supernatural tales whispered in countryside villages.

As the years pass—or perhaps as the catastrophes grow more frequent—the methods recorded in these books prove invaluable. Feng shui, geomancy, divination, warding off evil... She calls upon them all, more than once.

One could say these books deserve the lion's share of credit. For her making a name. For building the livelihood she has today. For becoming the renowned Immortal Master that people now speak of.

That said, she doesn't spend much time reading them anymore. There is a business to run. She only flips through the pages when she encounters a problem that needs solving.

Over time, she has memorized most of their contents. Performing those mystical arts has become second nature.

The Daoist priestess has a good idea why Practitioner Lin is here. And she likely won't need to resort to any of those arts at all.

### Lichen 3-Dialogue3

She tidies her hair, straightens her robes, and adds a touch of makeup. When she looks up, the mirror reflects a woman of refined, otherworldly grace.

Female Daoist: Not bad.

The Daoist priestess studies her reflection and nods, satisfied. For a moment, she lets herself remember how her shifu used to carry herself as she faced worshippers. She assumes that same demeanor now, quiet and measured, and goes to greet her guest.

### Lichen 3-Dialogue4

At the door, she pauses. One last time, she smooths the wrinkles from her hem.

When she steps through, she will no longer be Li Xianling. To the world beyond, she will be Daoist Lichen. Nothing more, nothing less.

She walks to the door and pauses, considering. Then, with quiet resolve, she turns back. The immortal's own disciple must carry herself with proper dignity.

### Lichen 3-Dialogue5

Practitioner Lin: Immortal Master Lichen! How are you?

The moment she steps into the reception room, the woman on the sofa rises at once to greet her.

Practitioner Lin's makeup is exquisite, her robe simple yet elegant; the fabric alone whispers of considerable expense. Rumour has it she is a lay practitioner of the Quanzhen Sect, strict in observing the precepts, faithful to a vegetarian diet. Far more devout than a certain unordained "charlatan" like her.

Female Daoist: My apologies for keeping you waiting, Practitioner Lin.

Female Daoist: But "Immortal Master" is too heavy a title. You are my elder. Let us simply call each other fellow Daoists.

Practitioner Lin: Oh, not at all. As the old saying goes, those who have attained the Dao take precedence...

Practitioner Lin: And truly, if you hadn't warned me to pull out of the neighboring city in time... I'd be counting coins on the street by now.

Practitioner Lin: At your age, such clarity of foresight? You are the living heir of Celestial Master Daoxuan, an immortal walking among us!

Female Daoist: Such praise will be my undoing... What little I can do is but a candle flame beside my shifu's sun.

Female Daoist: If I truly had the sight of an immortal, that city would not have fallen. So many lives. So much ruin.

Practitioner Lin: Ah, but that's exactly it: your heart aches for the world. Such compassion speaks louder than any miracle. Who could hear you and not be moved?

As the flattering words flow from her lips, Practitioner Lin's hand moves toward the cloth bundle at her side.

Practitioner Lin: I've long heard of your charitable work, Master Lichen. Please, take this. A small offering from one who wishes to help.

Female Daoist: My friend, I cannot. You've just withdrawn from the neighboring market; your own affairs must be unsettled. This is not the time for you to give.

Practitioner Lin: Without your warning, my losses would've been tenfold what I've sacrificed.

Practitioner Lin: And besides, am I not a Daoist too? How could I see suffering and do nothing?

Practitioner Lin: You carry the weight of all living things on your shoulders, Master. Please, don't deny us the chance to carry a little too.

Female Daoist: ...

Female Daoist: Then... on behalf of those who suffer in that city, I thank you.

The female Daoist offers a deep bow, but Lin is already on her feet, hurrying to her side to lift her up.

Practitioner Lin: Oh! No, no, please, you mustn't—

The two go back and forth like this for quite some time before finally settling back into their seats.

Lin takes a slow sip of fresh tea, then speaks, her words measured:

Practitioner Lin: Master Lichen... there is another reason I've come today. A small request, if I may...

Female Daoist: Speak freely, my friend.

Practitioner Lin: I have long admired Celestial Master Daoxuan. Every time I visit here and behold her visage, I feel... clarity. Peace. As if my mind has been swept clean.

Lin gazes at the statue of Daoxuan in the reception room, her eyes brimming with longing.

Practitioner Lin: I was wondering... might I be permitted to bring a statue of the Celestial Master home? To honor, and to learn from?

At last, her true purpose reveals itself. The female Daoist suppresses a smile.

What had once been dismissed as the ravings of a madwoman—her shifu's prophecies—have come true, one by one. And so those who had called her hysterical began to whisper instead of a sage. Worship followed. Reverence. Devotion.

Over the years, the name Celestial Master Daoxuan has not faded. It spreads wider, grows more mythical with each telling. And the female Daoist, as the Celestial Master's direct disciple, finds herself sought after by a growing circle of so-called devotees—among them, figures of no small influence in politics and commerce.

With such connections, it takes her only a few years to become a high-level broker. Her business flourishes quite splendidly.

In return for their devotion, she gifts her closest partners small statues of Celestial Master Daoxuan, carved by her own hand.

These are not mere symbols of status. In these strange and supernatural end times, many believe they are divine talismans, objects of protection against the chaos closing in.

Lin's generous contribution today has earned the Daoist's regard. Bestowing upon her the most coveted item in this circle—one never sold, only given—would not be out of the question.

Still, appearances must be kept.

Female Daoist: Crafting a statue of the Celestial Master is no small matter. I must fast for forty-nine days beforehand. No visitors, no distractions. Only then can I preserve my primordial spirit enough to seek my shifu's guidance.

Hearing this, Lin grows visibly anxious.

Practitioner Lin: I understand it's no easy task, but...

Female Daoist: However, in light of your generosity to the afflicted, your sincerity moves me. I believe my shifu would understand...

Female Daoist: Please wait forty-nine days. When the time is complete, I will personally deliver my shifu's statue to your door.

When Lin hears that her wish has been granted, joy lights up her face. She thanks the Daoist over and over, her words tumbling out in a grateful rush.

Practitioner Lin: Oh! Oh, Immortal Master, thank you! Thank you for granting my wish...
