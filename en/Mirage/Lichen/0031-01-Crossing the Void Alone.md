### Lichen 1-Dialogue1

It is the busy farming season, and by late morning, Houshan Village has emptied out, every able hand in the fields. A young girl wanders in, a morning glory stem clamped between her lips, looking as carefree as can be.

In recent years, some new fashion must have caught on in the city. The temple has seen more visitors than ever before, and the village has reaped the rewards. Even the roads have been paved with cement, making travel far easier.

But the girl rather misses the old dirt paths. Back then, she could stand beneath the archway at the village's southern entrance and, with a few good stomps, kick up clouds of dust.

And then she, the great "Immortal Lady Li of the Southern Heavenly Gate," would lead a band of village children—or rather, her celestial soldiers and generals—on a grand expedition.

Grrrumble...

Girl: Ugh, I'm starving... Ptooey.

The girl spits out the morning glory stem, now drained of its sweetness. The nectar has only sharpened her hunger. After all, she skipped breakfast this morning to escape her lesson.

The villagers are kind people; she cannot bring herself to steal food from their homes. Better to head back to the temple kitchen and see if any leftovers remain.

### Lichen 1-Dialogue2

Dog: Woof, woof, woof!

The girl had hoped to slip through the village unnoticed, but a watchdog catches sight of her.

Girl: Shh! It's me, Buddy! You don't know me now?

Dog: Woof, woof, woof, woof, woof!

Irritated, the girl snatches a bamboo pole leaning against the wall and raises it. The moment Buddy spots her stance, he lets out a whimper and scurries off.

Girl: Heh. That's right... Run.

The girl twirls the bamboo pole with a touch of smugness, only to find an elderly woman in the courtyard watching her, an amused smile playing at the corners of her mouth.

Girl: Granny Liu...

Granny Liu: Come here, sweetie.

The old woman has trouble getting around, so she usually spends her days in a wheelchair in the courtyard, soaking up the sun. She is dozing off when the barking startles her awake. The moment she opens her eyes, there is that naughty girl from the Daoist temple up the mountain, brandishing a bamboo pole at her dog.

But the old woman knows the girl would never actually strike. She beckons her over with a warm smile.

The girl shuffles over obediently and lets Granny Liu's weathered hands ruffle the hair bun she so carefully tied this morning.

Granny Liu: Have you eaten yet?

Girl: Not yet...

The girl regrets the words the moment they leave her mouth.

Granny Liu: I've got some fresh buns at home. Take a couple with you.

Girl: No, no, no, I can't just take! Shifu says we're not supposed to accept food from villagers without earning it...

Granny Liu: Look at the time, and what good is a starving little Daoist? By the grace of the immortals, this year's wheat was fat and sweet. Freshly ground and steamed... Mmm, the smell is divine!

The girl swallows hard, but still squirms free from the hand resting on her head.

Girl: But Shifu says, "Master things, but don't let things master you." If I eat your buns, I may want them every day...

Granny Liu: You talk just like those priests up on the mountain. This old granny can't win against all that temple talk... Oh, that reminds me. Daowei came by earlier, looking for you.

Girl: I know! That's why I'm heading back now!

Realizing that someone from the temple might come looking for her at any moment, the girl turns and hurries off, though not before turning to wave goodbye to the old woman.

Girl: Bye, Granny Liu!

The old woman watches the girl's figure until it disappears into the distance, then sighs with a fond smile.

Granny Liu: Kids, huh...

Basking in the warm sunlight, the old woman settles back into her wheelchair. Soon her eyelids droop, and she drifts off to sleep once more.

### Lichen 1-Dialogue3

Once inside the forest, the sunlight gives way to a heavy, moisture-laden chill. After walking a mile or two, the girl finds herself rubbing her arms for warmth.

The cold and damp air only makes her stomach growl louder. But fortune smiles upon her; she comes across a small temple that worships the Earth Guardian nestled on the mountainside.

Calling it a temple might be a stretch; it is merely a humble shrine housing a small clay idol draped in red silk. Still, the offerings laid before it show that the villagers have not forgotten their deity.

Girl: Sorry for the intrusion.

The steamed buns have gone sticky from the damp air, with mud spatters still clinging stubbornly to them. The fruit, at least, is fresh. The girl picks up a pear and an orange, gives them a quick wipe on her sleeve, and bites right in.

Of course, the villagers wouldn't offer their finest fruit to a roadside shrine, but as sweet juice bursts across her tongue, it is more than enough to chase away the immediate pangs of hunger.

Girl: Please don't be mad, Earth Guardian. Next time I'm up here, I'll bring you something nice from the temple...

She tosses the peels and cores into the woods, then continues on her way.

### Lichen 1-Dialogue4

Hidden deep within the mountain forest is a natural limestone cave known as the Immortal's Grotto. Many years ago, it served as the main path up the mountain.

But once the flagstone trail was laid, few people took this route anymore. After all, the cave is dark, cramped, and more than a little unsettling. In fact, the adults of Houshan Village often invoke the Immortal's Grotto to frighten their children into good behavior.

Villager: You keep acting up, and the grotto monster's gonna come snatch you right out of bed!

*Hmph. The Immortal's Grotto... if it is truly an immortal's dwelling, how could there be any monsters? Besides, there is... nothing in this cave at all.*

The girl shakes her head. She is the only one who still frequents this path—because her shifu, Master Daoxuan of the Tianyi Sect, a figure of ethereal grace and wisdom in both public and private, had once told her:

Daoxuan: There are no monsters in the Immortal's Grotto. In fact, there are no large beasts left on this mountain at all...

Girl: Then... does an immortal live there?

Daoxuan: No, not there.

Girl: Then where do the immortals live?

Daoxuan: In the heavens, naturally.

Girl: The heavens... is that somewhere very, very high?

Daoxuan: Indeed. The immortals dwell in the heights, each tending to their duties.

Girl: Immortals have duties?

Daoxuan: Of course. They receive the people's prayers and offerings, so in return, they bless the land of the Eastern Continent with calm winds, gentle rains, and protection from harm.

Girl: That sounds wonderful... Shifu, when will you become an immortal?

Daoxuan: Silly child. Where did that come from?

Girl: But don't you want to?

Daoxuan: Well... any Daoist who says they have no wish to ascend would be lying.

Girl: I knew it, Shifu! You're so good at chanting all those mysterious words...

Daoxuan: Impudent girl. That was the *Tao Te Ching*.

Girl: But Shifu... once you become an immortal, you'll be flying all over the Eastern Continent, and you won't have time to come see me anymore...

Daoxuan: That won't happen.

Daoxuan: I promise you, even if one day I do become an immortal, I will always return to find you...

### Lichen 1-Dialogue5

Stepping out of the dark, cramped Immortal's Grotto, the view suddenly opens up into light.

Beyond a stretch of woods lies the back courtyard of the temple, where a pavilion stands, a place meant for rest and for gazing upon the other temples of their sect. Few ever come this way, as the path is seldom used. Yet the young girl stops here.

What makes her pause is not the pavilion itself, but the mountain vista she has seen hundreds of times before:

A stone staircase winds its way up from the foot of the mountain, and through the drifting mist, a steady stream of worshippers comes and goes.

From where the girl stands, they look like little ants—scurrying, bustling, both funny and strangely endearing.

Girl: So many people come to pray to the immortals... Do they really think they can get every single wish granted?

Girl: But honestly, they're kind of silly. The immortals live way, way up in the heavens. If you only climb halfway up the mountain and then leave, how are they supposed to hear you?

Girl: Not me, though. If I ever have a real wish, I'll climb all the way to the very top and tell the immortals myself. The very top...

The young girl lifts her gaze toward the mountaintop. Though she can't see it from here, she knows that at the very highest point of the mountain stands a stone statue of a Celestial Master. It's said to have been erected by her shifu's shifu's shifu, many generations ago.

Girl: Come to think of it... I've never actually met an immortal.

### Lichen 1-Dialogue6

At this hour, the temple is bustling with worshippers, but the priests are few and far between, for most have been sent out by Daowei to search for the "missing" girl. Those who remain are too busy attending to worshippers to keep much of an eye out.

Seizing the opportunity, the young girl slips into the temple. While the priests are distracted, she swipes two plump steamed buns from the kitchen, tucks them into her robe, and makes her way straight toward the mountaintop.

### Lichen 1-Dialogue7

The path behind the temple leading to the summit is treacherous—not only steep, but choked with dense, overgrown vegetation. Even with her agility, there is no way the young girl can climb that way.

The only other option is the smooth stone stairway leading upward, but that route passes directly through the Shangqing Palace. And therein lies her dilemma.

How can she reach the summit without being spotted by the priests? More importantly, how can she avoid being caught and dragged back for a good scolding, or worse, having her palms paddled red?

### Lichen 1-Dialogue8

Shangqing Palace. A Daoist priestess sweeps into the inner hall, shoving aside the curtain, and nearly collides with a small figure seated in meditation on a cushion.

Fortunately, she catches herself just in time. After steadying herself from the stumble, she hastily gives the young boy on the cushion a nod.

Daowei: Forgive my intrusion.

Boy: No harm done. You didn't actually hit me.

The boy waves his hand, brushing aside her concern. His lanky frame, paired with such sage-like words, should have seemed comical. However, when he smiles, the gentle curve of his eyes and his composed bearing make him truly resemble a celestial child from Daoist legend, not one to be taken lightly.

Boy: Priestess Daowei, what has you in such a rush? Has the temple been burgled?

Daowei: No, it's just Li Xianling. Again. Say, have you seen her? She's vanished. No one's seen her all morning.

The boy lifts his head slightly, his gaze resting on the donation box before him. After a moment of quiet thought, he replies:

Boy: I have not.

Daowei: Well... if you do spot her, please let us know. That girl is in dire need of discipline...

Boy: I will.

The boy remains seated upright, his composure undisturbed throughout.

Boy: Priestess Daowei, please, compose yourself. This is the Shangqing Palace. Let's not disturb the Grandmaster.

Knowing she is somewhat in the wrong, the priestess nods and takes her leave from the inner hall.

Little does she know, her vantage point has failed her. Had she crouched down or paused to meditate on a cushion, she would have spotted a pair of big eyes hidden inside the donation box staring right back at the boy.

Girl: I almost suffocated in there!

The girl springs from the donation box and heads straight for the back door of the hall, without sparing the boy even a single glance.

Boy: Wait...

The boy rises to his feet. At this, the girl looks back.

Girl: What now, Lit—tle— Uncle?

Girl: I'm going to find an immortal. Unless you're planning to turn me in to Priestess Daowei for credit?

A hint of impatience creeps into the girl's voice, yet the boy takes no offense. Instead, he remains calm as he makes his request.

Boy: If you do meet one... come back and tell me what they look like.

Girl: Easy! You'll be the first to know.

Boy: And... don't be rude to them.

Girl: Yeah, yeah. Got it.

The girl dashes off in a flash, while the boy settles back onto his meditation cushion as if no one had ever been there.

### Lichen 1-Dialogue9

From Shangqing Palace to the summit, the remaining climb spans about a hundred meters in elevation, yet the actual distance traveled is over ten times that.

Despite the long journey, the girl munches on the buns and listens to the birdsong in the woods, covering the entire distance in just an hour.

At last, she arrives before the statue of the Celestial Master.

This is not her first time visiting the statue. She has often wandered around it, touching here, peeking there. Now, however, she has made up her mind to climb it. Gazing up at the stone figure towering over ten meters high, she cannot help but feel a twinge of hesitation.

Girl: What's there to be scared of... It's just a big rock. I've climbed plenty of those before!

The girl grits her teeth, steels her resolve, and scrambles upward—half climbing, half leaping.

### Lichen 1-Dialogue10

It's no easy ascent. Though the statue is regularly cleaned and maintained, years of weathering have left parts of it loose and crumbling.

Luckily, the girl is young and light. Were an adult to try, even an expert rock climber, they would struggle to make it this far.

Clambering over the stone-carved head of the Celestial Master, the girl finally reaches the summit. Cool mist drifts through her open palms as white cranes glide past the sun sinking toward the horizon.

Without a doubt, this is the place closest to the sky for hundreds of miles around, and therefore, the closest to the dwelling of the immortals.

Girl: Ahhhhhhhhh—

The girl cannot help but shout toward the sky, letting go of all the weariness she has carried along the way.

As her voice echoes through the woods, she finally remembers why she came. She cups her hands around her mouth and calls out again toward the vast sky.

Girl: Immortals! You there?

For a long moment, there is only silence. The girl tries again:

Girl: Immortals? Hello?

Girl: Can you hear me?

Still, no one answers. But at that very moment, the sunset-painted clouds above begin to shift until they take on the unmistakable likeness of a Daoist sage, seated upon a meditation cushion in the sky.

Girl: You definitely can hear me. I know it!

The girl sits cross-legged atop the Celestial Master's stone head, playfully swaying her head at the clouds above.

Girl: Okay, I'm gonna tell you my wish now. So listen close!

Girl: My wish... My wish is...

Girl: My wish is...

The girl falters. She hasn't actually considered what her wish might be.

When she stops to think, her life is already plenty full. She chases birds through the mountain woods, tumbles into dirt fights with the village kids, and never once wonders where her next meal will come from. There's always enough.

Morning lessons? A bit dull, maybe. But after everything it took to get here—after all that climbing—wasting a wish on something so small feels almost silly. And anyway, if it really came to it, she could always just skip class again.

After a long pause, her thoughts drift back to the worshippers she passed on the mountain trail. A thought settles over her: from so far below, the immortals must never hear them at all. And for the first time, she feels something like pity.

Girl: My wish is... for you immortals to come down from the heavens. Go to the people below. And listen to their wishes.

Girl: ...Yeah. That's it!

As the last word leaves her lips, the cloud that had taken the shape of a Daoist sage ripples once more, this time like a slow nod. Then a gust of wind sweeps through, scattering its form into wisps of light that drift lazily toward the horizon.

The girl takes this as an answer enough. And at the thought that the immortals would descend to the mortal realm, granting wishes to all those worshippers, all because of what she'd said, it feels like she's done something truly great.

Maybe it's the climb catching up with her. Too tired to find a proper spot, she simply sprawls out atop the statue's head. Before long, her breathing steadies, and she slips into a deep, dreamless sleep.
