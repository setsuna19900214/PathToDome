# Defying Fate

With sacred rites, I divine the heaven's unbending will; With doom's inferno, it forges fate until all life is gone.

I need not ask the hour when defying heaven's decree; For I serve only men, not the immortals.

It is early spring, and the stream at the mountain's foot has only just thawed. Lyu Buzhou sets down his carrying pole and pushes the empty buckets beneath the icy surface.

Since the earthquake two months ago destroyed the local water and power stations, the temple has had to do without modern conveniences.

Water must be carried by hand, firewood gathered for warmth, and candles or oil lamps lit against the dark. The most renowned Daoist mountain in the world now lives much as its ancestors did, and the younger Daoist disciples grumble endlessly about it.

Lyu Buzhou, on the other hand, feels oddly content. He has already read every book in the temple's collection, and games of chess or cards with the other young disciples hold little joy for him.

These new labors have given his days a certain rhythm, and his body has grown stronger for it.

The fresh mountain air has healed him, too. The small aches that came from years bent over a desk in the city have faded, and old muscles have returned where he had long forgotten them.

With both buckets brimming, Lyu Buzhou lifts the pole with little effort, crosses the gravel-strewn bank in a few light strides, and steps back onto the stone stairway with perfect balance.
