### test

Li Xianling: So, did you see her? The immortal, I mean.

Lyu Buzhou: Did you?

Lyu Buzhou counters with the question, but Li Xianling doesn't even hesitate before answering:

Li Xianling: Like hell I did.

Lyu Buzhou: She's your shifu. She didn't come to you?

Li Xianling: Isn't she your shifu too?

For a moment, neither of them speaks. Then, as if on cue, laughter spills from both ends.

Li Xianling: So, how've you been? The old fogeys still giving you trouble?

Lyu Buzhou: They fell silent once the paper hit the journals.

Li Xianling: Look at you, Professor Lyu. Keep the papers coming and make it to the department head already. Then I can latch onto your coattails and coast.

Lyu Buzhou: You flatter me, Boss Li. An immortal's disciple, and your ventures have flourished rather magnificently these years.

Lyu Buzhou: I'm half-expecting you to fund our research someday.

Li Xianling: Please... you, the famous Saint of the Tables, worrying over funds? A few rounds with the right politicians and names, and you'd have all you need.

Lyu Buzhou: ...Win too much, and you make enemies. Win too little, and the name suffers. A hollow reputation, perhaps, but a useful one at the right moment. I'd prefer not to squander it.

Li Xianling: Okay, okay—stop. Lectures give me a headache... Let's get to business.

Lyu Buzhou: ...You should have started with that.

As the conversation turns to serious matters, Li Xianling's voice grows noticeably quieter, and she lets Lyu Buzhou's teasing pass without acknowledgment.

Li Xianling: Can you spare the time to come here?

Two cities and over six hundred kilometers separate them, and one of those cities has just suffered severe flooding. The road between them is fraught with danger.

Such a demand would strike most as unreasonable. But Lyu Buzhou offers no protest, only waits patiently for Li Xianling to explain herself.

Li Xianling: I found it.

Lyu Buzhou: Found what?

Li Xianling: What your paper predicted.

Lyu Buzhou: ...

Li Xianling: I can't deal with it myself. You're the only one I can turn to.

Lyu Buzhou: Why not bring in the authorities?

Li Xianling: Do you want me to?

Her question hangs in the air, and the conversation stalls. A beat of silence passes before Lyu Buzhou answers, his voice low and steady:

Lyu Buzhou: Alright.

Li Xianling: It's growing unstable. Come quickly.

Lyu Buzhou: I'll arrive by early tomorrow.

The call drops, and Lyu Buzhou begins packing at once.

Director: No, no, it's nothing, Professor Lyu. I won't keep you. Just be careful on the road.

Li Xianling had made it clear that time was short, so Lyu Buzhou couldn't take a wide detour. Passing along the edges of the disaster area would be unavoidable.

That was why he reached out to the university's logistics director and secured a rugged, high-clearance vehicle, one normally reserved for the hydrology department.

The car rumbles to life, its headlights slicing through the downpour. Lyu Buzhou smiles and waves to the director once more.

Still, a low current of unease stirs within him, impossible to shake.

Before leaving, Lyu Buzhou performs one more divination.

Lyu Buzhou: Arguing. With sincerity, yet obstructed and wary. Good fortune in stopping midway, misfortune in seeing it through. It is favorable to seek a great person, unfavorable to great waters.

The divination before him makes Lyu Buzhou pause, his mind drifting. Once before, on a day burned into his memory, the very same hexagram had appeared.

It pulls him back into the past, back to the people he cherished in the Tianyi Sect, and to the Daoist who had first taught him the art of divination.

Lyu Buzhou: ...

Lyu Buzhou lied. Just days ago, like many others, he had dreamed of the "immortal." The one who had predicted the catastrophe, the figure known as Celestial Master Daoxuan.

His *shifu*.

And there is one more thing Lyu Buzhou has never confessed to anyone.

During the first few years after Daoxuan disappeared, he had cast divination after divination for her. Coins, the Dayan method, even ornithomancy...

He refused to give up, going so far as to attempt divining her physical location. Yet every single reading led to the same place.

The Immortal's Grotto, halfway up Mount Tianyi.

As the disasters spread, Lyu Buzhou's divinations as the Celestial Master's disciple grew more and more precise. For years now, he had rarely been wrong.

But he never again dared to divine anything concerning Daoxuan. It was not the fear of being involved in an "immortal's" karma that held him back. It was...

He has always kept a faint hope alive, unwilling to accept what those readings had revealed.

If nothing was ever found, then perhaps... perhaps that carefree, indulgent shifu, the one who used to sneak candies to him and Li Xianling behind Daowei's back...

Perhaps... she could still come back.

### test

Two hundred kilometers into the drive, the lane heading the other way begins to clog with vehicles.

These, Lyu Buzhou understands, are civilians fleeing the neighboring city.

But it is not only cars. Along the roadside, people walk in scattered groups. The expressions they wear, stunned or frightened, press against his chest like physical blows, each one heavier than the last.

Lyu Buzhou: ...

Lyu Buzhou had known all along that this was coming.

His divinations had foreseen the disaster, and he had reported it immediately to the authorities. Yet as with Daoxuan years before, his warnings fell on deaf ears.

On the day the floods struck, he donated his entire personal savings, everything he had earned over years of teaching. What else was left for him to do?

He doesn't know. He is not an immortal. Against the sweeping decree of Heaven, even a seeker of the Dao like him cannot push back, much less the countless... countless ordinary souls.

By the time Lyu Buzhou stirs awake, the rain has stopped outside. The clouds split open, and the sunlight reaches down to touch the city lying just ahead.

By noon, Lyu Buzhou, who is never late, still hasn't shown. Li Xianling moves restlessly around the office, her footsteps marking out her agitation.

News of the second breach has reached her. Since the night before, every call to Lyu Buzhou's terminal has failed. The gnawing dread and helplessness are nearly more than she can bear.

But the girl's older sister is still here, and her despair runs even deeper. Li Xianling must wear a mask of calm, if only to keep the woman from shattering before her eyes.

Assistant: Master Lichen! He's here!!

The door swings open, and behind the assistant comes a man whose exhausted face speaks of a sleepless night. Li Xianling crosses the room at once.

Li Xianling: What happened to you...? Are you alright?

Her young uncle has never looked this wrecked in all the years she's known him. Even so, he brushes it aside with a careless wave.

Lyu Buzhou: I'm quite alright. The one you spoke of... where are they?

Catching sight of the newcomer, the woman on the couch rushes forward and collapses to her knees in a frenzy of emotion.

Woman: Great master! Please, save my sister! She's all I have left...

Lyu Buzhou catches her arm to steady her.

Lyu Buzhou: I'm no great master. But I'll do everything I can.

Li Xianling: This way.

Without further words, the group passes through the office and into Li Xianling's bedroom.

On the bed lies a girl, her eyes closed and her face discolored to a deathly grey. Her tongue lolls out, unnaturally long like that of a serpent.

Even more grotesque, her neck has sprouted seven long, fleshy protrusions, each one etched with the faint suggestion of a face mirroring her own.

The condition resembles no disease known to medicine. Rather, it calls to mind a creature Lyu Buzhou has touched upon in his mythology and folklore courses.

### test

After another hundred kilometers, the traffic thins until the road ahead lies almost empty.

Then, a deep, rolling sound reaches Lyu Buzhou's ears, like thunder trapped underground. Unease coils in his stomach.

His instincts prove right. The closer he drives, the louder the sound becomes, until at last a churning torrent cuts across the road before him.

Lyu Buzhou: ...Great.

The main river channel nearby has breached, and the floodwater, dragging mud and rubble with it, has carved straight through the road.

Lyu Buzhou reaches for his coins to cast a divination, but then his hand hovers in the air, uncertain.

Lyu Buzhou: All this divining is turning my mind in circles...

Lyu Buzhou: Even if the omens turn dire, what would I do? Turn around?

Lyu Buzhou: The car was built for this, crossing tidal flats, rough waters. It should manage. It should...

Lyu Buzhou shakes off the thought. Gritting his teeth, he throws the car into gear and presses the accelerator to the floor.

He forces the door open with a kick and pulls himself out.

Lyu Buzhou: Unfavorable... to great waters, huh...

A wry smile crosses Lyu Buzhou's face. The rockslide caught the car and flipped it, but he had still managed to cross the flood. At the very least, he survived.

But what now?

The rain shows no sign of stopping. Half the vehicle is already swallowed by mud and debris, and the supplies inside are beyond recovery. On his person, he has only a dead terminal without signal and a few documents pressed close to his chest.

Still, it is worth the attempt. Where there is will, there is a path.
