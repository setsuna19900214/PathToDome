### Diver 1-Dialogue 5

?? ??, N.F. 16

Santis Port

Townie A: Heading out to sea today?

Townie B: Are you crazy? Do you have a death wish?

Townie B: The waves are strong enough to rip a boat apart! Old Zieg wouldn't listen yesterday, insisted on going out to sea... and well...

Townie B: All that's left on the shore now are splinters of wood!

Townie A: Sigh, if this wind doesn't let up soon, I might as well sell my boat cheap and find another trade. What's the use of staying here if I can't even set sail...

Townie A: Oh, young lady, the sea's been really rough lately. Stay away from here! Even if you're just playing around, it's best to keep your distance from the shore!

Pylgia: ...Okay, I'm just here for a walk.

After watching the two people settle their boats, Pylgia heads to her familiar entry point to the sea—the reef behind the boat storage facility.

With the escalating severity of this year's storms and the increasing intensity of calamities, port authorities have strictly banned civilians from approaching the water.

But where there's a will, there's a way—Pylgia has still found her path to the sea. With almost no one sailing anymore, the boat storage facility lies abandoned and forgotten, yet few realize it offers ideal access to the water.

Despite her parents' constant warnings to stay away from the sea, Pylgia remains enchanted by its beauty, always finding ways to slip into the water unnoticed.

Today is no different—as long as she times it right, no one will discover her little secret.

Pylgia: Pylgia, ready to go.

Dark clouds gather rapidly on the horizon as the sea breeze grows more erratic. The waters churn restlessly, filling the air with a palpable sense of foreboding.

Today's storm looks worse than the ones from the past few days. She has to find shelter from the howling winds.

At the very moment these thoughts drift through Pylgia's mind, an unfamiliar sound catches her attention—the low hum of a yacht's engine.

As she surfaces, the people on the yacht spot her and immediately steer their vessel in her direction.

As the lifeboat draws up beside her, Pylgia gets a clear look at its occupants—several people in waterproof suits, their faces etched with unusual anxiety, evidently not locals.

??: Child, what are you doing out here alone? A storm is coming!

A young man leans down and reaches out to help her aboard, but Pylgia ignores his gesture, asking her own question instead.

Pylgia: You all look troubled. Did anything happen?

Researcher A: Our research vessel hit a reef and the hull is damaged! Hurry, hop in—take us to the adults on shore!

Pylgia: So... you need a guide?

Researcher A: I'm telling you again! Get on the boat! The storm will hit in two hours. If we stay here, we'll all die!

Researcher B: Look, I appreciate you want to help, but we need the nautical chart of the area. Without it, we could hit another reef on the way to the port.

Researcher B: Plus, we need to establish communication with the control tower onshore for navigation instructions. These things need to be handled by professionals! Take us to shore! We're running out of time...

Pylgia: Why won't you trust me? Going to shore for help and coming back would only waste more time.

Researcher B: You're just a child! We can't take that risk!

Pylgia: I'm a child who appeared before you in this weather, in what you call "a dangerous place." Even so, you still won't trust me?

Pylgia's words leave the agitated researchers exchanging uncertain glances. Right then, she extends her hand towards them.

Pylgia: I'll be your guide. Time doesn't wait.

### Diver 1-Sanity 1

The storm gradually recedes into the distance as the dark clouds dissipate, revealing a clear night sky.

Pylgia wearily makes her way back towards town, her clothes drenched and her hair sticking to her forehead. Strands of seaweed stubbornly cling to her hands.

She has just survived a thrilling adventure, successfully rescuing a research vessel that had hit a reef.

As the familiar sight of town comes into view, however, she's greeted not by cheers or praise, but by the sound of her parents' hurried footsteps and worried calls.

Mother: Py! Where have you been?! Haven't I told you not to wander off in weather like this?!

Tears well up in her mother's eyes, her voice shaking uncontrollably. The woman rushes forward and wraps Pylgia in a tight embrace.

Father: The sea is extremely dangerous right now. Even I wouldn't dare go out there. If anything had happened to you...

Looking at her worried parents, Pylgia wants to explain—she had only meant to play in the sea, but ended up saving lives because of it. Besides, she has friends in the ocean depths. There's no reason for them to worry so much about her.

Pylgia: But Mom, Dad... my friends in the sea... they would never let anything hurt me.

Her mother lets out a weary sigh, her eyes filled with resignation and concern.

Mother: Sweetie, you're not a little girl anymore. You can't keep talking about fairy tales like you used to.

Father: The undercurrents and reefs could easily rip you apart at any moment! You may have saved someone this time, but what about the next time? Or the time after that?

Pylgia falls silent under her parents' reproach, unable to find the words to respond.

Researcher C: I understand she shouldn't have done it, but please don't be too hard on her. She really did save all of us.

The research vessel crew approaches. A woman among them kneels down beside Pylgia, tenderly caressing the girl's hair with gratitude shining in her eyes.

Researcher C: Without her guidance, our ship would have already been torn apart by the reefs.

Researcher C: Nevertheless, I agree that she shouldn't take such risks anymore. The sea has changed—it's now more dangerous and unpredictable. Storms are on the rise, and the ocean currents have become harder to navigate.

Researcher C: That's no environment for a young girl to handle—even we adults find ourselves overwhelmed by it.

Researcher C: You've done a wonderful job, but now let the adults handle things from here.

Researcher C: Listen to your parents, take care of yourself, and try not to make them worry anymore, okay?

Pylgia remains silent, offering a noncommittal response.

### Diver 1-Sanity 2

The storm still rumbles behind them, but the research vessel is nearly at shore—they're safe now.

Pylgia glances at the time. It's already 11:35 at night.

She would normally be in bed by now.

Researcher B: The tides and currents here are constantly changing, and there are countless underwater reefs and sandbars. Are you sure you can handle this?

Pylgia: Yeah, I'm sure. I've been here dozens of times.

Researcher B: Dozens of times? Even veteran fishermen wouldn't dare come here. You're not making this up, are you?

Pylgia: I'm not lying. My father never comes near here when he goes fishing. I only come here when I sneak out to play by myself.

Researcher B: You've got more courage than any kid your age should have.

Pylgia: ...? But I don't find this place dangerous at all. Besides, whenever there's trouble, my friends in the sea come to help me.

Pylgia: That's why I'm not afraid at all.

The researchers sigh in resignation, feeling as though the girl before them seems to exist entirely outside their realm of understanding. In the distance, thunder rumbles, and Pylgia immediately snaps to attention.

### Diver 2-Dialogue 3

Pylgia: There's a reef here, watch out!

Following Pylgia's directions, the researchers work on mapping the waters.

Tossed up and down by the surging waves, the ship leans precariously to one side. She is acutely aware that time is slipping away—they must reach the land before the storm engulfs them completely.

She closes her eyes and takes a deep breath, feeling the pulse of the sea.

Pylgia: Head northwest! It's safer there!

The storm devours her words, forcing her to shout to be heard. The violent winds relentlessly propel wave after wave against the ship, which fights against the tumultuous sea like a solitary leaf.

Pylgia: There's an invisible undercurrent ahead that can easily throw a ship off course! Follow the southern cliff face instead. The water's deeper there; it'll be much safer.

As the ship changes course once more, a dull thud echoes from beneath the hull.

Researcher A: Crap, we've hit something!

Pylgia: It's a sandbar! Keep going! There's a channel just ahead. If we can make it through, we'll escape the storm!

Though doubtful, the researcher sees the determination in the young girl's eyes and decides to trust her.

Gritting his teeth, he wrenches the helm around. The ship groans in protest, then begins to move—slowly at first, but finally breaking free from the sandbar's grip.

Researcher A: We made it! Let's keep going!

### Diver 2-One-stroke 1

Researcher B: We're finally back! We can head for the port anytime now!

Scratches and cracks mark the weathered hull of the research vessel. While seawater has infiltrated certain areas of the deck, the damage is not substantial enough to impede the voyage.

The researchers' attitude toward Pylgia has completely reversed during the journey—from skepticism to belief, and finally to awe. Now, they accept every word she says without question.

Pylgia: I'm not the amazing one... it's my friends who tell me these things.

Researcher B: Your friends? You mean the ones who sailed with you?

Pylgia: No... It's my friends living beneath the waves. They tell me everything about the sea.

The researchers display incredulous looks as they listen to Pylgia's words.

Pylgia: But I feel... you people and I are not so different. We both study the sea and learn its ways...

Pylgia watches the researchers at work, her head tilting with curiosity.

Researcher A: True, but our work here might just be the most tedious job you can imagine.

Pylgia: What makes you say that?

Researcher A: In this line of work, you might go months without setting foot on land, but we humans are creatures of the earth, after all.

Researcher A: After enough time at sea, you start missing everything about life on land.

Researcher A: What, thinking of becoming an ocean researcher when you grow up? Haha... I wouldn't recommend it. It's not exactly the most exciting career.

Pylgia: Not exciting? But isn't it wonderful to spend all day in the sea... and make friends with all the sea creatures?

Researcher A: ...You're really something special, aren't you?
