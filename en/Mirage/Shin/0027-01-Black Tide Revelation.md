She follows these sounds, peering into the abyssal depths, seeking only to witness the world's hidden truths.

## AZ-10001 Black Tide Revelation

### test

Shin: I'm truly honored to receive this award. Allow me to express my sincerest thanks to the selection committee. I'm also deeply grateful to the people who have supported and inspired me on my academic journey.

Shin: First, my family. Without their trust and support, I could not have dedicated myself so completely to my research. I feel their encouragement, wherever I am. Their belief in me has been a constant source of motivation.

A respectable middle-aged couple applauds in the crowd, barely concealing their excitement as they bask in the envious stares Shin has brought them. Their eyes are filled with smug pride and self-satisfaction, threatening to drown her.

Shin stands in the study with her arms at her sides. The wood grain of the bookcases warps into countless shrewd, calculating eyes, watching and appraising her from every angle, just like how her parents assess any family asset.

Father: I never expected Professor Wright to remember you, let alone write a recommendation letter. Choosing oceanography was the right move! It's obscure, challenging, but easy to make a name for yourself. You must seize this opportunity, Shin.

Mother: That's right. Professor Wright knows better than anyone how to produce results in this field. With his guidance, you'll join the review committee or academy yourself one day. I'm so proud of you!

Father: Just stick with him, and you'll never have to worry about funding or awards. I heard his students secured two Class A projects last year... Who even cares what he's studying? Funding is power.

Father: You need to learn his methods, Shin. That's the real secret to a career in research and academia. In just a few years, you'll become the symbol of our branch, the greatest pride of the family!

Shin observes her parents with cold detachment. The estranged couple now share the same rapturous expression, their eyes dreamy, as if seeing, through their daughter, a grand banquet hall teeming with influential guests.

Is this gaudy vision of fame and fortune truly more intoxicating than the pursuit of unfathomable mysteries and truths in the ocean depths?

Shin: Of course, Father, Mother. I'm looking forward to studying under Professor Wright and continuing my *research*.

Shin: And of course, the person I wish to thank most is my mentor, Professor Wright. It was his wisdom that guided me into the fascinating world of marine science, and helped me find my way whenever I felt lost.

In the front row, Professor Wright gives a small, satisfied nod. A soft light glimmers in his eyes, as if moved by the praise. But the gleam is too shallow to hide the greed and arrogance lurking beneath.

Behind the elderly professor hangs a biological specimen, collected on his last expedition before the Perishing Star arrived. Set in a glass case, it resembles a twisted pupil that has absorbed the ocean itself, gazing at Shin from its fathomless depths.

Professor Wright: I'm putting you on the next expedition. Don't worry, there won't be short-sighted opportunists aboard, just seasoned veterans. The route is conservative, steering clear of the unusually active zones. Everyone knows we're going to sea for publications, not adventure.

Professor Wright: Some young scholars are too ambitious. They're fascinated with evolution and the unknown, always chasing some grand discovery. But once you've been in academia long enough, you realize that's the worst return on investment. It's much easier to produce results by building on what's already known.

Professor Wright: You are my brightest, most talented student, Shin. Remember what I've told you. When you're back, I'll guide you through a paper. You'll be the second author. I trust you'll master this approach quickly.

The eyes of the scholar, so long estranged from the sea, are brimming with cowardice and conceit. He no longer sees the ocean.

Shin: Of course, Professor. I'm sure the final data will validate your theories. After all, you're the foremost authority on oceanography. You understand the changing seas better than anyone.

Shin: Finally, I'd like to take this opportunity to thank my family tutor, who first set me on this path. Her guidance helped me build a solid foundation in the field, and her vision, always fixed on the horizon, showed me the infinite possibilities within the ocean.

A ripple of confused whispers spreads through the audience, but Shin seems not to hear it. Her gaze shifts for a moment to the side of the podium, as if a woman with a faint smile were standing there.

The woman before her loses her composure for only a second before her familiar smile returns. It's a smile of gentle, persevering resilience, forged in the fires of endless setbacks. Her eyes are bright and warm, like sunlight penetrating the deep sea, guiding newborn creatures breaking free from their shells.

Tutor: Your answer in the competition is quite interesting. I doubt the examiner intended it, but the question does relate to what you asked before: "Why do deep-sea creatures follow such unique evolutionary paths?" Your thinking is on the right track.

Tutor: The poles and ocean trenches—places no one dares to go—are life's most extreme laboratories. The more dangerous the place, the more likely it is to hold possibilities that evolution has never shown us before. Most people are afraid, so they shrink from it...

Tutor: But you're different, Shin. I don't see fear or dread in your eyes, only curiosity and excitement. I believe that if anyone is ever to set foot in those unknown territories and witness the ultimate form of evolution, it would be you.

Shin: ...

The girl's gaze shifts from the unfamiliar application form in front of her and settles on the woman's face.

When their eyes meet, Shin feels as though her own eyes have been set ablaze by the light in her tutor's gaze. Then, she sees her teacher smile softly.

Tutor: But for that very reason, you have to be smarter about how you live, how you answer. Shin, sometimes this isn't about fitting in. It's about surviving, so you can keep moving forward, so you can see what lies ahead.

Tutor: Research demands the courage to challenge and explore. But until you've climbed high enough, you have to know when to keep quiet. You don't want to end up like me, treated like I don't exist. Funding pulled, papers erased, and my name struck from every conference list.

Desire fuels desire. Desire is traded for desire. Desire begets desire. Countless people have tried to bait Shin with the desires she's supposed to have, to make her a pawn for their own ambitions. She was the only one who showed Shin...

That without fame or fortune, without praise or applause, one could have such ardently burning eyes, just by gazing upon and pursuing the possibilities the rest of the world had forgotten.

Shin: I understand. I'll work hard. Until I can witness the end of evolution and the possibilities of life... I'll be good.

She worked steadily, step by step, toward her promise and aspiration. But the eyes that had shown her the possibilities of truth were buried long ago in an obituary, lost in a "reckless" and "insane" oceanographic expedition, never to return.

Even her name was gradually replaced by the word "lunatic," reaching Shin's ears from time to time.

The papers displayed on the screen are Shin's highly acclaimed academic works, yet they're filled with conservative research methods and results. How absurd. This cycle of desire called society cannot give Shin what she truly wants.

Shin: Marine life is changing at an unprecedented pace. At least five populations under observation are exhibiting mating and hunting behaviors that don't match our expectations... It's like they're mutating, evolving, turning into entirely new life forms.

Shin: Deep-sea species in particular—ancient, isolated, and resistant to change—are the farthest removed from human perception and imagination. But our technology has been compromised by the Perishing Star. Established methods are no longer reliable, and the ocean grows more dangerous every day...

Shin: Perhaps one day, we'll lose our ability to explore the ocean's forbidden zones altogether. Before that happens, we should at least try to understand the nascent biological order, rather than hiding in a bubble, fighting over a shrinking pie.

Shin: After all, when that day comes, who knows if humanity will still be at the top of the food chain.

Shin rips the draft of her speech from the notepad and crumples it into a ball. She tosses it into a nearby trash can, where it rolls around with a hollow echo.

Shin: ...Then again, are we now? Still the masters of the world, of the biosphere. The highest form of life.

No one knows.

Shin only knows that in an academic world unwilling to face radical change, her own yearning makes her feel as empty and restless as a deep-sea predator trapped in a fish tank. Just as she knows she can never, ever say these words in an acceptance speech.

Shin: That concludes my speech. Thank you for your time.

Host: Thank you, Miss Shin, for that wonderful speech! That brings us to the end of today's award ceremony. Please join us in the banquet hall to congratulate our outstanding winners and catch up with friends old and new!

The host has barely finished his sentence when the hall fills with the sound of scraping chairs. Shin is surrounded before she can even step down from the podium.

Participant A: Miss Shin, I find your new observational methods somewhat... unrefined. Wasn't it a bit reckless to apply them directly to your research?

Participant B: Miss Shin, I'm a director at a venture capital firm. Regarding the new species you've observed, perhaps we could discuss its commercial value and development roadmap...

They circle her like sharks drawn to blood, hoping to profit from her work. Shin simply offers a placid smile as she handles the deluge of questions and propositions, like a sea creature adjusting its breathing as it navigates ocean currents.

Shin: Please, everyone, one at a time. I'm only human, after all. I can't answer your questions all at once, and we're holding up the staff here... Why don't we continue our discussion in the banquet hall?

She leads the group toward the hall, like a leviathan guiding a school of fish. Perhaps in these murkier waters, she can find the prey she's truly hunting for.

### test

The crescendo of chatter and clinking glasses begins to subside. Shin finally gets a moment to breathe. Another fruitless evening. Another hollow, uninteresting day.

The carrion-feeders, as empty-handed as she is, are growing restless. A few close in around her again, desperate for a final gamble.

Broker: Miss Shin, I've sent you several emails but never received a reply. This is a rare opportunity, and I wanted to speak with you in person...

Journal Editor: Miss Shin, we're a newly established academic journal, and we're wondering if you might...

??: Miss Shin. I don't know if Captain Maud has mentioned me. My name is Joseph, and I've partnered with him a few times. Been going to sea for research for over a decade now. I heard... you're interested in the pole?

Among the swarm of vultures, this middle-aged man is unremarkable in his wrinkled suit, almost shabby. But his opening words make Shin drop her practiced smile.

For the first time, she truly looks at him, into those eyes...

Dark and murky, yet flickering with a faint phosphorescence. Like an old, scarred shark from the abyss, weary but stubborn. A mad, yet interesting light.

Shin: I'm sorry, did I hear you correctly? Are you talking about... the pole?

Joseph: Yes, the abyss between heaven and sea, the forbidden zone of the distant ocean, the unknown territories shrouded in mist. To be frank, ever since the heyday of polar expeditions, I've always wanted to go there. To the place only the best teams could reach. The pinnacle of human science.

Joseph: Unfortunately, I'm no genius. Couldn't get a spot on a crew. By the time I'd beefed up my credentials, the Perishing Star struck. The seas grew more treacherous. People got scared. They pulled back. They forgot. I waited all this time, only to end up with nothing.

Joseph: But I know that many scholars, like you, dream of seeing that place. There might be biological laws out there that transcend human understanding, miracles of life beyond our shallow imagination.

His voice is steady, but his eyes quiver, as if he can barely contain his absurd longing. Someone sneers, calling him a lunatic. But Joseph doesn't flinch. His gaze is trained on Shin.

He isn't betting on the opinions of others, but the singular will of the young academic leader before him. She shows no weaknesses, so he must take a risky gambit to try and move her.

Shin: So... you're saying you're willing to put together an expedition to the pole, a place no one dares to go? Just because you want to? Because you think there might be something there?

Joseph: That's right. You can call me crazy. But Miss Shin, you're a brilliant scientist with ambitions. You know better than anyone that the places no one dares to go are the ones most worth exploring.

He might be a mediocre scholar, but he's certainly an exceptional salesman. At least he knows exactly what role to play, what he's selling, and who he's selling it to.

Shin: But why me? Simply because I once told Captain Maud that I wanted to go to the pole? To do research there? That could have just been... the teenage fantasy of a girl who didn't know any better.

Joseph: I came to you because I need you. I want to go to a place everyone's afraid of. Most institutions are hesitant about polar expeditions. If you join, securing funding and assembling a team would be much easier.

Joseph: The only question left is whether you need this team. I'll handle all the tedious logistics—the funding, the crew. All you need to think about, Miss Shin, is this...

Joseph: As a researcher, do you still want to go to the pole? To study the life there? To decipher its mysteries? This is a transaction. And in a transaction, you only need to consider what you stand to gain.

With that, Joseph lays his cards on the table. His true purpose is clear. He'll use the allure of a polar expedition to reel Shin in, use her name to raise a team and funds, and gamble it all on a shot at fame and fortune.

But Shin doesn't care about his schemes. So what if it's madness? This is what she wants. What she's been waiting for all this time.

On the path to truth, if one isn't mad, how can one ever hope to glimpse things that were never meant for human eyes?

Shin: An interesting proposal. I enjoy doing business with lunatics.

Shin: I look forward to this polar expedition you're organizing. I hope we can both push our limits, get what we desire, and see what you described... a miracle of life that transcends humanity's shallow imagination.

Scholar: Miss Shin, I don't know if you remember me. We worked together on a project in an elective course at university...

Shin: Of course I do. You had a keen eye for anomalous data; I was very impressed. I've read a few of your papers as well. Your research direction is quite novel.

Scholar: Heh, I wish the people who call my research unfounded speculations could hear you say that! W-well, in that case, perhaps you'd be interested in funding my research on subtropical benthic organisms? I just need...

Money? Of all the pursuits, that's got to be the most unexciting. Shin's silence seems to put him under immense pressure. He grows anxious, stammering out a defense.

Scholar: I-I didn't inflate the numbers! It's a little high, but that's because I won't be on the ship myself. I'll have to hire extra hands to make sure the crew doesn't cut corners. I have no choice... It's so dangerous these days. Nothing's more valuable than human life!

So, not just greed, but fear. He's afraid of the sea, afraid to witness its inexplicable transformations. He thinks that by hiding on shore, he can ensure everything still has meaning.

Shin: Yes, I understand. People... especially talented "elite professionals," are our most important assets, of course.

Her tone is gentle, her smile unchanged. But beneath the calm surface, pity and contempt for the coward churn like a violent undertow.

Useful to humankind? But humanity is so much more cowardly and stagnant than the marine life that embraces change. Do humans really have the authority, the right, to halt and exploit the ocean's creatures for their own gain?

But none of this misgiving or disgust shows on her face. They remain buried in the deep, dark currents of her thoughts.

Shin: You have a point. My funding comes from grants and sponsors. As an established researcher, I must consider which outcomes will yield sufficient returns. That is the foundation of any sustainable research program.
