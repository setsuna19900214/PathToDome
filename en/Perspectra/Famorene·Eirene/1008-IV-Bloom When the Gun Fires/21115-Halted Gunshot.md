# Halted Gunshot

## IV

Bloom When the Gun Fires

Endgame of the Long Night

Grace steadies White as he braces a hand against the long table, his fingers knotted into the tablecloth, trembling beyond his control.

White: Wh-what... what choices...

Eirene: One: repay every coin. Two... leave the Campbell family and live out your years in a prison cell or some distant satellite town. The choice is yours.

Grace: Eirene, you can't do this to us!

Grace: Have you forgotten how we treated you when you first came here?!

Eirene gives a quiet laugh, as if something amusing has just reached her ears.

And for Grace, it is the first time she has ever seen such a smile on Eirene's face.

Eirene: Let me tell you something you may not know. White roses are not so fragile as people believe. They can be grown outdoors, through wind and weather. My mother loved them... She used to say the color reminded her of the white pieces on a chessboard.

Eirene: So when she was buried, I asked the men who laid her to rest to plant a few beside her.

Eirene: They'd grown beautifully. Which is why, I imagine, you chose to cut them and bring them to me.

Grace: You... you...

Eirene: Why you came to me, what you wanted from me—I've known all along. Every moment of it.

White's shoulders slump, his frame bowing lower and lower, until he seems to realize there is no road left behind him.

Grace: No... this isn't over.

Grace catches hold of her deflated father and pulls him back up.

Grace: I have a factory. I've just sold it to a shareholder willing to buy it out.

Grace: I only have to sign the papers, and we'll have more than enough to cover the gap!

Grace: You... you will not drive us out!

Grace draws a folder from her handbag and lifts it high above her head, chin tilted up. She is nothing like the cousin who used to beam at Eirene every single day. Eirene glances at it and shakes her head.

Eirene: Forgive me for being blunt, Uncle White.

Eirene: Had you only walked away from the racecourse and the card tables, this document might have saved you.

Watching Eirene's smile, Grace senses something in the words left unsaid.

She flips the folder open in a rush, scanning the contract's dense print.

And there, at last, on the signature line of the acquisition page, she finds a name obscured beneath a golden rose seal. The name is—

*Eirene Campbell*

Eirene: I told you. I took the money from the account my father gave me, the one you never thought worth looking at, and I made a losing deal.

Eirene: Now you see what that losing deal was.

Eirene: You must have been in too much of a hurry to read it properly, Grace. When your finances have rotted through this far... wouldn't it be better to go back to school?

Grace's eyes open wider and wider, her face a mask of pure disbelief. Her grip fails her, and she lets her father fall to the ground.

Grace: No... the factory isn't worth that much. What else are you after?

Eirene: Clever. Truly, Grace, you never disappoint.

Something flickers in Eirene's eyes. She has just heard the exact words she was waiting for.

Eirene: The factory isn't enough to cover the debt. But you still have the estate and the land.

Grace stands frozen, the stack of documents clutched tight in her hands, her eyes fixed hard on Eirene.

Grace: You... this was planned. From the moment we came to you... it had already started...

Eirene: Yes. All of it. You each wore your masks well enough, but... I could still smell the reek on you.

Chin in hand, Eirene drums a single finger against the tabletop, the rhythm light and deliberate, as if she were cradling a chess piece between her fingers.

Eirene: You see no further than a courtyard, cooped up in this tiny little cage. That alone proves neither of you was ever fit to be a Campbell.

Eirene: Who would be content with a single room in a building?

Eirene: If you want something, it should be an estate unbound by any one place, free to spread and flourish as it will. Wouldn't you agree?

Eirene: And that is precisely why you and your daughter amount to nothing more than pieces on my board.

Eirene: And tonight, at this banquet...

Eirene: Checkmate.

White can no longer find any words. Facing all that he is powerless to reverse, he sits anxiously on the ground, biting at his fingers, his eyes darting wildly as he searches for anything that might still save him.

That's right!

His daughter... he still has his daughter! As long as she marries and the trust conditions are satisfied, he will still have money! There is still room to turn this around—

But just as he wants to open his mouth and fabricate a new lie, he sees what Grace is holding in her hands.

Grace: I'll hand over the beneficiary rights as well. And I have the records, of White Campbell's fraudulent withdrawals from the family trust funds, and proof of his gambling.

White: Grace...? Not you...?

White cannot believe it. The daughter he raised has disobeyed him for the first time in her life.

Eirene: Oh? You wish to bargain?

Grace holds the documents, trembling faintly.

Grace: The estate is yours. The factory, too. You can hold the largest share. But I will not leave the board.

Grace: Business is what keeps a Campbell standing. I can do without a home. I can do without a father... But I will prove myself worthy of the name!

Grace takes up the pen. With shaking hands, she draws several sheets from the folder and begins to write out her testimony.

White: Grace! Are you insane?! I am your father!!!

Grace: And what of it?! Get away from me! The moment I learned what you were planning, you were no longer my father!

White's eyes go red and bulge with anger. Snarling, he seizes the hem of Grace's dress and claws at the documents in her hands.

Father and daughter wrestle, sending a candelabra crashing and plates shattering. As the farce plays out beside the table, every guest wishes only to be anywhere else.

Bang!!!

A gunshot cracks through the hall. A bullet tears straight through the documents, and the shock of it sends the two lurching apart, hands flying up to cover their ears.

Only the buzzing aftermath of the blast remains. The gunsmoke clears, and the crowd finally sees the shooter.

Eirene: How embarrassing.

Eirene puts the gun away, then lays it neatly before her father.

Only Celitane saw it clearly: how Eirene reached past her, snatched the pistol from her father's belt, and leveled it at her own family.

Eirene: Still, I find the terms you've offered quite satisfactory. You truly are... my dear cousin.

Eirene: You have one day to say your goodbyes to your father.

The shot puts an end to the spectacle. White and Grace say nothing more. They allow the security personnel to tidy the aftermath and take them separately to sign and stamp.

When the attorney arrives with the verdict and the various agreements, Grace catches sight of the personal seal in Eirene's hand.

Eirene: Ah, I forgot to mention.

Eirene holds the seal up for her to see.

Eirene: It's true I don't need to attend those classes. Not because I'm unqualified, but because I've long since moved past them.

With a decisive press, Eirene stamps the seal beside her signature. The red ink is threaded with fine gold powder, and a golden rose—the emblem of the Campbell family—blooms across the white page.

After watching everyone file out, Bryce stands at Eirene's side. He does not ask why she did any of it. He only asks:

Bryce: You know how to use a gun?

Eirene: My mother had one. Almost the same as this. I saw her take it out once and click off the safety.

Eirene: But she never pulled the trigger.

Eirene: I think she understood, even then, pointing a gun at yourself solves nothing.
