The banquet hall's dome arches high overhead. White stands at his place, fine beads of sweat gathering at his temples, glistening greasily under the pallid light above.

It seems it had never once occurred to him that the niece who understood nothing, who bent to his every wish, could turn on him with such a face.

White: They were just... just some necessary expenditures... for the upkeep of the family's holdings, nothing more...

Grace: Eirene, every Campbell trust account is sustained by our family assets. My father is your trustee and sole signatory. It's only a few necessary expenditures on his part. What could be more ordinary?

From the seat beside him, Grace rises and speaks in her father's defense.

Eirene: Ordinary, yes. Otherwise you couldn't have taken the money at all.

Eirene keeps her chin propped on her hand, her tone unhurried, and rather than answering her guilty Uncle White, she turns to her cousin.

Eirene: But Grace, did you never discuss with your own father what you've been learning?

Eirene: Even if he is my legal signatory, and the trust fund sits nominally in his name...

Eirene: The money still has to be spent on me.

Eirene: Otherwise it becomes... a fraudulent transfer. Doesn't it?

Grace's face holds not a shred of color. The marble table seems to have become a sheer divide, and across it sits a cousin she no longer knows—the very one who, just days before, had smiled at her with such tenderness.

Eirene: Ah, forgive me... I forgot. Those very classes, I sat through them in your place.

Whispers ripple through the seats, some guests murmuring among themselves, others savoring the humiliation of White's family as a fine dish to go with their wine.

Eirene: The bank manager and the family's attorney are waiting just outside. You have two choices, my dear uncle.

Eirene: I'd ask you to choose quickly. The banquet, after all, must go on.
