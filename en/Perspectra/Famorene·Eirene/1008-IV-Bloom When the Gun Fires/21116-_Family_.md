Endgame of the Long Night

May. A baby's cry tears through the stillness, bright and shrill. Eirene pushes the door open and enters, the candle flame setting shadows swaying across her face. Against the headboard Celitan leans, exhausted, wisps of hair matted to her damp forehead, and with a faint, weary motion of her head, she indicates the small inner room.

Eirene: White has been cast out of the Campbell family. And Grace will be married off early. She loses her share of the trust fund either way.

Eirene: All those assets are now in the child's name. I'm even listed as the signatory. She'll take her rightful place as the family's legitimate daughter.

A faint crease forms between the noblewoman's brows. Something impatient and guarded flits across her eyes, and she studies Eirene's face, as if searching for something just beneath the surface, yet her effort comes to nothing.

Celitane: Honestly... I still don't understand why you did any of this. Which is why I haven't trusted you... Not fully.

With unhurried steps, Eirene crosses to the door of the inner room and eases it open with a light touch.

Eirene: The enemy of my enemy is a friend. I helped you this once so that I'd have another piece to play when the time comes.

Eirene: After all, whatever White and the rest of them might claim, my blood runs closer to this child's than theirs ever will.

The noblewoman asks nothing more. She only closes her eyes, exhausted, as the candlelight carves deep shadows into her face. Eirene makes her way into the adjoining room, her footsteps ringing clearly through the quiet.

When White and his daughter first came to her, Eirene reopened that chess manual that night. Before, she had gone no further than the first page. Now she leafed through it one page at a time. The book was elementary, hardly touched, and yet in the middle, a handful of offhand scribbles had been left behind.

Adelaide: And the piece that delivers checkmate... turns out to be a pawn.

Adelaide: Heh... serves him right for having eyes only for the queen handed to him on a platter.

The page holds a composed endgame. White surrenders the queen on purpose; Black takes it greedily, blind to the knight and the rook already posted behind, every exit from their king's square long since walled off. It is Légal's Mate, the trick in the manual that wins big by risking small, bait laid for anyone too hungry to look past the pieces lurking nearby.

Two penciled circles sit beside the position. One surrounds the black king, stranded with nowhere to flee, with a string of numbers written next to it. Eirene knows what they are: the account number of the company registered under White's name.

The other circle encloses a white pawn, the least valuable piece on the board.

Eirene knows who it stands for. Herself.

She had been a piece set on the board from the very first move, and the least valuable of them, the kind that can be swept off without a flicker of regret.

The eldest son, it seemed, had long meant to wipe out this side branch their father so despised, yet he would not soil his own hands doing it.

What he had failed to foresee was that she would take that very plan and turn it to her own ends.

Eirene: A pawn... He wasn't wrong about that.

Eirene: He just forgot one thing... a pawn that reaches the last rank gets to promote.

Eirene lifts the pawn that has advanced all the way to the final rank off the board and sets a black queen in its place.

Eirene: But... for the game from here on... I'll need every piece I can get my hands on.

In the sitting room beyond the small inner chamber, a warm amber glow drapes the whole space in soft gold. A cradle stands quietly in the corner, where a rosy-cheeked infant lies swaddled in white, fast asleep. Look closely, and there's a hint of Eirene in the baby's features. Or rather, a hint of the man who brought Eirene into the Campbell family—her "father."

Ten-year-old Eirene stoops over the cradle, her fingertips settling lightly on its edge, rocking it in a slow rhythm, and then, quietly, she breathes out.

*I wonder what Mother felt, all those years ago, looking down at me like this.*

Eirene: I'm glad you made it into this world safely, Hypatia.

Eirene: We'll love each other dearly... won't we? Just like... all our siblings and cousins do.
