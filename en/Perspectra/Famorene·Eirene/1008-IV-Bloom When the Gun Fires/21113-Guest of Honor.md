## IV

Bloom When the Gun Fires

Endgame of the Long Night

A long marble table is laid with a red tablecloth stitched in gold. Solid gold candelabras stand in a line, casting their glow down over porcelain of the finest quality.

The glossy plates catch the reflections perfectly, and in them are two faces drawn tight with nerves.

Bryce: An unexplained late arrival is no arrival at all.

Bryce: There's no reason to wait any longer. She is not the one this dinner is for.

The Campbell family banquets have always been lavish affairs. The grand dining hall is packed with Campbells young and old. Beneath blond hair of varying shades, pairs of mismatched eyes quietly size one another up. Dishes travel down the long table one after another, and so, quietly, do everyone's calculations.

???: My apologies.

A voice comes from the doorway, neither loud nor quiet, and every motion in the room stops dead.

Under the weight of every gaze, Eirene glances at the empty place White's family has left for her along the table. She smiles, then walks the whole length to the far end, and lowers herself slowly into the seat beside Bryce and his honored guest.

Grace: Eirene! That seat is—!

Ignoring Grace, who has half-risen from her chair, Eirene settles into her seat, calls the butler over, and asks for a bottle of fruit wine.

Eirene: I do apologize, Lady Celitane. I hope my lateness hasn't spoiled your welcome dinner.

Beside Eirene sits the woman who, from the moment the dinner began, has occupied the seat at Bryce's side—the very sight of which has soured White's family all evening. She gives the faintest shake of her head.

Eirene: Even so, the fault is mine for arriving late. I know Uncle White had a special bottle of fruit wine prepared for tonight. Allow me to borrow his generosity and offer a toast to you, as an apology.

The butler rolls the wine cart forward. Eirene rummages through it at her leisure, then pulls out a black bottle, plain and unremarkable. The year on its label has been traced over in gold paint, hiding whatever was printed beneath.

She uncorks it and pours herself a glass; the deep crimson wine slips down the glass, and one drop leaps free, landing on the tablecloth and disappearing into the red.

Eirene: Lady Celitane, may you and the Campbell child not yet born know health and peace.

Eirene lifts her glass in a light toast to the lady beside her, then drains the fruit wine in a single swallow. She raises a hand to stop Lady Celitane, who has motioned for a glass as well.

Eirene: It's a fine wine, Uncle White. Only... far too sweet.

Eirene: My mother always said a wine's character grows from fermentation alone. However fierce it turns out, it earned that fire on its own.

Eirene: Trying to mask that with sweetness added from the outside... not the wisest approach.

Grace's face pales, then flushes red. Her jaw is clenched tight, and she says nothing.

White: I haven't the faintest idea what you mean. And may I remind you, you arrived late. That is inexcusably rude, and pretending you're close to Lady Celitane won't smooth that over.

Eirene: I wasn't trying to smooth anything over, Uncle.

Eirene tilts her head and blinks, looking for all the world like a little girl nestling against Lady Celitane's shoulder.

Eirene: While I was covering Grace's classes, I came across Lady Celitane, who'd lost her way. I walked her through the main house. We got to talking, and here we are.

Bryce: Class? I don't recall telling you to go.

Bryce's sudden interjection makes White, who had still wanted to retort, shrink back and sit down again.

Eirene: Forgive me, Father. I was only curious about what everyone is learning these days. In case there's something I ought to catch up on.

Eirene: Take the investment account you opened for each Campbell child, for instance. Using my own judgment, I put money into a company that looked decent enough.

Eirene: But it did not go well.

Eirene: So I thought, perhaps a little schooling would help me find a way to make back the losses.

Eirene turns her fork over the peas, nudging them one at a time. She doesn't particularly like the taste, but the little round peas roll and resist, refusing to be speared. And she enjoys that—the small, quiet struggle of it.

Eirene: If this investment doesn't earn something soon, I won't be able to afford a gift at all.

Eirene: I counted the days. The child will most likely come in May. I thought... I might have a chess set made for her, of emerald, if I can manage it.

Bryce: If you can't even handle a sum that small, then clearly you could use a few lessons.

Eirene: You're quite right, Father. So I went to Lady Celitane. And she told me... that beyond the investment account, every Campbell child has a trust fund of their own. I thought I'd draw on mine. Just enough to have the gift made properly.

Eirene slows her words, props her chin in her hand, and wears a proper smile. Her gaze is fixed on White and his daughter not far off as the color suddenly drains from their faces.

Eirene: Only... it seems my trust account can't release the funds.

Eirene lifts a thin sheet of paper and lets it go. It flutters down to the middle of the long table. The fine print is densely packed, hard to read and even harder to care about. But the elegant signature at the bottom, written in flowing Disian script, stands out unmistakably: White Campbell.

Eirene: Uncle White, do you have any idea what this might be about...?
