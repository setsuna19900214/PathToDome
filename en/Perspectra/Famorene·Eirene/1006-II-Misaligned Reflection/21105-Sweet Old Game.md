# Sweet Old Game

## II

Misaligned Reflection

Endgame of the Long Night

There are two windows in Eirene's room. Through one, the view reaches past the city walls to the bustle of Fraser's docks; the other faces inward, toward the heart of the main house, the tower where the one who holds power resides.

Every heir lives in a room like this. Each is a special case; each is no exception at all. Like pieces packed neatly into a chess box, they wait for the player to set them on the board and send them into the fray.

The beginner's chess manual from that brother of hers—cheaply given, cheaply meant—lies tossed on the desk. Eirene picks it up and turns past the first page, where the simplest of games is laid out.

Eirene: ...Huh, the first game Mother ever taught me.

Eirene's hand comes to rest on the page, and for a long while she does not turn to the next.

Click.

Click. Click.

Young Woman's Voice: Heh...

Young Woman's Voice: You see, if you move there, your whole queenside is left wide open.

Girl's Voice: But if I get one step ahead of you, I'll promote to a queen and check you.

Young Woman's Voice: That's a rather bold move.

Girl's Voice: But... it wins.

At a manor on the outskirts of Fraser City, the setting sun had climbed past the balustrade and now gazed through the window, lacquering the simple tea table in honey-gold. Upon it rested a chessboard, its paint chipped and worn. A little girl and her mother sat facing one another, each with a piece in hand.

Mother: Ah, you really are your father's daughter. You've had a killer instinct on the board since you were little.

Eirene: No. It's because you have too many things you want to protect, Mother.

Mother: ...

Mother: It's not that there are too many. It's that... the things I want to protect are so very hard to protect.

Her mother's voice was so faint it might have been drowned out by the click of a piece on the board. Perhaps it was the illness. Or perhaps she had always been this way, a woman whose frailty itself was graceful, like an oil painting slowly losing its color.

But Eirene looked up at the photographs her mother kept so carefully in the display cabinet. In them, her mother was a radiant young woman, full of fire. Even in a still image, one could see those slender arms poised with aggression as she played her final move against her opponent.

Those same hands were now thin and strengthless. The piece her mother lifted trembled uncontrollably above the board. Eirene closed her hands around her mother's and guided the piece down. It landed with a clear, crisp click.

*What was it... that made her so weak?*

*Was it me? Giving birth to me wore her down... Her arms are so thin now.*

*No, it's not me. Or at least... not only me.*

Mother: Never mind. I can't beat you anymore, anyway... Only your father can keep you in check.

Mother: When was the last time he came...?

Mother: I remember he said he'd come play chess with you again...

Her mother rose slowly and drifted toward the room, that man's name still on her lips.
