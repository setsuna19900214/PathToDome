# A Loveless Body

## II

Misaligned Reflection

Endgame of the Long Night

> Description after completion:

At the center of the long hall table sat a lone chessboard. Its pieces were carved from ivory and ebony, all but two, and those two empty squares glared up like the gaps in a broken smile.

Her mother was not at the table as she always was. Only her father sat there.

Bryce set two transparent pieces onto the empty squares.

Bryce: Come. Play me.

Her father's opening was the same as always. Eirene took her seat without complaint.

Eirene: You take white this time.

Her fingers found the board and turned it with an easy flick of the wrist. The ebony pieces swung around to her side of the table, and there, square before her heart, sat the black king.

Bryce: Very well.

Bryce said nothing more. He simply accepted his daughter's "courtesy."

Thump-thump... thump-thump...

Silence held the hall before the match. Within it, Eirene could hear her own heartbeat with mounting clarity. It was a trembling thrill that welled up from her chest, raced through her mind, and spilled into her limbs.

She knew precisely what stirred it. She was about to play a match against her father.

And she meant to beat him, the man she had never once overcome, the man everyone else had placed on a pedestal.

Click!

A white piece advanced, and just like that, Eirene's wandering thoughts snapped back to the board. Her fingers found her own piece, closed around it with steady certainty, and brought it down, answering the move with a crisp click of its own.

No words passed between them; the match simply began. The butler placed an hourglass beside the board to mark the time.

Bryce moved with a merciless swiftness, leaving Eirene scarcely a breath to think. Each of his pieces came down like a knife stroke, driving straight for the soft heart of her position.

A fine sheen of sweat gathered at Eirene's brow, and still her eyes chased every piece he set down, never once falling behind.

Black descended. White devoured.

She ignored it and pressed forward.

Another black piece landed; white seized it at once and sent it off the board.

Black fell, white fed, the cycle grinding on without pause. Yet the tighter the trap closed around her, the fiercer her excitement grew.

Eirene: More... Keep it coming.

Just then, the hall door was pushed open a crack. The butler leaned half his body through the gap, not daring to step inside.

Butler: Master... the doctor says the lady may not last the night.

Eirene's hand hung suspended above the board, her fingertips still pinching the black rook she had yet to set down.

In the hourglass, the sand ran on. Bryce never raised his eyes; he merely drew his hand back from the board and waited, as though the decision would be Eirene's alone to make.

Eirene: ...Tell the doctor to stay with her.

Down came the black rook, and the crack it struck against the board rang out louder than any move before.

Eirene: I'm finishing this game.

One after another, the black pieces fell. And in their wake, the white pieces unfurled like silk, stretching into a long road across the board.

Drip!

A single drop of sweat traced its way down the pale curve of her cheek before striking the table. Then came the scrape of wood on wood, shrill and drawn-out, as the girl pushed back her chair and stood.

Eirene: ......

*Checkmate*

Click. Clack! A piece tumbled off the board and rolled across the floor.

The black bishop and the black rook now flanked the white king's throne on opposing diagonals. Escape was no longer an option.

Eirene gasped for breath and raised her eyes to meet the man who shared her hair, her features, her very face. Bryce did not look away. Father and daughter held each other's gaze for a long moment, the light between them seeming to freeze.

Eirene: I won, Father.

Bryce: Sacrificing pieces to press the attack. Well learned.

Bryce finally rose from his seat, picked up the hourglass, and knocked it twice against the table. The sand in the upper half poured down all at once, filling the lower chamber.

Bryce: Happy tenth birthday, Eirene.

Bryce: From today on, you'll move to the main house and live with your siblings and cousins.

Bryce: And don't forget, say goodbye to your mother.

Before the door to her mother's room, the butler stood. The dark wood held shut, yielding neither sound nor sign of what lay beyond.

Eirene: Mother said she loved you. You mean a lot to her.

Eirene walked to the door and pushed it open. Her mother lay peacefully in bed, her face drained of all color.

Eirene: She waited and waited until she burned herself out, and still couldn't beat you.

Eirene: But I can.

Eirene takes her mother's cold hand in hers, eyes fixed on Bryce standing in the doorway. The man she calls Father.

Eirene: Do you think... I love you?

Bryce: You don't need to.
