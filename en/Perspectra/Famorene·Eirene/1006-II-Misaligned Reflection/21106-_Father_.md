## II

Misaligned Reflection

Endgame of the Long Night

Eirene remembered him, of course, the one she called "father."

For as long as she could remember, it had always been just the two of them, she and her mother, living in this manor. They never lacked for anything materially, yet whenever that man made one of his rare visits, the whispers of the outside world followed him through the gates.

Mistress. Bastard child. Regret. Social climber... No matter how prominent a person might be, setting foot in this manor meant being swallowed whole by all of it.

Bryce: Come. Play me.

That man—her father in name only—did only one thing whenever he came to the manor: he played chess. Eirene had scarcely finished memorizing the rules when she was made to sit across the board from him.

Bryce: What are you hesitating for? It's your move.

Eirene: This one... I-I'll play here.

Bryce: Hmph.

Bryce: You lost again.

The rook and the knight joined forces: the rook sealed off one line, and the knight filled the last square. It was clean and decisive, with almost no piece wasted. Eirene clenched her back teeth as that man swept her king from the board without a flicker of mercy.

The click of wood on wood rang out sharp and clear, cutting through the quiet room like a gunshot.

Bryce: No resolve. No backbone. You disappoint me.

Eirene: ...

Bryce rose and strode out, not sparing so much as a glance for his wife or daughter. The door closed behind him. The light dimmed again, and the chess room settled back into its amber haze.

Her mother rested a hand on the crown of Eirene's head. Her touch was so light, as if she were afraid of breaking something.

Mother: That was your father. He's very skilled. It's only natural you can't beat him yet... When you're older—

Eirene: Is he better than you?

Her mother's smile faltered for the briefest moment. Something complex flickered through her eyes, something Eirene had no name for yet, before it was swiftly pressed back down.

Mother: Yes... he's better than me.

Eirene: ...I see.

How could that be possible?

You are the best chess player in Fraser City.

When her mother was away, Eirene would steal into her private collection, and she had leafed through the newspapers at the city archive as well. Chess books with her mother's name in the author's column. Newspapers whose bold headlines sat beneath photographs of a radiant, triumphant woman. Fan letters so many they had to be sealed away in the archive...

*She may be able to fool herself, but she can't fool me.*

She had been a chess master of a caliber that Fraser City—even DisCity—seldom produced. Prize after prize had come her way. And yet, in the end, she was left with nothing but this manor, hardly larger than a chessboard itself, where she taught her daughter the game and played it against that man.

The thought recalled an interview Eirene had once stumbled upon at the archive. One that was about her mother.

Beneath the glare of flashbulbs, a younger version of her mother sat facing a reporter, smiling. A placard beside them marked it as a post-match interview from some chess tournament.

Reporter: You must love your husband very much.

Mother: Yes. He means a lot to me.

Her mother kept her eyes lowered, yet even filtered through a screen, the candor in her words lost none of its weight.

Reporter: Will you keep competing after this? I've heard you want to open a school. Won't the marriage get in the way of those plans...

Mother: Heh. We love each other deeply. I'm sure he'll support whatever I want to do.

*Love.*
