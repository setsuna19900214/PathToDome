Surging Darkness

Endgame of the Long Night

On Eirene's last visit to White's estate, the air had not been so heavy. She sorts the documents into a neat stack and sets them on White's desk.

Grace—off in a rush to get "hands-on practice" at the account firm she runs—had left without even gathering all her papers.

Eirene collects them as a matter of course and places them by White's hand too.

Eirene: Uncle, when will Grace be back?

Eirene: She hasn't attended class in quite some time. I'm worried the teachers will figure out I've been sitting in for her and doing her assignments.

Eirene: Is it really wise for her to focus only on helping you manage those accounts?

White: Eirene.

White slaps the papers onto the desk, cutting Eirene's question short. Then he pulls over the stack she has prepared for signature and signs each one without so much as a glance.

Over the next three months, White dips into the trust fund time and again through "legal" means. Before long, he doesn't even bother reading the convoluted clauses anymore.

White: It's signed. Take it to the bank.

White: As for Grace...

White rocks his chair back and swings it halfway around, folding his hands as he studies Eirene with a frown.

White: The Campbells have been a family of business for generations. A company that turns a hundred percent profit is worth more to our family than a full grade on an exam.

White: That's exactly what Grace is working toward. She has the ability, and I'm sure the family will recognize it. She is the eldest daughter of the Campbells, after all.

Eirene: So then...

Eirene bows her head, cradling her chin in her hand as if deep in thought. She advances a single small step, and her words, spoken at a perfectly ordinary volume, send a chill straight through White.

Eirene: They're clearing out Adelaide's old room... to move Grace in?

White: What... what did you just say?!

White's fingers clench around the armrest, his gaze sharpening in an instant, while a low, unsteady murmur of shock falls from his lips.

White: No... it can't be... Adelaide vanished long ago, and no new brats have come to the city recently...

Then a thought lands. White jerks his head up and stares at Eirene.

Eirene: What's wrong, Uncle White? Are you taking me to the main house to pick out a room?

White: That can't be... impossible... you... you've barely had any schooling... you're no different from those spoiled brats who do nothing but spend money...

In the midst of this strange, charged silence, a sudden knock comes at the door.

Eirene goes to open it, and there, of all people, stands the butler who once attended Adelaide.

Butler: Miss Eirene. It's been some time. I see you and Mr. White remain as close as ever.

Eirene: Uncle White and Grace have been very good to me. That's why I'm here so often.

Butler: Then I suppose this saves me the trouble of a second errand.

The butler places two invitations into Eirene's hands and delivers a bow of impeccable professionalism.

Butler: Master Bryce is hosting a dinner. He asks that all of you attend the Campbell family gathering next week.
