And yet, among them all, a few stand apart.

???: My apologies, Eirene. I was too busy yesterday to come and greet you properly, so I've brought my daughter along today.

???: Grace, don't just stand there.

The middle-aged man gives a gentle nudge to the girl half-hidden behind him. She, too, has long blonde hair, and stands half a head taller than Eirene. A little shyly, she holds the white roses out to Eirene.

Grace: For your mother... from me.

Not far off, a servant busy with cleaning catches sight of the father and daughter and hurriedly drops into a bow.

Servant: Master White, you came as well? Is your leg any better? From the riding accident?

White: A scratch, nothing more. Eirene is my own niece, and she's just lost her mother. What kind of uncle would I be if I stayed away?

White: Hello there, Eirene. I'm White, your uncle. Your father is my very dear older brother.

White: And this is my daughter, Grace. Your cousin.

Eirene cradles the roses and bends in a courtesy toward the two of them. A small green worm drops from the bouquet and wriggles across the ground, crawling away.

Grace: Eek—!

Grace springs back half a step and, with a quick flick of her toe, sweeps the worm beneath the stone steps. She then catches herself, covers her mouth, and laughs at her own lapse.

Eirene does not move. She watches the worm crawl into the crack between the bricks until it vanishes, and only then straightens up and looks to White at her side.

White: Your father's a busy man. He may not have much time for you, but don't worry. Anything that happens in this house, you can ask me. Whatever's troubling you, your uncle and cousin are always here to help.

Since Bryce brought Eirene to the main house, he has indeed never appeared again. Eirene cannot quite fathom what her father intends, but...

Eirene: Thank you, Uncle White. Grace. I think we'll get along.

Grace: I'm glad you've warmed up to us so fast.

Grace takes Eirene's arm in hers and leans in close, talking with easy warmth.

Grace: So did Uncle Bryce get you into the Campbell school?

Eirene: School?

Grace: Yeah, it's exclusive to Campbell kids. Sometimes... outer-estate kids get in too, if Uncle Bryce approves. They teach things you'd never learn at an ordinary school. Especially anything to do with the Campbell family's business.

With a touch of pride, Grace angles the golden rose badge on her chest toward Eirene.

Eirene: Father never gave me one.

Grace: Huh? He didn't?

Grace covers her mouth, eyes widening in surprise.

Grace: I heard you beat Uncle Bryce at chess. I figured you'd have an offer for sure.

Eirene: Maybe I'm not good enough yet.

White: Don't say that. If Bryce acknowledges you, you must be an extraordinary child.

Grace: Father, even though Eirene didn't get a spot... I agree with you! I'm sure she's something special!

Grace: Let her sit in on my classes for a few days! If she can handle the material, then maybe... maybe we can ask Uncle Bryce to let her study with me! And if he doesn't agree... then I'll just teach you everything after school every day!

White: Sigh, I can never argue with you... All right, I'll talk to the teachers tomorrow.

Eirene's face, which has held its blank calm all this while, at last eases a fraction under Grace's smile.

Eirene: Thank you... Grace. Uncle White.

Eirene: I won't waste your kindness. I'll do my best to keep up.
