Endgame of the Long Night

The next day, before the gravestone in the small garden, a few figures appear. They linger a brief moment, then vanish without a trace.

Eirene: ...

???: Good morning, child.

She turns toward the voice. There, walking toward her with a genial smile, is a middle-aged man leading a young girl by the hand. The girl carries a bundle of white roses.

The petals still glisten with morning dew; the flowers might have been plucked from the garden only moments ago.

Eirene: Hello.

Adelaide holds the name of the eldest son, but the name buys him little. Still, ever since he gave Eirene that chess manual, a scent has drawn people in. Relatives arrive under the guise of mourning, all to steal a look at the illegitimate daughter who has finally been "made legitimate."

The faces that come bear fragments of Eirene's own; the looks they cast are desire laid bare. But desire without power turns the eyes into something else—the eyes of creatures born in caves. Dark. Empty. Yet locked, unblinking, on the light.

Eirene accepts the white and yellow flowers her visitors bring. Once they have gone, she crushes a handful in her palm and tosses the remains into the garden.
