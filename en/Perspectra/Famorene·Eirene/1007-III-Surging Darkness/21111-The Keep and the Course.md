# The Keep and the Course

## III

Surging Darkness

Endgame of the Long Night

> Description after completion:
> Fight through the ruins of the old night to forge a path paved with gold and pearls.

The family's private school is no less grand than the main house itself. Marble columns run from floor to dome, their shafts carved with tangled rope motifs; overhead stretches an entire ceiling of joined stained glass, mapping the principal sea routes of WhiteSands. When sunlight pierces the glass, it lays drifting bands of gold across the floor, as though the wakes of ships had been traced there.

Glass display cases run down both sides of the hall, holding scale models of the family's flagships: from the crude Wavecutter of the first patriarch to the newest great-sailed sand ships that now trade with DisCity. A dozen generations of hulls stand in a single unbroken line.

Behind these models, along the amber brick of the corridor walls, hang portraits of Campbells past. The "Bryces" within those frames stare down with cold eyes at the children who pass beneath—fair-skinned, blonde noble children who bear more than a passing resemblance to them.

Eirene: I read about it. Decades ago, the Campbells became the ruling family of Fraser City. We had desert navigation down to an art, and through it, we controlled eighty percent of WhiteSands' shipping lanes, trading with DisCity thousands of kilometers away and with every little Outland faction in between.

Eirene: After all these years, there isn't a corner of WhiteSands without a Campbell business. Not one route without a Campbell ship.

Eirene stands at the center of the corridor and lets the portraits look down on her as they will. Her gaze, meanwhile, moves greedily across the model ships in the cases on either side.

Grace: You're... you're really not scared? I hate this hall. Those old... old family portraits. They scare me to death.

Grace: I heard if you flunk an exam, the teachers make you come down here at night and recite the Campbell finance textbook to the ancestors...

Grace lowers her head a little, peering at the portraits from the corner of her eye, as if she might begin trembling at any moment.

Eirene: If you just work hard, there's nothing to be scared of...

Eirene pats the arm looped through hers, about to say something reassuring, when she catches Grace frowning with her eyes locked on the classroom doorway not far ahead.

Eirene follows her gaze and sees Adelaide, the brother she has not laid eyes on in days, striding out of the classroom without so much as a backward glance at the teacher shouting after him in fury.

Teacher: Your behavior is a disgrace to the Campbell name!!

Adelaide: Tell it to the Campbells in the corridor.

The bold words have barely left his mouth when Adelaide catches sight of the two girls standing at the center of the corridor.

Adelaide: Heh. I suppose that means you two as well.

Adelaide strides off, never looking back, and is gone around the corridor's turn. Grace snorts softly, then leans close and speaks in a whisper only the two of them can catch.

Grace: What right does a firstborn like that have to be here? If he wants to leave so badly, then let him go.

Eirene's thoughts turn to the farewell gift Adelaide had given her.

The eldest son's seat is the very thing everyone here would give anything for. So why throw it away? Because he lacks the ability? Because he is afraid? Or...?

Grace: Once he's out of the main house, I can move in.

Eirene: The main house?

Grace gestures toward a splendid building just beyond the window. Its white colonnade gleams in the sun, and the top-floor windows flash back brilliant points of light. Along the path leading to it, rows of magnolias and coralroot orchids stand in bloom.

Grace: The building we're in now is called the main house, sure. But the ones with real inheritance rights... they live farther in.

Grace: If Adelaide keeps this up, the Campbells won't have any heirs left! When we... when we're better placed, as the eldest daughter of the Campbells, I'll be the one to take the main house.

Grace: And then you can pick whichever room you like best. I'll always keep one for you.

Grace holds Eirene's hand, gazing at her softly. Eirene returns the look with a smile just as warm.

Eirene: After coming back to the Campbells, you're the first person I've gotten close to. I feel lucky, Grace.

Eirene: You're always willing to share everything with me.

Grace: Honestly, I try to be friendly with every child Uncle brings back...

Grace: But you're the only one... the only one who ever thanked me. That's why I'm happy to do it... Let's keep supporting each other and growing together...

With a smile, Eirene reaches out and takes Grace's hand.

Grace: I know you love chess. We could set up a chess room in the main house. You could play anything you like.

Eirene: Do you play too?

Grace: Sadly, chess is for people with free time. I've got too much to juggle. Classes, exams, practical work... Just keeping up with the accounts Father gave me is enough to wear me out.

Eirene: You're already managing accounts for the family? That's impressive.

Eirene: It seems I still have a great deal to learn...

Grace: Oh! The account is actually my own! But I put it into my father's company, so he manages it with me.

Grace: And... you've got one too, you know!

Eirene: Huh? I do?

Grace: Every Campbell child gets a sum of funding from Uncle Bryce once they come to the city! And it's got a name... what was it... trust... trust some...

Eirene: Trust fund.

Grace: Oh, right! Trust fund! You always remember everything, Eirene! Lots of Campbell kids are good with finance and business. Plenty of them turned that money into real success before they even came of age.

Grace: And honestly, it's fine if you don't get it, or don't know what to do.

Grace leans in close to Eirene and blinks her eyes at her.

Grace: You can just leave it to Father and me. Some of the children who came back before you did exactly that!

Manager: Miss Eirene Campbell, your account and trust fund don't currently have a designated guardian. Technically, they fall under Campbell family oversight by default, but you are entitled to appoint a trustee of your own choosing.

Manager: Are you sure you want to go through with this? It would place certain restrictions on you.

Manager: From what I know of the Campbells, most of them prefer to manage their own funds personally.

Eirene: I'm sure.

Eirene takes the contract from the manager's hand and uncaps the pen, yet the nib hangs poised above the signature line and stops.

Grace: *And then you can pick whichever room you like best. I'll always keep one for you.*

When Grace spoke those words, she seemed every inch the sheltered, unworldly young noble.

But the pause lasts only a moment. This is the first time Eirene has written her full name—Eirene Campbell—in elegant cursive, and yet the letters feel somehow split apart, as though the two halves do not quite belong together.

It doesn't matter. There will be plenty of chances to write it again. Her pen lingers at the end, leaving a single small dot of ink.

Manager: Very well, Miss Eirene. As of today... the trustee for your account and trust fund will be Mr. White Campbell.

Eirene: Mm. Oh, and here's a consent form signed by my guardian. Please open a separate account for me as well.

Eirene: Uncle White, Grace, I'm back.

White's estate is larger than the one Eirene grew up in, and set nearer to the Campbell main house.

For the next two months or so, Eirene gets what she was promised and follows Grace into the Campbell school, soaking up every subject on offer. The golden rose badge that marks a true Campbell never comes to her, but it doesn't matter for now. She is thrilled simply to be learning so much she had never been taught.

Grace: You're late, Eirene.

Grace sits at the dining table, its surface strewn with papers dense with writing. Seeing Eirene arrive at this hour, she cannot help but frown, and a note of complaint slips into her voice.

Eirene: Sorry, Grace. After class I remembered the contract for Uncle White, so I stopped by the bank...

White: The bank?! Don't tell me... you've already signed it?!

Grace brings both hands down on the table and springs up. White, catching the exchange from the living room, drops his poker cards at once and hurries over, folding Eirene into a hug.

White: You're the best niece an uncle could ask for!

Grace presses a kiss to Eirene's cheek, then seizes the contract at once and bends over it with her father.

Eirene: Oh, and Grace, about that investment account you mentioned before...

Grace: Just handle that however you want.

Grace's hand pauses over the page, as though she has caught the indifference in her own voice.

Grace: Why not try running it yourself? You've learned a lot these past weeks, haven't you?

Grace: Oh, and if there's anything you don't get, just ask me first! You're still new, so your questions are probably pretty basic. No need to bother the teachers.

Eirene: Alright, Grace.

Eirene: Then, Uncle White, could I ask you to sign something for me later? That account... requires the sole signatory to authorize it.

Eirene: Uncle White, these are the withdrawal authorization papers.

White: Leave them there. I'll sign in a moment.
