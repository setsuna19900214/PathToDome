Pearl of WhiteSands

The farther he walks, the more the sunlight is blotted out, until the shadow of the resplendent main house swallows Adelaide entirely. A straight, somber corridor opens before him.

The main house is a place reserved for the Campbell patriarch alone and the descendants he sees fit to acknowledge. All around it, the lesser estates can do nothing but look up at it, filled with envy and longing.

Adelaide: Did her mother come with her? I should see about preparing a congratulatory gift for the woman.

Butler: I'm told her mother did come along, but...

The butler's words are cut short by the sound of a chant. The boy looks up, and there, at the edge of the veranda before a doorway, sits a blonde girl, her eyes fixed unblinkingly on the garden beyond.

Adelaide: Ah... there she is.

At the sound of conversation, the girl turns her head toward the two standing before her.

Her mismatched eyes are somewhat dim, just as the servants had said. Mixed blood, plain for anyone to see.

Eirene: Is there something you need, Brother?

Adelaide: Oh? You know who I am?

The girl's gaze moves first to the ring on the boy's hand, its family crest set into the band, then rises to his eyes—a green cleaner, purer than the shade she sees in her own.

Eirene: I saw the family photographs on the way here.

Eirene: A pleasure. I'm Eirene. Your half-sister.

The exchange is brief. Eirene does not even rise to curtsy; she simply turns her gaze back toward the garden, aloof and unbothered.

Adelaide: ...

A Campbell to the bone—however young, his eye is keen enough. Instinct tells Adelaide that this sister of his is not like the other descendants trotted out as "acknowledged" in recent years.

Compared to those children, the Campbell standing before him resembles their father far more.

Adelaide: I'm told your mother came along. If I may, I'd like to greet her.

Eirene lifts her chin, gesturing toward the garden—

There, in the small garden, a priest in black robes stands before a white gravestone, chanting a prayer. The words, once broken and far away, now come through clear.

Priest: That day was a day of wrath, a day of ruin and pain, a day beyond measure in both greatness and grief.

Priest: Heaven and earth, give her eternal rest.

Priest: Let everlasting light shine upon her.

Eirene: My mother was a fine chess player. A shame, though; she lost to Father in the end. By a single move.

Eirene: She passed away while I was playing chess with him.

Eirene: I won. That's why I was allowed to move here and attend her funeral.

Eirene: Would you like to go there and greet her?

Adelaide's expression remains unchanged. There is not a single flicker of awkwardness. He simply turns toward the small garden and offers a bow.

Adelaide: That won't be necessary. She was not the one I came to see.

Adelaide: I'm here for you, and...

Adelaide takes the book from the butler's hands that he had been reading only moments before.

Adelaide: A gift for you.

Eirene takes the book and studies the gold-stamped title on its cover: The Complete Beginner's Chess Manual. She says nothing more and accepts it.

Eirene: A welcome gift?

Adelaide: A farewell gift.

Eirene: ...

Adelaide doesn't linger. He turns and walks away, never looking again into those eyes of Eirene's.

Adelaide: You're very much a Campbell.

Eirene: ...So are you.

The funeral prayer in the small garden goes on, broken and intermittent. Beneath the sunlight, within the shadow, the two figures draw farther and farther away.

And from that day forward, Eirene never sees her brother—the one who has given her the chess book—at the main house again.
