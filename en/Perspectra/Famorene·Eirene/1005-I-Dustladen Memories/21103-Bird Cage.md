Laughter and the patter of running feet seep through into the room. A boy, midway through turning a page, pauses for just a moment, then lets out a cold, dismissive huff.

Tutor: Young Master Adelaide.

The shelves along the wall sag under thick, dust-laden tomes whose spines bear scripts few could decipher. Beside them hangs a slate-grey blackboard. The old tutor adjusts his glasses and speaks, his voice thick with displeasure.

Tutor: Whatever you may think of it, so long as you remain here, you are my student, and you will listen to me.

His eyes lift from the page and drift toward the door left ajar, and through the narrow gap, he catches the barest glimpse of children's shadows darting past.

Tutor: You are different from the rest of them, in the end. Not every member of the Campbell family is granted an education like this.

Adelaide: Am I? I don't see the difference. We're all birds in a cage. Does it really matter whose perch sits a little higher?

The youth leaves on his own, bringing the butler along as he walks toward a tall building, leaving the family tutor's voice behind him.

Tutor: Young Master Adelaide! The lesson isn't finished. You cannot simply walk out!

Adelaide: Heh. Even that old fossil couldn't stop me... Who do you think you are?

The boy closes the door behind him and lets the clamor swallow him whole.

Adelaide: She's already here?

Butler: You mean...?

Adelaide: Heh. Isn't there a Campbell daughter who is supposed to arrive today?

Butler: Yes. Miss Campbell came in by ship to the main house early this morning. Would you like to go and see her?

Adelaide: The tutor wasn't entirely wrong, I suppose. As long as I remain here, I'm still the eldest son of the Campbell family.

Adelaide: I believe that entitles me to meet this little sister of mine, the one Father has acknowledged as his own.
